## Early termination rights that override the lease-break fee

### Military service (federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act)
*50 U.S.C. § 3955*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant enters military service after signing it; or if Tenant, while in military service, signed this Lease and then receives orders for a permanent change of station (which includes separation or retirement) or to deploy with a military unit or in support of a military operation for 90 days or more; or if Tenant signed this Lease upon such orders and then receives a stop movement order of 30 days or more (or indefinite) that prevents occupancy. A spouse or dependent may terminate within one year after Tenant dies in military service or while performing covered National Guard or Reserve duty, and a Tenant who suffers a catastrophic injury or illness in such service (or the spouse or dependent, if Tenant lacks capacity) may terminate within one year after it occurs. Termination also ends the obligations of Tenant's dependents under this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant delivers written notice to Landlord by hand, private business carrier, mail with return receipt requested, or electronic means. Because rent is paid monthly, termination is effective 30 days after the first date on which the next rent payment is due after the notice is delivered; termination after a stop movement order is effective when the notice and orders are delivered.
- **Documentation:** The notice must include a copy of the military orders (official orders, including orders for separation or retirement, or a notification, certification, or verification from the commanding officer).
- **Owes:** Rent is owed only on a prorated basis through the effective date of termination. Landlord may not impose an early termination charge, but Tenant still owes unpaid amounts due under this Lease, including reasonable charges for excess wear. Rent paid in advance for any period after termination will be refunded within 30 days after the effective date. Landlord may not hold Tenant's belongings or security deposit to satisfy a claim for rent accruing after termination.

### National Guard or state defense force on state active duty (Wisconsin law)
*Wis. Stat. § 321.62(1)(c), (14)*

- **Who:** A member of the Wisconsin National Guard or state defense force who is ordered into state active duty for 30 days or more, and who entered state active duty after this Lease was signed, may terminate this Lease if the Premises are occupied by the member or the member and the member's dependents.
- **Notice:** Tenant may terminate at any time after the period of state active duty begins by written notice delivered personally or by first-class mail to Landlord or Landlord's managing agent. When rent is paid monthly, termination takes effect 30 days after the first date on which the next rent payment is due after the notice is delivered or mailed.
- **Owes:** Unpaid rent for the period before termination is prorated, and Landlord shall refund rent paid in advance for any period after termination. Landlord may not seize or hold Tenant's belongings for rent accruing after termination. A court may modify this relief if Landlord applies before the termination date.

### Imminent threat of serious physical harm, domestic abuse, stalking, or sexual assault
*Wis. Stat. § 704.16*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate the tenancy and move out if Tenant or Tenant's child faces an imminent threat of serious physical harm from another person if Tenant remains on the Premises, or if Tenant or Tenant's child was sexually assaulted, as shown by a qualifying document.
- **Notice:** Tenant must give Landlord written notice delivered as required by Wis. Stat. § 704.21 (personally, by registered or certified mail, or another permitted method).
- **Documentation:** For a threat of harm, the notice must include a certified copy of one of the following: a domestic abuse, child abuse, or harassment injunction protecting Tenant or Tenant's child; a condition of release ordering the person not to contact Tenant; a criminal complaint alleging the person stalked Tenant or Tenant's child; or a criminal complaint filed after the person's arrest for a domestic abuse offense against Tenant. For sexual assault, the notice must include a certified copy of a harassment injunction based on sexual assault or a criminal complaint alleging sexual assault of Tenant or Tenant's child.
- **Owes:** Tenant is not liable for rent after the end of the month following the month in which Tenant gives the notice or moves out, whichever is later, and that liability is further reduced by Landlord's duty to mitigate. On Tenant's request with a certified copy of a qualifying document, Landlord will change the locks (or allow Tenant to) within 48 hours, at Tenant's cost.

### Death of Tenant
*Wis. Stat. § 704.165*

- **Who:** If Tenant dies, the tenancy ends automatically.
- **Notice:** The tenancy ends on the earlier of 60 days after Landlord learns of Tenant's death or the end of the Lease term (for a periodic tenancy, 60 days after Landlord learns of the death).
- **Owes:** Tenant's estate is not liable for rent after the tenancy ends, subject to Landlord's duty to mitigate. Other adult tenants remain bound by this Lease. Landlord will not seek rent from family members who are not liable for it.

### Untenantable premises
*Wis. Stat. § 704.07(4)*

- **Who:** If the Premises become untenantable because of fire, water, or other casualty, or because of a condition hazardous to health, or if there is a substantial violation of Landlord's repair duties materially affecting Tenant's health or safety, Tenant may move out unless Landlord promptly repairs, rebuilds, or eliminates the hazard. Tenant may also move out if the repair period would impose undue hardship on Tenant.
- **Notice:** 
- **Owes:** If Tenant justifiably moves out, Tenant owes no rent after the Premises became untenantable, and Landlord shall repay any rent paid in advance for that period. If Tenant stays, rent abates to the extent Tenant is deprived of full normal use. This does not apply if Tenant's negligence or improper use caused the condition.

## Open questions for the reviewing attorney

- HIGH RISK — whole-lease voidness: under Wis. Stat. § 704.44 and ATCP 134.08 a residential rental agreement is VOID AND UNENFORCEABLE in its entirety (not just the offending clause) if it: lets the landlord penalize a tenant for calling police/health/safety services; authorizes non-judicial eviction; accelerates rent or waives the duty to mitigate; requires the tenant to pay the landlord's attorney fees or costs (other than court-awarded); allows confession of judgment; exculpates the landlord for its own negligence; makes the tenant liable for injuries/damage beyond the tenant's control; waives habitability duties; allows termination of a crime-victim tenant based on that crime; or allows termination for a crime without the § 704.14 notice. The shared Keycove lease body must be checked clause by clause for WI (any 'Tenant pays Landlord's attorney fees' or 'all remaining rent becomes due' clause voids the whole lease). See Koble Investments v. Marquardt, 2024 WI App 26 (annotated in § 704.14): a lease with an 'unlawful purpose' clause but no § 704.14 notice was void.
- The § 704.14 notice text is reproduced verbatim as a disclosure; it is mandatory in the lease or an addendum.
- Nonstandard rental provisions (extra deposit deductions such as routine carpet cleaning, expanded entry rights, liens on property) must be in a separate signed document titled exactly 'NONSTANDARD RENTAL PROVISIONS'. If Keycove's lease has any such clause (e.g., a fixed carpet-cleaning deduction), it must be moved to that separate document for WI.
- Early termination fee: Wisconsin has no statutory cap, but 704.44(3m) voids any clause that 'otherwise waives the landlord's obligation to mitigate damages'. A flat lease-break fee that the tenant chooses in exchange for release is probably different from a clause waiving mitigation, but the attorney should confirm Keycove's optional early-termination-fee clause cannot be read as a mitigation waiver or rent acceleration.
- Military: Wis. Stat. § 321.62(14) covers National Guard or state defense force members on state active duty for 30+ days (the federal SCRA covers federal duty). It requires that the member entered state active duty AFTER the lease was executed, and § 704.29 (mitigation) does not apply. The statute does not expressly require copies of orders; documentation is left blank. Non-monthly leases end on the last day of the month after the notice month.
- Late fees: no statutory cap or grace period statewide. ATCP 134.09(8) requires the fee to be in the rental agreement, prepayments applied first, and no fee on a late fee. Some Wisconsin authorities treat unreasonable late fees as penalties; amount not verified against a primary-source cap.
- Security deposit statement: the statement of withholdings obligation comes from ATCP 134.06(4) (see State v. Lasecki, 2020 WI App 36, annotated in § 704.28); failure can be prosecuted as an unfair trade practice. No forwarding-address prerequisite to return; ATCP 134.06(5) excuses a landlord only if the postal service can't deliver.
- Wisconsin does not require deposit interest or a separate deposit account.
- The abandoned-property disclosure assumes the landlord will NOT store property left behind (the common choice under § 704.05(5)(bf)). If a landlord intends to store property, this notice must be omitted and the landlord must follow the pre-2011 procedure (§ 704.05, 2009 stats.). Consider making it a landlord setting.
- Wis. Stat. § 704.085 caps tenant-paid credit report fees at $25 (and background checks for nonresidents at $25), and ATCP 134.05 regulates earnest money; not encoded in the lease.
- Local law: state law preempts many local landlord-tenant ordinances (2011 Act 143, 2013 Act 76; Wis. Stat. § 66.0104). Madison and Milwaukee still have rental registration/inspection and some notice ordinances. Not encoded.
