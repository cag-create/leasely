## Early termination rights that override the lease-break fee

### Military service (federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act)
*50 U.S.C. § 3955*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant enters military service after signing it; or if Tenant, while in military service, signed this Lease and then receives orders for a permanent change of station (which includes separation or retirement) or to deploy with a military unit or in support of a military operation for 90 days or more; or if Tenant signed this Lease upon such orders and then receives a stop movement order of 30 days or more (or indefinite) that prevents occupancy. A spouse or dependent may terminate within one year after Tenant dies in military service or while performing covered National Guard or Reserve duty, and a Tenant who suffers a catastrophic injury or illness in such service (or the spouse or dependent, if Tenant lacks capacity) may terminate within one year after it occurs. Termination also ends the obligations of Tenant's dependents under this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant delivers written notice to Landlord by hand, private business carrier, mail with return receipt requested, or electronic means. Because rent is paid monthly, termination is effective 30 days after the first date on which the next rent payment is due after the notice is delivered; termination after a stop movement order is effective when the notice and orders are delivered.
- **Documentation:** The notice must include a copy of the military orders (official orders, including orders for separation or retirement, or a notification, certification, or verification from the commanding officer).
- **Owes:** Rent is owed only on a prorated basis through the effective date of termination. Landlord may not impose an early termination charge, but Tenant still owes unpaid amounts due under this Lease, including reasonable charges for excess wear. Rent paid in advance for any period after termination will be refunded within 30 days after the effective date. Landlord may not hold Tenant's belongings or security deposit to satisfy a claim for rent accruing after termination.

### Military service (Oregon law)
*Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.475*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant provides proof of official orders showing that Tenant is enlisting for active service in the United States Armed Forces (Air Force, Army, Coast Guard, Marine Corps, Navy or Space Force); is serving in the National Guard or another reserve or active component and is ordered to active service outside the area for more than 90 days; is terminating active service; is a U.S. Public Health Service member detailed to the Army or Navy who is ordered to active service outside the area for more than 90 days or is ending that duty and moving outside the area; or is a NOAA commissioned corps member ordered to active service outside the area for more than 90 days.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives Landlord written notice. Termination is effective on the earlier of the date set by applicable federal law or the latest of: 30 days after delivery of the notice; 30 days before the earliest reporting date on the orders; a date specified in the notice; or, when terminating active service or Public Health Service duty, 90 days before the effective date of the orders.
- **Documentation:** The notice must be accompanied by proof of the official orders.
- **Owes:** Tenant is not subject to any penalty, fee, charge or loss of deposit because of the termination and is not liable for rent after the effective termination date.

### Domestic violence, sexual assault, bias crime or stalking
*Or. Rev. Stat. §§ 90.453, 90.456, 90.459*

- **Who:** A Tenant who is protected by a valid order of protection, or who has been the victim of domestic violence, sexual assault, bias crime or stalking within the 90 days before the notice, may require Landlord to release Tenant, and any immediate family members of Tenant who are not perpetrators, from this Lease. Time the perpetrator was incarcerated or living more than 100 miles from Tenant's home does not count toward the 90 days.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives Landlord at least 14 days' written notice requesting release, specifying the release date and listing the names of any immediate family members to be released.
- **Documentation:** The notice must be accompanied by one of the following: a copy of a valid order of protection or other court order restraining a person from contact with Tenant; a copy of a federal, state, local or tribal police report about the domestic violence, sexual assault, bias crime or stalking; a copy of a conviction of any person for such an act against Tenant; or a verification statement in the form set out in Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.453(3), signed by Tenant and by a qualified third party (a law enforcement officer, attorney, licensed health professional, Oregon Department of Justice victim services employee, or victim's advocate at a victim services provider) who has had individual contact with Tenant.
- **Owes:** Tenant and released family members are not liable for rent or damage to the dwelling unit incurred after the release date and are not subject to any fee solely because of the termination. Other tenants remain bound by this Lease, and any deposit, fee or prepaid rent is accounted for when the remaining tenancy ends. Landlord will keep the information provided confidential except as the statute allows. Tenant may also have the locks changed at Tenant's expense on notice that Tenant is a victim, without providing verification.

### Oregon National Guard called into active state service
*Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.472*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant is a member of the organized militia called into active service of the state by the Governor under Or. Rev. Stat. § 399.065(1) for 90 or more consecutive days.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives Landlord written notice. Termination is effective on the earlier of 30 days after the date the next rental payment is due, or the last day of the month after the month in which the notice is given.
- **Documentation:** Tenant must provide proof of official orders showing that Tenant is a state service member.
- **Owes:** Tenant is not subject to any penalty, fee, charge or loss of deposit because of the termination and is not liable for rent after the effective termination date.

### Dwelling posted unsafe, or serious hazard at move-in
*Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.380(2), (5)–(8)*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease immediately if a governmental agency posts the dwelling unit as unsafe and unlawful to occupy because of conditions not caused by Tenant, or if conditions that existed at the start of the tenancy and were not caused by Tenant pose an imminent and serious threat to the health or safety of occupants within six months after the tenancy begins.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives Landlord actual notice of the termination and the reason for it.
- **Owes:** Landlord will return the security deposit, any prepaid rent, and rent prepaid for the month of termination prorated to the later of the termination date or the date Tenant vacates (within 14 days for a posted unit, or within four days for a hazard existing at move-in). Tenant may also recover damages where the statute allows.

### Loss of utility service because of another's nonpayment
*Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.315(5)–(8)*

- **Who:** If Tenant cannot obtain a utility or service Tenant is responsible for because of an amount owed by a previous tenant or the owner, or if a utility or service Landlord is responsible for is shut off for nonpayment, Tenant may terminate this Lease.
- **Notice:** Before moving in, Tenant may terminate immediately by actual notice stating the reason. After moving in, Tenant gives actual notice 72 hours before the termination date stating the reason, and the tenancy does not terminate if service is restored within the 72 hours.
- **Owes:** Landlord will return deposits, prepaid rent and fees as the statute requires (rent and fees within four days after termination), and Tenant may recover actual damages from the shutoff.

### Landlord's material noncompliance or failure to maintain
*Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.360(1)*

- **Who:** If Landlord materially fails to comply with this Lease or fails to maintain the dwelling unit as required by Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.320, Tenant may terminate this Lease unless Landlord remedies the breach in time. Tenant may not terminate for a condition caused by Tenant or a person on the premises with Tenant's consent.
- **Notice:** Tenant delivers written notice specifying the breach and stating that this Lease will terminate on a date not less than 30 days after delivery if the breach is not remedied within 7 days (essential services) or 30 days (all other cases). If substantially the same breach recurs within six months after a prior notice, Tenant may terminate on at least 14 days' written notice.
- **Owes:** If this Lease terminates, Landlord will return all recoverable security deposits and prepaid rent.

### Unlawful ouster or interruption of essential services
*Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.375*

- **Who:** If Landlord unlawfully removes or excludes Tenant, seriously attempts or threatens to do so, or willfully diminishes or threatens to diminish heat, running water, hot water, electricity or other essential service, Tenant may terminate this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant may terminate by notice to Landlord.
- **Owes:** Landlord will return all recoverable security deposits and prepaid rent, and Tenant may recover up to two months' periodic rent or twice actual damages, whichever is greater.

### Unlawful or harassing entry
*Or. Rev. Stat. §§ 90.322(8), 90.360(1)*

- **Who:** If Landlord makes an unlawful entry, a lawful entry in an unreasonable manner, or repeated demands for entry that unreasonably harass Tenant, Tenant may terminate this Lease under Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.360(1).
- **Notice:** Tenant follows the written notice procedure in Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.360(1).
- **Owes:** Tenant may recover actual damages of not less than one month's rent (one week's rent for a week-to-week tenancy).

### Foreclosure (fixed term tenancy)
*Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.367(6)*

- **Who:** A Tenant with a fixed term tenancy who receives actual notice that the property is in foreclosure may terminate this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant delivers written notice stating that Tenant has received notice of the foreclosure and that the tenancy will terminate on a designated date not less than 60 days after delivery. The tenancy terminates on that date unless, within 30 days after delivery, Landlord provides written evidence from a lender or trustee that the property is no longer in foreclosure, or that a court-appointed receiver oversees the property.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes rent through the termination date.


## State disclosures in the lease

- **Owner and manager identification** (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.305(1)–(2)): Landlord discloses below the name and address of the person authorized to manage the premises and of an owner of the premises or a person authorized to act for the owner for service of process and for receiving and receipting for notices and demands. Landlord will keep this information current.
- **Smoking policy** (Or. Rev. Stat. §§ 90.220(4), 479.305): Landlord states in this Lease whether smoking is prohibited on the premises, allowed on the entire premises, or allowed only in limited areas, and if smoking is allowed in limited areas, identifies those areas.
- **100-year flood plain** (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.228) *Only if the dwelling unit is located in a 100-year flood plain*: The dwelling unit is located within the 100-year flood plain as determined by the National Flood Insurance Program of the Federal Emergency Management Agency.
- **Utilities that benefit Landlord or other tenants** (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.315(2)–(3)) *Only if Tenant pays directly for a utility or service delivered to any area outside Tenant's dwelling unit*: Landlord discloses in this Lease each utility or service that Tenant pays directly to a provider that also benefits Landlord or other tenants because it is delivered to an area other than Tenant's dwelling unit.
- **Utility charges billed by Landlord** (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.315(4)) *Only if Landlord bills Tenant for utility, service or public service charges*: Landlord may bill Tenant for the utility or service charges described in this Lease. Common-area charges are described separately from charges for Tenant's unit. This Lease explains how the provider assesses each charge and how charges covering multiple tenants are allocated. Landlord will bill Tenant in writing within 30 days after receiving the provider's bill, will include a copy of the provider's bill or tell Tenant how to inspect it, and will charge only the provider's cost except as Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.315(4)(c) allows for video and internet services.
- **Carbon monoxide alarm** (Or. Rev. Stat. §§ 90.316, 90.317) *Only if the dwelling unit contains a carbon monoxide source, or is in a structure with a carbon monoxide source connected to the unit by a door, ductwork or ventilation shaft*: The dwelling unit has one or more properly functioning carbon monoxide alarms. Landlord has given Tenant written instructions for testing the alarms and has supplied working batteries for any battery-operated alarm at the start of the tenancy. Tenant will test the alarms as instructed and notify Landlord in writing of any deficiency other than dead batteries.
- **Pending foreclosure or legal proceedings** (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.310) *Only for premises with four or fewer dwelling units that are subject to one of these circumstances when this Lease is signed; must be disclosed in writing before signing*: Landlord discloses that the premises are subject to one or more of the following, as identified by Landlord: an outstanding notice of default or notice of trustee's sale under a trust deed, mortgage or contract of sale; a pending suit to foreclose a mortgage, trust deed or vendor's lien; a pending declaration of forfeiture or suit for specific performance of a contract of sale; or a pending proceeding to foreclose a tax lien.
- **Deposits and fees** (Or. Rev. Stat. §§ 90.300(3), 90.302(1)): This Lease lists the security deposit Tenant paid or Landlord requires and describes every fee Landlord may charge. Landlord will charge only the fees allowed by Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.302 and described in this Lease.
- **Late charge terms** (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.260(1)(b)): This Lease states Tenant's obligation to pay a late charge on delinquent rent, the type and amount of the late charge, the date rent is due, and the date on which the late charge becomes due.
- **Renter's liability insurance** (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.222(1), (5), (7)) *Only if this Lease requires renter's liability insurance; not permitted where household income is at or below 50% of area median income or the unit is publicly subsidized as described in the statute*: If this Lease requires Tenant to obtain renter's liability insurance, Landlord obtains and maintains comparable liability insurance and will provide documentation of it to any tenant who asks, in person, by mail or by posting in a common area or office. The required coverage will not exceed $100,000 per occurrence or the customary amount for similar properties, whichever is greater, and Landlord will not require a particular insurer or that Landlord be named as an additional insured.
- **Carpet cleaning** (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.300(7)(c)(A)) *Only if Landlord intends to deduct carpet cleaning from the deposit*: Landlord may deduct the cost of machine carpet cleaning from the security deposit regardless of whether Tenant cleans the carpet before delivering possession, provided the carpet was cleaned or replaced after the previous tenancy and before Tenant took possession.

## Open questions for the reviewing attorney

- EARLY TERMINATION FEE CAP (surprising): ORS 90.302(2)(e) caps a fixed-term lease-break fee at 1.5x monthly rent, and if the landlord elects that fee it cannot also collect rent after it knew of the abandonment or re-renting costs. ORS 90.302(5) and 90.245(1)(d) ban every other form of liquidated damages, and a landlord who knowingly uses a lease with prohibited provisions faces up to three months' rent (90.245(2)). The generator's early termination fee clause for Oregon must be optional, must be stated in the lease, must not exceed 1.5 months, and must say the landlord then gives up future rent and re-letting costs. A violation of 90.302 exposes the landlord to twice actual damages or $300 (90.302(8)).
- TWO state military statutes: ORS 90.475 (enlisting, reserve/Guard ordered to active service outside 'the area' for 90+ days, terminating active service, PHS and NOAA; 'area' is not defined) and ORS 90.472 (Oregon militia called to active state service by the Governor for 90+ consecutive days). Both bar any penalty, fee, charge or loss of deposit. 90.475 is in `military`; 90.472 is in `other` because the schema allows one military entry. 90.475 was amended in 2025 (ch. 453). Related: ORS 105.111 lets a court stay eviction up to 90 days for a state service member (30+ days of state service) whose dependents occupy a unit renting for $1,200/month or less.
- DV/sexual assault/bias crime/stalking release (90.453) requires only 14 days' notice, releases the victim and listed immediate family members, keeps other tenants bound (90.456), and has no termination fee. Waiver barred by 90.245(1)(a). Bias crime is included; human trafficking is not separately listed.
- RENT CAP and JUST CAUSE (statewide): ORS 90.323/90.324 bar any rent increase in the first year, require 90 days' written notice, allow one increase per 12 months, and cap increases at 7% + CPI (max 10%) unless the first certificate of occupancy is under 15 years old or the unit is regulated affordable housing; violations cost three months' rent plus damages. ORS 90.427 limits landlord no-cause terminations to the first year of occupancy (30 days) and requires a qualifying landlord reason, 90 days' notice and one month's rent relocation payment (unless the landlord owns 4 or fewer units) thereafter; owner-occupied duplex/ADU exception in 90.427(8) (60 days). Violations: three months' rent plus damages. These are not encoded beyond the notice text; the generator's renewal/termination and rent-increase clauses must comply.
- Late fee: Oregon allows three alternative late-charge structures (90.260(2)); capType is 'reasonable' because none is a single numeric cap. The written lease must state the type and amount and due dates (90.260(1)(b)). Late charges cannot make later rent 'delinquent' (90.260(4)).
- Nonpayment notice: two options (10-day notice served on/after day 8, or 13-day notice served on/after day 5); encoded as 10. Every termination notice must include the veterans' information required by ORS 408.515 (90.391), and every nonpayment notice and summons must include the Judicial Department rental assistance notice (90.395) or the case will be dismissed.
- Deposit: no statewide cap and no interest. Labor charges must be at a reasonable hourly rate; failure to give the 31-day accounting exposes landlord to twice the amount withheld (90.300(16)). Fees are limited to those listed in 90.302; general 'move-in', 'admin' or 'redecorating' fees for anticipated landlord expenses are prohibited (90.302(1)).
- Payments: landlord must apply tenant payments in the statutory order (rent arrears, current rent, utilities, late charges, then fees/damages) notwithstanding any lease clause (90.220(9)).
- ORS 90.155(5) electronic-mail service of notices requires specific written-agreement terms; if Keycove wants e-mail notices in Oregon, the lease needs a compliant addendum (not researched in detail here).
- LOCAL LAW: Portland (Portland City Code ch. 30.01: relocation assistance for no-cause terminations and 10%+ rent increases, FAIR ordinance rules on security deposits, move-in condition reports and screening criteria, and rental registration; verify current requirements), Milwaukie, Bend and Eugene have additional tenant protections. Don't encode; flag for Portland-area properties.
- Applicant-stage rules (not lease terms): 90.295 screening charges, 90.297 holding deposits, 90.303/90.304 evaluation and denial statements, 90.306 (2025) ban on asking about immigration status, 90.308 (2025) affordability-restriction notice.
