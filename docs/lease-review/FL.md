## Early termination rights that override the lease-break fee

### Military service (federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act)
*50 U.S.C. § 3955*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant enters military service after signing it; or if Tenant, while in military service, signed this Lease and then receives orders for a permanent change of station (which includes separation or retirement) or to deploy with a military unit or in support of a military operation for 90 days or more; or if Tenant signed this Lease upon such orders and then receives a stop movement order of 30 days or more (or indefinite) that prevents occupancy. A spouse or dependent may terminate within one year after Tenant dies in military service or while performing covered National Guard or Reserve duty, and a Tenant who suffers a catastrophic injury or illness in such service (or the spouse or dependent, if Tenant lacks capacity) may terminate within one year after it occurs. Termination also ends the obligations of Tenant's dependents under this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant delivers written notice to Landlord by hand, private business carrier, mail with return receipt requested, or electronic means. Because rent is paid monthly, termination is effective 30 days after the first date on which the next rent payment is due after the notice is delivered; termination after a stop movement order is effective when the notice and orders are delivered.
- **Documentation:** The notice must include a copy of the military orders (official orders, including orders for separation or retirement, or a notification, certification, or verification from the commanding officer).
- **Owes:** Rent is owed only on a prorated basis through the effective date of termination. Landlord may not impose an early termination charge, but Tenant still owes unpaid amounts due under this Lease, including reasonable charges for excess wear. Rent paid in advance for any period after termination will be refunded within 30 days after the effective date. Landlord may not hold Tenant's belongings or security deposit to satisfy a claim for rent accruing after termination.

### Military service (Florida law)
*Fla. Stat. §§ 83.682, 83.43(15)-(16), 250.01(19), (21)*

- **Who:** A servicemember (any member of the United States Armed Forces on active duty or state active duty, and any member of the Florida National Guard or the United States Reserve Forces) may terminate this Lease if: (1) permanent change of station orders require a move of 35 miles or more from the Property; (2) the servicemember is prematurely or involuntarily discharged or released from active duty or state active duty; (3) the servicemember is released from active duty or state active duty after leasing the Property while on that duty, and the Property is 35 miles or more from the servicemember's home of record before entering that duty; (4) after signing this Lease, the servicemember receives orders to move into government quarters (including privatized military housing) or becomes eligible for and chooses to move into government quarters; (5) the servicemember receives temporary duty orders, temporary change of station orders, or state active duty orders for more than 60 days to an area 35 miles or more from the Property; or (6) before taking possession, the servicemember receives a change of orders to an area 35 miles or more from the Property. If a servicemember dies during active duty, an adult member of the servicemember's immediate family may terminate this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives Landlord written notice of termination, effective on a date stated in the notice that is at least 30 days after Landlord receives it.
- **Documentation:** The notice must include a copy of the official military orders or a written verification signed by the servicemember's commanding officer. For a death during active duty, the notice must include a copy of the orders showing active duty or the commanding officer's written verification, and a copy of the death certificate.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes rent prorated to the effective date of termination, payable when it would otherwise be due, and no other rent or damages because of the early termination, including the early termination fee. No damages or penalties of any kind are owed if Tenant terminates 14 or more days before occupancy. This right cannot be waived or modified by agreement.

### Casualty damage
*Fla. Stat. § 83.63*

- **Who:** If the Property is damaged or destroyed, other than by the wrongful or negligent acts of Tenant, so that Tenant's enjoyment of it is substantially impaired, Tenant may terminate this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant may terminate and vacate immediately. Tenant may instead vacate only the part of the Property made unusable, in which case rent is reduced by the fair rental value of that part.
- **Owes:** No early termination fee is owed. Landlord handles the security deposit as required by Fla. Stat. § 83.49(3). Tenant will be given the opportunity to collect belongings when it is safe to do so, or notice of a date within a reasonable time by which Tenant may collect them.

### Landlord's material noncompliance
*Fla. Stat. § 83.56(1)*

- **Who:** If Landlord materially fails to comply with Fla. Stat. § 83.51(1) (building, housing, and health codes, or structural and plumbing maintenance where no code applies) or with material provisions of this Lease, Tenant may terminate this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant delivers written notice specifying the noncompliance and stating Tenant's intent to terminate for that reason. Tenant may terminate if Landlord does not comply within 7 days after the notice is delivered.
- **Owes:** No early termination fee is owed. If the failure is beyond Landlord's control and Landlord is making every reasonable effort to correct it, and the Property is untenantable and Tenant vacates, Tenant owes no rent while the Property remains uninhabitable.

### Flooding after a missing flood disclosure
*Fla. Stat. § 83.512(2)-(4)*

- **Who:** If Landlord did not provide the flood disclosure required by Fla. Stat. § 83.512 and Tenant suffers substantial loss or damage to Tenant's personal property from flooding (repair or replacement costing 50% or more of the property's market value on the date of the flood), Tenant may terminate this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives written notice of termination and surrenders possession no later than 30 days after the loss or damage. Termination is effective when Tenant surrenders possession.
- **Owes:** Landlord refunds all rent and other amounts paid in advance for any period after termination. Tenant still owes any delinquent rent or other sums owed before termination. No early termination fee is owed.

## Additional state clauses

- **Florida Early Termination Fee Addendum Required** (Fla. Stat. § 83.595(4)): The early termination fee option in this Lease applies only if Tenant signed, when this Lease was made, a separate addendum in substantially the following form and checked the first box: "[ ] I agree, as provided in the rental agreement, to pay $____ (an amount that does not exceed 2 months' rent) as liquidated damages or an early termination fee if I elect to terminate the rental agreement, and the landlord waives the right to seek additional rent beyond the month in which the landlord retakes possession. [ ] I do not agree to liquidated damages or an early termination fee, and I acknowledge that the landlord may seek damages as provided by law." When the fee applies, Landlord may also collect rent and other charges accrued through the end of the month in which Landlord retakes possession and charges for damage to the Property, and the advance notice required of Tenant may not exceed 60 days.
- **Electronic Delivery of Notices** (Fla. Stat. §§ 83.505, 83.56(4)): Notices required by this Lease or by Part II of Chapter 83, Florida Statutes, may be delivered by e-mail only if Landlord and Tenant have signed a separate addendum in the form required by Fla. Stat. § 83.505, each designating an e-mail address. That election is voluntary and either party may revoke it or update the designated e-mail address at any time by written notice. Otherwise, notices are delivered by mail or by hand, or, if Tenant is absent from the Property, by leaving a copy at the residence.

## Open questions for the reviewing attorney

- No domestic violence lease-termination statute. The 2026 text of Part II of Chapter 83 has no such section; bills filed in 2005, 2007, 2009, and 2022 were never enacted. domesticViolence is null, and VAWA protections in federally assisted housing are outside this profile.
- EARLY TERMINATION FEE (important): § 83.595(4) caps liquidated damages or an early termination fee at two months' rent, limits the required notice to 60 days, and makes the fee available ONLY if the tenant checked 'I agree' on a SEPARATE ADDENDUM in the statutory form at signing. compute.ts clamps the notice period at 90 days (MAX_EARLY_TERMINATION_NOTICE_DAYS); Florida needs a 60-day clamp. Keycove also needs to generate and collect the separate addendum. The additionalClauses entry quotes the form but is not itself a separate signed addendum. If the tenant declines, the builder's paragraph (a) fee must not be charged.
- E-MAIL NOTICES: § 83.505 (2025, ch. 2025-16) allows e-mail delivery of Chapter 83 notices only after both parties sign a voluntary addendum in the statutory form. The builder's General Provisions say notices may go 'by email ... unless the law requires a particular method,' which arguably conflicts in Florida. The FL additional clause states the addendum condition, and Keycove should offer the § 83.505 addendum as a separate signed document.
- FLOOD DISCLOSURE: § 83.512 (ch. 2025-166, effective 2025) requires a separate-document flood disclosure in the statutory form, delivered at or before execution of any lease of 1 year or longer. The lease disclosure only recites that it was given. Keycove must generate the separate form, with the three has/has not checkboxes and the definition of 'flooding'.
- Military: § 83.682 is broad. It covers active duty AND state active duty, all Florida National Guard and Reserve members (via § 250.01(19)), government-quarters moves, and orders of more than 60 days at 35 miles or more, and it bars any damages or penalties. It cannot be waived. § 83.67(3) also bars discrimination against servicemembers in renting or lease terms.
- Deposit interest is required only if the landlord elects an interest-bearing account or a surety bond (5%), so interestRequired is false. The 30-day depository notice and the § 83.49(2)(d) disclosure are mandatory only for landlords renting five or more units, but including them is harmless. The returnDays value (30) is the deadline for the notice of claim. With no claim, the deposit is due in 15 days.
- § 83.49(5): the 7-day move-out notice from tenant applies 'except when otherwise provided by the terms of a written rental agreement.' The lease restates the statutory default.
- § 83.575: a fixed-term lease may require 30 to 60 days' notice of non-renewal from the tenant only if it imposes the same notice duty on the landlord. Liquidated damages for missing that notice require a landlord reminder 15 days before the notice period. The builder does not use this; § 83.575(3) adds one month's rent if a holdover tenant fails to give month-to-month notice.
- No late-fee statute. Courts apply general liquidated-damages and unconscionability rules (§ 83.45). Late fees may be designated as 'rent' in a written lease (§ 83.43(12)), which brings them into the 3-day notice. Attorney to decide whether the builder should designate late fees as rent.
- Mitigation: § 83.595 lets the landlord choose to (1) terminate and release the tenant, (2) retake possession for the tenant's account with a good-faith duty to relet, (3) stand by and collect rent as it comes due, or (4) charge the addendum fee. The builder's paragraph (c) (rent until a replacement's rent begins, plus reletting costs) corresponds to option (2).
- PREEMPTION: § 83.425 (ch. 2023-314) preempts all local regulation of residential tenancies, including screening, deposits, application fees, lease terms, disclosures, fees, and notices. Local 'tenant bill of rights' ordinances adopted before 2023 are preempted as to Part II matters. Specific local ordinances were not reviewed. Local rent control is separately restricted by § 125.0103 (not read).
- 2026 amendment: § 83.56 was amended by ch. 2026-143. The 2026 text read includes fraudulent-entry noncompliance (§ 817.537(2)) as non-curable. The 3-day nonpayment notice is unchanged.
- § 83.491 (fee in lieu of security deposit, 2023) was not researched in detail. If Keycove offers deposit-alternative products in Florida, that section's disclosure and opt-in rules apply.
- § 83.67(5): to avoid § 715.104 abandoned-property procedures, the lease may include a specific capitalized legend. The builder does not include it.
- Habitability: § 83.51(1) duties may be altered in writing for a single-family home or duplex. The § 83.51(2) duties apply 'unless otherwise agreed in writing.'
