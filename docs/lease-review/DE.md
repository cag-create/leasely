## Early termination rights that override the lease-break fee

### Military service (federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act)
*50 U.S.C. § 3955*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant enters military service after signing it; or if Tenant, while in military service, signed this Lease and then receives orders for a permanent change of station (which includes separation or retirement) or to deploy with a military unit or in support of a military operation for 90 days or more; or if Tenant signed this Lease upon such orders and then receives a stop movement order of 30 days or more (or indefinite) that prevents occupancy. A spouse or dependent may terminate within one year after Tenant dies in military service or while performing covered National Guard or Reserve duty, and a Tenant who suffers a catastrophic injury or illness in such service (or the spouse or dependent, if Tenant lacks capacity) may terminate within one year after it occurs. Termination also ends the obligations of Tenant's dependents under this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant delivers written notice to Landlord by hand, private business carrier, mail with return receipt requested, or electronic means. Because rent is paid monthly, termination is effective 30 days after the first date on which the next rent payment is due after the notice is delivered; termination after a stop movement order is effective when the notice and orders are delivered.
- **Documentation:** The notice must include a copy of the military orders (official orders, including orders for separation or retirement, or a notification, certification, or verification from the commanding officer).
- **Owes:** Rent is owed only on a prorated basis through the effective date of termination. Landlord may not impose an early termination charge, but Tenant still owes unpaid amounts due under this Lease, including reasonable charges for excess wear. Rent paid in advance for any period after termination will be refunded within 30 days after the effective date. Landlord may not hold Tenant's belongings or security deposit to satisfy a claim for rent accruing after termination.

### Entry into active military service (Delaware law)
*25 Del. C. § 5314(b)(5)*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if, after signing it, Tenant enters the military service of the United States on active duty.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives Landlord at least 30 days' written notice, and the 30-day period begins on the first day of the month after Landlord actually receives the notice.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes rent through the termination date, after which the parties' duties under this Lease end. Tenant does not owe the early termination fee. Delaware law limits any reimbursement for Landlord's reasonable expenses of renovating and re-renting the Property caused by this termination to no more than one month's rent.

### Domestic abuse, sexual offenses, or stalking
*25 Del. C. §§ 5314(b)(6), 5141*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant is a victim of domestic abuse, a sexual offense, or stalking, or has obtained or is seeking relief from domestic violence or abuse from any court, police agency, or domestic violence program or service.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives Landlord at least 30 days' written notice, and the 30-day period begins on the first day of the month after Landlord actually receives the notice.
- **Documentation:** The abuse, sexual offense, or stalking may be verified by an official document such as a court order, or by a statement from a reliable third-party professional such as a law enforcement agency or officer, a domestic violence or sexual assault service provider, or a health care provider. It is Tenant's responsibility to provide the statement from the third party.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes rent through the termination date, after which the parties' duties under this Lease end. Tenant does not owe the early termination fee. Delaware law limits any reimbursement for Landlord's reasonable expenses of renovating and re-renting the Property caused by this termination to no more than one month's rent. Landlord may not evict, raise rent, reduce services, or otherwise force out a tenant because the tenant is a victim who has sought help for domestic abuse, sexual offenses, or stalking.

### Job relocation, serious illness, or death in the family
*25 Del. C. § 5314(b)(1)–(2)*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if a change in the location of Tenant's employment with Tenant's present employer requires Tenant to move more than 30 miles, or if the serious illness of Tenant, or the death or serious illness of a member of Tenant's immediate family living in the Property, requires Tenant to move permanently.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives Landlord at least 30 days' written notice, and the 30-day period begins on the first day of the month after Landlord actually receives the notice.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes rent through the termination date and no early termination fee. Any reimbursement for Landlord's reasonable renovation and re-renting expenses caused by the termination may not exceed one month's rent.

### Admission to senior, retirement, or subsidized housing
*25 Del. C. § 5314(b)(3)–(4)*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant is accepted for admission to a senior citizens' housing facility (including subsidized public or private housing), a group or cooperative living facility, or a retirement home, or is accepted for admission into a rental unit subsidized by a government entity or a private nonprofit corporation.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives Landlord at least 30 days' written notice, and the 30-day period begins on the first day of the month after Landlord actually receives the notice.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes rent through the termination date and no early termination fee. Any reimbursement for Landlord's reasonable renovation and re-renting expenses caused by the termination may not exceed one month's rent.

### Death of Tenant
*25 Del. C. § 5314(b)(7)*

- **Who:** If Tenant dies, Tenant's surviving spouse or the personal representative of Tenant's estate may terminate this Lease.
- **Notice:** The surviving spouse or personal representative gives Landlord at least 30 days' written notice, and the 30-day period begins on the first day of the month after Landlord actually receives the notice.
- **Owes:** Rent is owed through the termination date and no early termination fee is owed. Any reimbursement for Landlord's reasonable renovation and re-renting expenses caused by the termination may not exceed one month's rent.

### Uninhabitable conditions or loss of essential services
*25 Del. C. §§ 5306, 5308*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if a condition deprives Tenant of a substantial part of the benefit of the bargain and Landlord does not remedy it within 15 days after receiving written notice, or immediately after notice if the condition makes the Property uninhabitable or poses an imminent threat to health or safety. Tenant may also terminate if Landlord substantially fails to provide hot water, heat, water, or electricity for 48 hours or more after Tenant gives actual or written notice. These rights do not apply to a condition caused by the lack of due care of Tenant, a family member, or a guest.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives Landlord written notice of the condition and, where required, written notice of termination.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes no early termination fee. If Landlord caused the condition willfully or negligently, Tenant may recover the greater of the extra cost of equivalent substitute housing for the rest of the term or one month's rent plus the security deposit.

### Fire or casualty
*25 Del. C. § 5309*

- **Who:** If the Property is damaged or destroyed by fire or casualty, without fault of Tenant, a member of Tenant's family, or a guest, so that enjoyment of the Property is substantially impaired, Tenant may immediately move out.
- **Notice:** Tenant notifies Landlord in writing of the election to quit within one week after vacating, and this Lease ends on the date Tenant vacates. If Tenant does not give that notice, Tenant owes rent until Landlord actually learns that Tenant has vacated or that occupancy is impossible.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes rent only up to the date of the fire or casualty and no early termination fee. Landlord shall promptly return the security deposit, pet deposit, and prepaid rent except amounts Landlord may lawfully keep.

### Unlawful ouster by Landlord
*25 Del. C. § 5313*

- **Who:** If Landlord or Landlord's agent removes or excludes Tenant from the Property without a valid court order, Tenant may recover possession or terminate this Lease.
- **Notice:** No advance notice period is required.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes no early termination fee, and Tenant may recover the greater of three times the damages sustained or three times the daily rent for the period Tenant was excluded, plus court costs.


## State disclosures in the lease

- **Owner and Agent Identity** (25 Del. C. § 5105): The names and usual business addresses of all owners of the Property (or their appointed resident agents), and of any person acting as Landlord, are stated in this Lease. Landlord will give Tenant a copy of this signed Lease free of charge.
- **Summary of the Landlord-Tenant Code** (25 Del. C. § 5118): At the beginning of the rental term Landlord will give Tenant the summary of the Delaware Residential Landlord-Tenant Code prepared by the Consumer Protection Unit of the Attorney General's Office. If Landlord fails to provide it, Tenant may plead ignorance of the law as a defense.
- **Information on Legal Representation for Tenants** (25 Del. C. §§ 5602(b)(1), 5606): When this Lease is signed, Landlord will give Tenant the informational materials approved by the Right to Representation Coordinator explaining the legal representation available to tenants, including the right to representation in eviction proceedings, and available resources. Landlord will provide the same materials again with any past-due rent notice, breach notice, or termination notice covered by law.
- **Security Deposit Account** (25 Del. C. § 5514(b), (g)(2)): Landlord will disclose to Tenant the location of the security deposit escrow account. If Landlord fails to disclose the location within 20 days after Tenant's written request, or fails to keep the deposit in a federally insured institution with an office in Delaware that accepts deposits, Landlord forfeits the security deposit to Tenant.
- **Bed Bugs** (25 Del. C. § 5317): Before renting, Landlord has visually inspected the Property for evidence of bed bugs and will disclose to Tenant whether any adjacent unit is currently infested with or being treated for bed bugs. Landlord will not rent a unit Landlord knows or reasonably suspects has a current bed bug infestation. If Tenant reports a suspected infestation within 60 days after move-in, or within 30 days after an infestation is discovered in an adjoining unit, Landlord pays the costs of investigation and remediation; after that, Landlord remains responsible for investigation and remediation but Tenant shares in the reasonable cost of remediating Tenant's unit.
- **No Nonrefundable Fees** (25 Del. C. §§ 5311, 5514(d)): Except for optional fees for services actually rendered (such as a pool or tennis court fee), Landlord does not charge any nonrefundable fee as a condition of occupancy. Any application fee may not exceed the greater of 10% of the monthly rent or $50.

## Open questions for the reviewing attorney

- 25 Del. C. § 5101(a) makes any rental agreement provision unenforceable insofar as it conflicts with the Code and 'is not expressly authorized herein.' The generator's optional liquidated early termination fee (default 2 months' rent) is not expressly authorized and may conflict with the § 5507(d) abandonment damages formula. Attorney should confirm the fee option is enforceable in Delaware, or whether DE leases should drop it or cap it. feeCap is null because the only statutory cap found (§ 5514(c)(3): renovation/re-renting reimbursement may not exceed 1 month's rent) applies only to § 5314 terminations, not to an ordinary lease break.
- Inconsistency to resolve in builder: § 5514(c)(3) expressly lets the landlord apply the deposit to reasonable renovation and re-renting expenses caused by a § 5314 termination (capped at 1 month's rent), but the builder's deposit paragraph promises not to apply the deposit to early termination damages after a paragraph (d) termination. The liability text in each § 5314 right states the statutory cap; the builder's promise is more tenant-protective, so it is not unlawful, but the two read inconsistently.
- § 5314 does not itself require documentation for any ground (military, illness, job relocation, senior/subsidized housing). For DV, § 5141 says abuse 'can be verified' by a court order or a reliable third-party statement and puts the burden on the victim to supply it; I described that as the verification method rather than a strict condition. No co-tenant release rule and no explicit waiver-ban language in § 5314 itself, but § 5301(a)(1) bars any lease clause waiving Code rights.
- § 5314 notice: 30 days, but the period starts on the first day of the month following actual notice, so effective notice is 30 to 61 days. Same structure for 60-day month-to-month notice under § 5106(d).
- End-of-term notice tension: § 5106(c) says a tenant 'may terminate' a fixed term by 60 days' written notice before expiration, while § 5108(a) converts a 1-year-plus lease to month-to-month only if the tenant fails to give 45 days' notice. Both are stated in monthToMonthRule; attorney should confirm which controls for tenants who give 45–59 days' notice.
- Deposit cap applies only to leases of 1 year or more and to month-to-month tenancies after 1 year; furnished units are uncapped; a separate pet deposit up to 1 month is allowed. maxMonths is set to 1 for the standard 12-month unfurnished lease. A short-term (<1 year) unfurnished lease has no statutory cap.
- Disclosures not yet in force, so not encoded: (1) § 5124 guide (85 Del. Laws c. 277, approved June 10, 2026) must be given to a prospective tenant before signing, with signed acknowledgment, once implemented — implementation is the earlier of June 10, 2027 or a Division of Professional Regulation notice; violation is a consumer-fraud unlawful practice. Add it to DE disclosures on implementation. (2) § 5406 lead-safe/lead-free certification disclosure in every lease for pre-1978 units, implemented no later than March 1, 2028 (85 Del. Laws c. 98 § 5).
- § 5102 exclusions change on March 8, 2027 (new list; still excludes transient lodging and certified recovery houses). Co-living/room rentals are covered by the Code (rental unit = 'portion' of a structure occupied as a residence).
- § 5502(c)–(d) (amended by 85 Del. Laws c. 436): landlord may not refuse rent paid by or on behalf of tenant and cannot maintain a nonpayment action once all rent due is paid, even after filing; after judgment landlord may require certified check or money order.
- No Delaware state statute giving National Guard members on state active duty lease-termination rights was found (searched Title 20 PDF for lease/rental/dwelling terms). Only § 5314(b)(5) (entering federal active duty after signing) applies.
- § 5125 (key surrender): if the lease conditions deposit return on key surrender, it must contain a clear key-surrender procedure and may not require surrender before the end of the term. Builder's general text requires returning keys on early move-out; attorney should confirm a key-return procedure is stated somewhere if deposit return depends on it.
- § 5123 tenant-employee disclosure form required if housing is part of employee compensation (not encoded). § 5301(a)(4): lease may not require declawing a cat. § 5111: attorney-fee clauses unenforceable. § 5121: confession of judgment void.
- Local: Wilmington and other municipalities have rental licensing/registration and inspection ordinances; no rent control in Delaware. Sussex County beach-area Hundreds exclude nonrenewable seasonal rentals of 120 days or less (§ 5102(4)).
