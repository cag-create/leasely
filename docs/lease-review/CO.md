## Early termination rights that override the lease-break fee

### Military service (federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act)
*50 U.S.C. § 3955*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant enters military service after signing it; or if Tenant, while in military service, signed this Lease and then receives orders for a permanent change of station (which includes separation or retirement) or to deploy with a military unit or in support of a military operation for 90 days or more; or if Tenant signed this Lease upon such orders and then receives a stop movement order of 30 days or more (or indefinite) that prevents occupancy. A spouse or dependent may terminate within one year after Tenant dies in military service or while performing covered National Guard or Reserve duty, and a Tenant who suffers a catastrophic injury or illness in such service (or the spouse or dependent, if Tenant lacks capacity) may terminate within one year after it occurs. Termination also ends the obligations of Tenant's dependents under this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant delivers written notice to Landlord by hand, private business carrier, mail with return receipt requested, or electronic means. Because rent is paid monthly, termination is effective 30 days after the first date on which the next rent payment is due after the notice is delivered; termination after a stop movement order is effective when the notice and orders are delivered.
- **Documentation:** The notice must include a copy of the military orders (official orders, including orders for separation or retirement, or a notification, certification, or verification from the commanding officer).
- **Owes:** Rent is owed only on a prorated basis through the effective date of termination. Landlord may not impose an early termination charge, but Tenant still owes unpaid amounts due under this Lease, including reasonable charges for excess wear. Rent paid in advance for any period after termination will be refunded within 30 days after the effective date. Landlord may not hold Tenant's belongings or security deposit to satisfy a claim for rent accruing after termination.

### Unlawful sexual behavior, stalking, domestic violence, or domestic abuse
*C.R.S. §§ 38-12-401, 38-12-402, 38-12-103(1)*

- **Who:** A Tenant who is a victim-survivor of unlawful sexual behavior, stalking, domestic violence, or domestic abuse and who intends to vacate the Property because of fear of danger from that conduct may terminate this Lease and vacate without further obligation except as stated below.
- **Notice:** Tenant, or a qualified third party on Tenant's behalf, notifies Landlord in writing that Tenant is a victim-survivor and intends to vacate. The statute sets no minimum advance-notice period. Tenant may use this right even while an eviction case is pending, as long as the court has not yet entered an eviction order.
- **Documentation:** Tenant provides at least one of the following: (1) a self-attestation affidavit stating that Tenant is a victim-survivor and that the incident caused or contributed to the termination, naming the responsible party if known and if Tenant determines it is safe to do so; (2) a letter signed by a qualified third party from whom Tenant sought assistance, such as a victim-services provider, attorney, social worker, medical professional, faith leader, or mental health provider, stating that they believe the incident occurred and is the grounds for terminating; (3) a police report from the prior 120 days stating that Tenant reported the conduct; or (4) a valid civil, criminal, or emergency protection order or restraining order. Landlord may require a qualified third-party letter only if the documentation provided contains conflicting information, and may not require any other documentation. If Tenant has not yet provided documentation, Tenant must do so within 7 days after Landlord's written request. Landlord will not disclose that Tenant is a victim-survivor, or Tenant's new address, to anyone, including a co-tenant, without Tenant's written consent or a court order.
- **Owes:** If Landlord incurs economic damages as a direct result of the early termination and gives Tenant a written statement documenting those actual damages within 30 days after Tenant vacates, Tenant owes no more than one month's rent, payable within 90 days after vacating; if Landlord does not provide that statement within 30 days, Landlord forfeits any right to collect future rent. If another tenant remains on this Lease, Landlord will not collect that month's rent from the victim-survivor. Landlord may hold the security deposit until any such month's rent is paid, but will not deduct from the deposit or otherwise charge the victim-survivor for damage caused by the abuse. Landlord will not assign this debt to a collector without first complying with these rules and giving at least 90 days' written notice.

### Breach of the warranty of habitability
*C.R.S. §§ 38-12-503, 38-12-507(1)(a)–(b), 38-12-503(4)(c)*

- **Who:** If Landlord breaches the warranty of habitability (the Property is uninhabitable or materially interferes with Tenant's life, health, or safety, and Landlord fails to begin remedial action within 24 hours or 72 hours, as applicable, after notice, or fails to complete it within a reasonable time) and the condition remains unremedied, Tenant may terminate this Lease. Tenant may also terminate if a remedied condition recurs within six months, or if Landlord notifies Tenant that a condition requiring hotel accommodations cannot be remedied within 60 days.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives Landlord 10 to 60 days' written notice stating the unremedied condition, Tenant's intent to terminate and vacate, and a termination date at least 10 days after the notice. For a recurring condition, Tenant gives the notice within 30 days after the condition recurs, with a termination date at least 10 days after the notice. If Landlord begins or completes the remedy before the termination date, the parties may agree in writing to continue this Lease.
- **Owes:** Tenant may terminate without any liability or financial penalty. Any lease provision charging a fee or penalty for exercising this right is void.

### Hazardous gas condition not repaired
*C.R.S. § 38-12-104*

- **Who:** If gas service personnel notify Tenant in writing of a hazardous condition in a gas appliance, piping, or other gas equipment, Tenant immediately informs Landlord in writing, and Landlord does not have the hazard repaired by a licensed or otherwise qualified professional within 72 hours (excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays) after actually receiving that notice, Tenant may vacate the Property.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives Landlord written notice of the hazard and, after the 72-hour period, may vacate; this Lease becomes void when Tenant vacates. Tenant must give Landlord a correct forwarding address.
- **Documentation:** The written notice of the hazardous condition from the gas service personnel.
- **Owes:** All future obligations under this Lease end. Within 72 hours after Tenant vacates, Landlord will deliver the security deposit (less lawful deductions with a written statement of reasons) plus any rent paid for the period after Tenant vacated.

### Landlord retaliation
*C.R.S. § 38-12-509(1)–(2)*

- **Who:** If Landlord retaliates against Tenant (for example, by raising rent, decreasing services, refusing to renew, threatening eviction, harassing Tenant, or charging fees) because Tenant made a good-faith complaint about habitability, organized or joined a tenants' association, or exercised a habitability remedy, Tenant may terminate this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives Landlord written notice of termination.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes no early termination fee and may recover the greater of three months' rent or three times actual damages, plus attorney fees and costs.

### Elevated radon or missing radon disclosure
*C.R.S. § 38-12-803(3)*

- **Who:** For a lease with a term longer than one year, if Landlord fails to provide the radon disclosure required by Colorado law, or fails to make a reasonable effort to mitigate radon within 180 days after being notified that a radon measurement professional found an air concentration of 4 picocuries per liter or more, Tenant may void this Lease and vacate.
- **Notice:** Tenant follows the notice procedure for terminating for breach of the warranty of habitability: 10 to 60 days' written notice with a termination date at least 10 days after the notice.
- **Documentation:** For the mitigation ground, the radon measurement professional's results.
- **Owes:** Tenant may terminate without any liability or financial penalty.

### Death of a tenant
*C.R.S. § 38-12-801(3.5)*

- **Who:** If this Lease is terminated before the end of its term because of the death of a Tenant, the limits below apply.
- **Notice:** The personal representative of the deceased Tenant's estate, or another person acting for the estate, notifies Landlord of the death and of the surrender of the Property.
- **Owes:** Landlord will not charge liquidated damages; accelerated rent for any period beyond the end of the month or more than 10 business days after the unit is vacated following notice of the death, whichever is later; repayment of concessions or move-in discounts; or any other fee, damages, or penalty for the early termination. Landlord may retain deposit amounts needed to cover damage caused by the death.


## State disclosures in the lease

- **Landlord or Authorized Agent** (C.R.S. § 38-12-801(1)–(2)): The name and address of Landlord, or of Landlord's authorized agent, are stated in this Lease. If the identity of Landlord or Landlord's authorized agent changes, the new landlord or agent will, within one business day, give Tenant written or electronic notice of the change or post the new identity in a conspicuous location on the premises. Landlord will provide Tenant a copy of this Lease signed by both parties no later than seven days after Tenant signs it (on paper if Tenant requests).
- **Source of Income Discrimination Prohibited** (C.R.S. §§ 38-12-801(2.5), 24-34-502(1)) *Not required if Landlord owns five or fewer single-family rental homes and no more than five total rental units*: Colorado law, C.R.S. § 24-34-502(1), prohibits source of income discrimination and requires a non-exempt landlord to accept any lawful and verifiable source of money paid directly, indirectly, or on behalf of a person, including income derived from any lawful profession or occupation and income or rental payments derived from any government or private assistance, grant, or loan program.
- **Radon Disclosure** (C.R.S. § 38-12-803(2)): The Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment strongly recommends that ALL tenants have an indoor radon test performed before leasing residential real property and recommends having the radon levels mitigated if elevated radon concentrations are found. Elevated radon concentrations can be reduced by a radon mitigation professional. Residential real property may present exposure to dangerous levels of indoor radon gas that may place the occupants at risk of developing radon-induced lung cancer. Radon, a Class A human carcinogen, is the leading cause of lung cancer in nonsmokers and the second leading cause of lung cancer overall. A landlord is required to provide the tenant with any known information on radon test results of the residential real property. Landlord discloses in a radon disclosure attached to this Lease whether any radon test has been conducted on the Property, the most current records and reports of radon concentrations, any radon concentrations detected and any mitigation or remediation performed, and information about any radon mitigation system installed, and has given Tenant a copy of the most recent radon brochure published by the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment. Tenant acknowledges receipt of this disclosure by signing this Lease.
- **Emergency Calls and Safety Measures** (C.R.S. § 38-12-402(1), (7)): Landlord will not terminate this Lease, impose a penalty, or take any adverse action against Tenant because Tenant calls for police or other emergency assistance in response to unlawful sexual behavior, stalking, domestic violence, or domestic abuse, and nothing in this Lease waives Tenant's right to call for police or emergency assistance. A Tenant who has given Landlord the documentation described in C.R.S. § 38-12-402(2)(a.5) may change the locks (including electronic locks) or take other reasonable, nonpermanent safety measures such as installing window bars or cameras without fee or penalty, and will give Landlord a key or access code within 14 days unless the person responsible for the abuse is Landlord or Landlord's manager.
- **Utility Cost Allocation** (C.R.S. § 6-1-737(4.5)(a)) *Only if Landlord allocates utility costs among tenants using a ratio utility billing system*: Landlord allocates utility costs among the tenants of the premises using a ratio utility billing system. The method of allocation for this dwelling unit is stated in this Lease or its utilities addendum. The total billed to all tenants will not exceed the amount the utility provider charges for service to the entire premises, Landlord will not add any markup, surcharge, administrative fee, or other amount beyond the utility's actual charges except as the law allows, and utility costs for common areas and shared facilities are excluded from Tenant's allocation.

## Open questions for the reviewing attorney

- Source currency: values are from the official 2026 C.R.S. (OLLS), which includes 2026 session amendments effective through August 12, 2026 (e.g., SB 26-054 deposit cap carve-out, SB 26-169 DV edits, HB 26-1013 RUBS, HB 26-1196 application notices effective Jan. 1, 2027). 2025 changes incorporated: HB 25-1249 (deposit return overhaul, effective Jan. 1, 2026), HB 25-1168 (victim-survivor protections), HB 25-1108 (death of tenant), HB 25-1090 (junk fees, § 6-1-737), HB 25-1240 (CARES notice, § 38-12-1502).
- Military: Colorado has NO state lease-termination statute for servicemembers. The Colorado State Military Service Civil Relief Act (§§ 28-3-1401 to 1407) only stays evictions and rent actions for persons on state military service or state defense force duty for more than 30 days who gave the landlord written notice within 30 days of call-up. Consider a reviewNote-level clause in the lease; federal SCRA is handled by the generator.
- Domestic violence (§ 38-12-402): self-attestation affidavit is sufficient documentation; maximum liability is one month's rent and only if the landlord documents actual economic damages within 30 days of move-out (otherwise forfeited); no liability at all if a co-tenant remains. Statutory damages for violations: actual damages plus the greater of 3x monthly rent or $5,000. § 38-12-402(5) bars asking applicants about victim-survivor status.
- Late fees (§ 38-12-105): graceDays is set to 7 because no fee may be charged unless rent is late by at least seven calendar days. Cap is the GREATER of $50 or 5% of the past-due payment; $50 penalty per violation and up to $1,000 per uncured violation. Late fees cannot support an eviction.
- For-cause eviction (HB 24-1098, §§ 38-12-1301 to 1307): applies once the tenant has been a tenant for 12 months, except short-term rentals, owner-occupied SFH/duplex/triplex (including owner living on an adjacent property), employer-provided housing, and some mobile home arrangements. This matters for Keycove co-living: an owner-occupied single-family home is exempt, but a non-owner-occupied room rental is NOT. No-fault terminations need 90 days' written notice (45 days for an active-duty military landlord moving in) and a statement of the legal and factual basis. The 'refusal to sign a new lease' ground requires 'reasonable terms.' Non-waivable.
- Fixed-term non-renewal: § 13-40-107 (as amended by HB 24-1098) requires a LANDLORD to give 91 days' written notice to not renew a fixed term of one year or longer (28 days for 6–12 months; 21 days for 1–6 months) for premises/tenants exempt from for-cause; tenants with a fixed term ending at a certain time need not give notice (§ 13-40-107(4)). The builder's renewal/expiration clause must reflect this.
- Nonpayment: 10-day demand (5 days only for an 'exempt residential agreement' — landlord with ≤5 single-family rentals who opts out in the lease; the builder could offer this election). § 38-12-1502 (HB 25-1240) requires compliance with the CARES Act 30-day notice, 15 U.S.C. § 9058(c), 'notwithstanding any changes in federal law' — it is unclear whether this applies to all landlords or only 'covered dwellings' (federally backed mortgages / federal housing programs); the companion directive to help landlords determine covered-dwelling status suggests the latter. Attorney should decide whether Keycove issues a 30-day notice for all Colorado nonpayment cases.
- Mitigation: no Colorado statute. Leading case believed to be Schneiker v. Gordon, 732 P.2d 603 (Colo. 1987) (landlord must mitigate when tenant abandons), but it was NOT located in a primary source during this research — verify.
- Early termination fees: no general statutory cap. But § 38-12-801(3)(a) voids: penalties stemming from an eviction notice or action; one-way attorney-fee clauses (fee-shifting must be prevailing-party, court-determined, reasonable); jury-trial waivers (except possession hearings); class-action waivers; waivers of good faith or quiet enjoyment; fees for failing to give notice of nonrenewal beyond actual losses; labeling non-rent charges as 'rent'; third-party billing markups above 2% or $10/month; and fees violating § 6-1-737 (junk-fee law: fees for property tax, rent processing without a free option, overdue non-rent fees, common-area maintenance, landlord's habitability obligations, or non-utility fees increasing more than 2% during a lease of a year or less). The builder's fee, attorney-fee, and renewal clauses must be checked against these. Owner-occupied duplex/triplex/ADU leases are exempt from some of these (§ 38-12-801(8)).
- Entry: no general entry statute; 24-hour written notice applies to habitability-repair entries and 48 hours to bed bug entries (lease may set a different bed bug notice). The 24-hour notice for other entries is contractual.
- Rent increases: no more than once in any 12-month period (§ 38-12-702); 60 days' written notice if there is no written lease (§ 38-12-701). Rent control remains preempted (§ 38-12-301).
- Applications (§§ 38-12-902 to 905): application fees limited to actual/average cost, must be uniform, receipt and itemization required, portable tenant screening reports must be accepted (with advisement), income requirement capped at 200% of rent, rental/credit history limited to 7 years, criminal history to 5 years with exceptions, written denial notice within 20 days. $2,500 statutory liability per violation. Effective Jan. 1, 2027, applications must describe screening factors and any screening service used (§ 38-12-904(1.7)).
- Immigration status: landlords may not ask about or disclose immigration/citizenship status (§ 38-12-1203), but may request the same financial verification (SSN or ITIN) from all applicants (§ 38-12-1204).
- Local law (not verified here): Denver, Boulder, Aurora, and other Colorado cities have rental licensing programs and additional local tenant protections (e.g., Boulder's eviction-representation program and occupancy limits). Flag for Keycove users in those cities.
