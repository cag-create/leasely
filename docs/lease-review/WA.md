## Early termination rights that override the lease-break fee

### Military service (federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act)
*50 U.S.C. § 3955*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant enters military service after signing it; or if Tenant, while in military service, signed this Lease and then receives orders for a permanent change of station (which includes separation or retirement) or to deploy with a military unit or in support of a military operation for 90 days or more; or if Tenant signed this Lease upon such orders and then receives a stop movement order of 30 days or more (or indefinite) that prevents occupancy. A spouse or dependent may terminate within one year after Tenant dies in military service or while performing covered National Guard or Reserve duty, and a Tenant who suffers a catastrophic injury or illness in such service (or the spouse or dependent, if Tenant lacks capacity) may terminate within one year after it occurs. Termination also ends the obligations of Tenant's dependents under this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant delivers written notice to Landlord by hand, private business carrier, mail with return receipt requested, or electronic means. Because rent is paid monthly, termination is effective 30 days after the first date on which the next rent payment is due after the notice is delivered; termination after a stop movement order is effective when the notice and orders are delivered.
- **Documentation:** The notice must include a copy of the military orders (official orders, including orders for separation or retirement, or a notification, certification, or verification from the commanding officer).
- **Owes:** Rent is owed only on a prorated basis through the effective date of termination. Landlord may not impose an early termination charge, but Tenant still owes unpaid amounts due under this Lease, including reasonable charges for excess wear. Rent paid in advance for any period after termination will be refunded within 30 days after the effective date. Landlord may not hold Tenant's belongings or security deposit to satisfy a claim for rent accruing after termination.

### Military service (Washington law)
*RCW 59.18.220(2); RCW 59.18.200(1)(b)*

- **Who:** Tenant may end this Lease early if Tenant is a member of the armed forces, including the national guard and armed forces reserves, or the spouse or dependent of such a member, and the service member receives permanent change of station or deployment orders and one of the following applies: (a) permanent change of station orders require the service member to move 35 miles or more from the Property; (b) the service member is prematurely or involuntarily discharged or released from active duty; (c) the service member is released from active duty after leasing the Property while on active duty, and the Property is 35 miles or more from the service member's home of record before entering active duty; (d) after this Lease is signed, the commanding officer directs the service member to move into government-provided housing; (e) the service member receives temporary duty, temporary change of station, or active duty orders for 90 days or more to an area 35 miles or more from the Property; or (f) before taking possession, the service member receives change of station orders to an area 35 miles or more from the Property.
- **Notice:** Tenant (or the service member's spouse or dependent) must give Landlord written notice of 20 days or more before ending the tenancy. In a month-to-month tenancy, a Tenant who receives permanent change of station or deployment orders that do not allow 20 days' notice may end the tenancy on shorter written notice.
- **Documentation:** The notice must include a copy of the official military orders or a signed letter from the service member's commanding officer confirming that one of the criteria above is met.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes rent through the termination date. RCW 59.18.220 does not provide for any early termination fee, liquidated damages, or rent for the remainder of the term when Tenant terminates in compliance with it.

### Domestic violence, sexual assault, unlawful harassment, or stalking
*RCW 59.18.570; RCW 59.18.575*

- **Who:** Tenant may end this Lease early if Tenant or a household member was the victim of an act that constitutes a crime of domestic violence, sexual assault, unlawful harassment, or stalking, and either (a) Tenant or the household member has a valid protection order covering that conduct, or (b) Tenant or the household member reported the act to a qualified third party acting in an official capacity (a law enforcement officer, a court employee, a licensed mental health professional or other licensed counselor, a health care provider regulated under chapter 18.120 RCW, a trained advocate of a crime victim/witness program, or a member of the clergy) who provided a signed written record of the report.
- **Notice:** Tenant must notify Landlord in writing and request termination within 90 days after the act, event, or circumstance that gave rise to the protection order or the report, and may then quit the Property. If the act was sexual assault, stalking, or unlawful harassment committed by Landlord (including Landlord's employees), Tenant may quit first and deliver the documentation by mail, fax, or personal delivery by a third party within seven days after quitting.
- **Documentation:** Tenant must make available to Landlord a copy of the valid order for protection or the qualified third party's signed and dated written record of the report, substantially in the form set out in RCW 59.18.575(1)(b). The record given to Landlord does not include the name of the alleged perpetrator.
- **Owes:** Tenant is released from further obligation under this Lease. Tenant owes no rent for any period after the last day of the month in which Tenant quits, and remains liable for that month's rent unless the termination is made in accordance with RCW 59.18.200(1). Notwithstanding any Lease provision allowing forfeiture of a deposit for early termination, Tenant is entitled to the return of the full deposit, subject to lawful deductions under RCW 59.18.280. If the act was committed by Landlord, Tenant owes no rent after the later of the date Tenant vacates or the date the documentation and written notice of vacating are delivered, and prepaid rent is refunded pro rata. Other tenants on this Lease, other than household members who are victims, remain bound by it.

### Landlord's failure to repair after notice
*RCW 59.18.070; RCW 59.18.080; RCW 59.18.090(1)*

- **Who:** If Landlord fails to remedy a defective condition that Landlord must repair under RCW 59.18.060 or this Lease within a reasonable time after receiving Tenant's written notice and after the applicable period has passed (24 hours where the condition deprives Tenant of hot or cold water, heat, or electricity or is imminently hazardous to life; 72 hours where it deprives Tenant of a refrigerator, range and oven, or major plumbing fixture supplied by Landlord; 10 days in all other cases), Tenant may terminate this Lease. Tenant must be current in rent, including any utilities Tenant agreed to pay, to use this remedy, and it is not available for a condition caused by Tenant, Tenant's family, or invitees.
- **Notice:** Tenant terminates by written notice to Landlord and quitting the Property.
- **Documentation:** Tenant's earlier written notice of the defect must identify the Property, the owner's name if known, and the nature of the defective condition.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes no rent for any period after the quitting date, is entitled to a pro rata refund of any prepaid rent, and will receive the deposit statement and any refund due under RCW 59.18.280.

### Threat with a deadly weapon by another tenant
*RCW 59.18.352*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant, or another tenant sharing the dwelling unit, is threatened by another tenant with a firearm or other deadly weapon, the tenant who made the threat is arrested as a result, and Landlord does not file an unlawful detainer action against that tenant within seven calendar days after receiving notice of the arrest from a law enforcement agency.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives Landlord written notice and quits the Property.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes no rent for any period after the quitting date, is entitled to a pro rata refund of any prepaid rent, and will receive the deposit statement and any refund due under RCW 59.18.280.

### Threat with a deadly weapon by Landlord
*RCW 59.18.354*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Landlord threatens Tenant with a firearm or other deadly weapon and the threat leads to Landlord's arrest.
- **Notice:** Tenant may quit the Property without further obligation under this Lease.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes no rent for any period after the quitting date, is entitled to a pro rata refund of any prepaid rent, and will receive the deposit statement and any refund due under RCW 59.18.280.

### Unlawful removal or exclusion by Landlord
*RCW 59.18.290(1)*

- **Who:** If Landlord removes or excludes Tenant from the Property without a court order authorizing it, Tenant may recover possession or terminate this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant may terminate by notice to Landlord.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes no further rent after termination and may recover actual damages, costs, and reasonable attorneys' fees.

### Rent increase above the state limit
*RCW 59.18.700(3)*

- **Who:** If Landlord gives notice of a rent increase that exceeds the limit in RCW 59.18.700 and is not authorized by an exemption under RCW 59.18.710, Tenant may terminate this Lease at any time before the increase takes effect, in addition to demanding that Landlord reduce the increase.
- **Notice:** Tenant must give Landlord written notice at least 20 days before terminating.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes rent for the full month in which Tenant vacates. Landlord may not charge Tenant any fine or fee for terminating under this right.

### Condemned or unlawful dwelling
*RCW 59.18.085*

- **Who:** If a government agency has notified Landlord that the dwelling is condemned or unlawful to occupy because of code violations, and Tenant elects to terminate the tenancy because of those conditions (or the agency requires Tenant to vacate), Tenant may terminate this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant may terminate by notice to Landlord.
- **Owes:** Tenant is entitled to the return of the entire deposit and all prepaid rent. If Landlord knowingly rented a dwelling after being notified it was condemned or unlawful to occupy, Tenant may also recover the greater of three months' rent or up to three times actual damages, plus costs and attorneys' fees, and relocation assistance may be owed under RCW 59.18.085(3).

### Death of a tenant who is the sole occupant
*RCW 59.18.590; RCW 59.18.595*

- **Who:** If Tenant is the sole occupant of the dwelling unit and dies, Landlord will give written notice to any known personal representative, designated person, emergency contact, and known successor, and to Tenant at the Property.
- **Notice:** The tenancy terminates 15 days after the notice is mailed or personally delivered, or on the date through which rent is paid, whichever is later, unless a tenant representative arranges within that time to pay rent in advance for no more than 60 days from the date of death to allow orderly removal of Tenant's property.
- **Owes:** Within the time allowed by RCW 59.18.280 after the property is removed, Landlord will refund any unearned rent and provide the deposit statement and any refund to the tenant representative.

## Open questions for the reviewing attorney

- Rent stabilization IS enacted: 2025 c 209 (HB 1217), RCW 59.18.700-.720, effective May 7, 2025, expires July 1, 2040. No increase in the first 12 months of any tenancy; cap of 7% + Seattle-area CPI or 10%, whichever is less, per 12 months; exemptions for buildings with a first certificate of occupancy 12 or fewer years before the notice, owner-shared kitchen/bath, owner-occupied single-family with up to two rented units/bedrooms, and owner-occupied 2-4 plexes (owner-occupant exemptions unavailable to REITs, corporations, or LLCs with a corporate member). Also a 5% 'parity' rule: rent for month-to-month vs. fixed-term offers for the same unit may differ by no more than 5%, and month-to-month terms may not be more burdensome (RCW 59.18.700(4)). Keycove pricing UI should enforce this.
- Payment-method conflict for Keycove: RCW 59.18.230(2)(j) voids any lease term requiring electronic-only rent payment, and RCW 59.18.063 requires landlords to accept personal check, cashier's check, or money order and to allow payment by mail. A Stripe-only rent flow cannot be mandatory in Washington; the lease must allow an offline method. Knowingly using a prohibited provision exposes the landlord to statutory damages up to two times the monthly rent (RCW 59.18.230(3)).
- Statewide just cause (RCW 59.18.650): a fixed-term lease converts to month-to-month at expiration unless the narrow no-cause exceptions in 59.18.650(1)(b)-(c) apply (initial term of 6-12 months that rolls to month-to-month, or a 12+ month fixed term / successive 6+ month terms never periodic, each requiring 60 days' written notice before the term ends). The builder's renewal/expiration clause must not imply the landlord can simply decline to renew. A revised version of 59.18.650 takes effect January 1, 2028 and should be re-read before then.
- RCW 59.18.575(2)(a) is internally ambiguous: the tenant is 'discharged from the payment of rent for any period following the last day of the month of the quitting date' but 'shall remain liable for the rent for the month in which he or she terminated ... unless the termination is in accordance with RCW 59.18.200(1).' The lease text repeats the statute rather than interpreting it; attorney should decide whether to state a clearer rule.
- RCW 59.18.220(2) (military) is silent on rent proration and fees. The lease says Tenant owes rent through the termination date and no early termination fee is provided for; confirm. Note it covers spouses and dependents directly and uses a 35-mile test, broader than the SCRA in several respects (e.g., government-housing directives, pre-possession orders). RCW 38.42 (Washington SCRA analogue) contains no residential lease-termination right beyond incorporating the federal SCRA (RCW 38.42.120).
- No statutory late-fee cap; the only rule is no late fee for rent paid within five days of the due date (RCW 59.18.170(2), 59.18.230(2)(i)). After day five, the fee may run from the first day after the due date. Tenants whose primary income is monthly government assistance may require the due date be moved up to five days later (RCW 59.18.170(3)). No early-termination fee cap exists outside the specific statutes listed.
- Flood/hazard disclosure in RCW 59.18.060(13) (added by 2026 c 234) is mandatory only for leases entered into after December 31, 2026; included for all WA leases because it is harmless. The fire-safety notice under RCW 59.18.060(12) must be SIGNED by both parties, and for multifamily buildings needs building-specific answers (sprinklers, alarm, smoking policy, plans, evacuation diagram) that the builder does not currently collect.
- Do not put the RCW 59.18.590 death-of-tenant designation inside the lease: the statute requires it to be in writing and separate from the rental agreement.
- Other RCW 59.18.230(2) prohibited lease terms: tenant paying landlord's attorneys' fees except as the chapter authorizes and a court awards; nondisclosure agreements about rent or concessions; pre-selected arbitrators; arbitration unless the landlord pays all costs and the agreement is notarized; class-action waivers.
- Local law warnings (not encoded): Seattle (SMC 7.24 and 22.206 - just cause, longer rent-increase notice, economic displacement relocation assistance, cap on combined deposit plus nonrefundable fees, longer installment periods, Fair Chance Housing, winter and school-year eviction defenses, first-in-time), Tacoma Rental Housing Code (longer notices and relocation assistance), Spokane, Burien, Federal Way, Kenmore, Auburn, Bellingham, Olympia, Redmond, Kirkland, and unincorporated King County have their own rent-increase notice, fee-cap, relocation, or rent-limit ordinances. Verify current local rules for any WA property.
