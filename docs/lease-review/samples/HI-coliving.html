Hawaii Co-Living / Room Rental Agreement

Room rental in a shared residence. Hawaii landlord-tenant law, including its eviction procedure, applies in full.

1. Parties

Landlord: Sample Landlord LLC (100 Main Street, Suite 2)

Tenant: Jordan Sample (jordan@example.com)

2. Nature of This Agreement

Tenant rents Bedroom B (the "Room") at 123 Sample Ave, Apt 4 (the "Residence") and may use the shared common areas. Authorized occupants of the Room: Tenant only.

Landlord may move Tenant to a comparable room in the Residence only with Tenant's written consent or on at least 30 days' written notice with no increase in rent. The "co-living" and "room rental" terms of this Agreement govern house rules and room assignment only. Tenant has the rights of a residential tenant under Hawaii law. If Landlord seeks to remove Tenant from possession, Landlord will use the court eviction process described in this Agreement and will never use self-help removal.

3. Term

This Agreement begins on January 1, 2027 and ends on December 31, 2027. If Tenant stays after the end date with Landlord's consent (including Landlord's acceptance of rent), the tenancy continues month-to-month on the same terms. Landlord may end a month-to-month tenancy by written notice at least 45 days before the termination date; after receiving that notice, Tenant may vacate at any time within the last 45 days by telling Landlord the move-out date and paying prorated rent for the time occupied. Tenant may end a month-to-month tenancy by written notice at least 28 days before the termination date and is responsible for rent through the twenty-eighth day. If Landlord plans voluntary demolition, conversion to a condominium, or conversion to transient vacation rentals, Landlord must give at least 120 days' written notice. A tenancy shorter than month-to-month may be ended by either party on at least 10 days' notice. A termination notice given to evade the notice requirements for rent increases is void. (HRS § 521-71(a)-(d), (f))

Tenant shall pay rent of $1,500.00 per month, due on the 1st day of each month. Rent covers the Room, use of the common areas, and these shared utilities: Water and trash.

Payment methods. Tenant shall pay rent by any of these methods accepted by Landlord: Keycove online payment (ACH or card). Rent is paid on the date Landlord receives the funds.

Late fee. If rent is not paid on time, Landlord may charge a late fee of $75.00. may not exceed 8% of the amount of rent due (HRS § 521-21(f))

5. Security Deposit

Tenant shall pay a security deposit of $1,500.00. Landlord may require a security deposit of no more than one month's rent. If a pet animal is allowed to reside on the Property, Landlord may also require an additional pet deposit of no more than one month's rent to cover damage caused by the pet; no pet deposit may be required from a tenant without a resident pet or for an assistance animal that is a reasonable accommodation for a tenant with a disability. At the beginning of the tenancy Landlord may not require or receive any money other than the first month's rent and the security deposit (so no separate nonrefundable move-in, cleaning, or administrative fees may be collected). No part of the deposit may be treated as the last month's rent unless Landlord and Tenant agree in writing and Tenant gives 45 days' notice of vacating. (HRS § 521-44(b))

Landlord holds the security deposit for Tenant, and Tenant's claim to it is prior to the claim of any creditor of Landlord, including a trustee in bankruptcy, even if deposits are commingled. If Landlord transfers Landlord's interest in the unit, Landlord will give the successor an accounting of the deposit, and within 20 days the successor will notify Tenant in writing of the amount credited to Tenant. (HRS § 521-44(b), (f))

Landlord may use the security deposit only to remedy Tenant's defaults for damage resulting from Tenant's failure to meet Tenant's obligations under HRS § 521-51, unpaid rent, or failure to return keys, key fobs, parking cards, garage door openers, and mailbox keys; to clean the unit to the condition in which Tenant received it; to compensate for damages if Tenant wrongfully quits; to compensate for damage caused by an allowed pet; and to pay amounts Tenant owes for landlord-provided utilities not included in rent. If Landlord proposes to keep any part of the deposit, Landlord will notify Tenant in writing, within 14 days after this Lease ends, of the particulars of and grounds for the retention, with written evidence of the costs such as estimates, invoices, or receipts, and will return the remaining balance within the same 14 days. Mailing to an address Tenant supplies, with proof of mailing postmarked before midnight of the fourteenth day, is presumed to comply. If Landlord does not provide the notice and evidence within 14 days, Landlord may not keep any part of the deposit. If a court finds Landlord wrongfully and wilfully kept any part of the deposit, it may award Tenant up to three times the amount wrongfully kept. If Tenant is absent from the unit for 20 or more consecutive days without written notice to Landlord (and without rent having been paid for that period), Tenant is deemed to have wrongfully quit the unit. (HRS § 521-44(a), (c), (d), (h))

Before Tenant first occupies the unit, Landlord will inventory the premises and make a written record detailing the condition of the premises and any furnishings or appliances provided. Landlord and Tenant will each sign duplicate copies, and each tenant will receive a copy. The signed inventory is presumed correct; if Landlord does not make one, the condition of the premises at the end of the tenancy is presumed to be the same as when Tenant moved in.

A posted notice is received on the date of posting, and a mailed notice two business days after the postmark. The notice must include the information required by HRS § 521-68(b), and Landlord must also send a copy to a state-funded mediation center. If mediation is scheduled within the 10 days, Landlord may not file for possession until 20 calendar days after Tenant received the notice unless Tenant fails to appear at or cancels the mediation, and Landlord must participate in any mediation Tenant schedules. (HRS § 521-68 (as amended by L 2025, c 278, effective February 5, 2026))

Landlord may recover possession of the Property only through a summary possession proceeding in district court under HRS chapter 666 and HRS § 521-68 or § 521-71. Landlord will not remove or exclude Tenant without a court order, and will not try to recover possession by wilfully interrupting or reducing running water, hot water, electric, gas, or other essential service, except in case of abandonment or surrender.
