Delaware Co-Living / Room Rental Agreement

Room rental in a shared residence. Delaware landlord-tenant law, including its eviction procedure, applies in full.

1. Parties

Landlord: Sample Landlord LLC (100 Main Street, Suite 2)

Tenant: Jordan Sample (jordan@example.com)

2. Nature of This Agreement

Tenant rents Bedroom B (the "Room") at 123 Sample Ave, Apt 4 (the "Residence") and may use the shared common areas. Authorized occupants of the Room: Tenant only.

Landlord may move Tenant to a comparable room in the Residence only with Tenant's written consent or on at least 30 days' written notice with no increase in rent. The "co-living" and "room rental" terms of this Agreement govern house rules and room assignment only. Tenant has the rights of a residential tenant under Delaware law. If Landlord seeks to remove Tenant from possession, Landlord will use the court eviction process described in this Agreement and will never use self-help removal.

3. Term

This Agreement begins on January 1, 2027 and ends on December 31, 2027. If Tenant stays after the end date with Landlord's consent (including Landlord's acceptance of rent), the tenancy continues month-to-month on the same terms. Either Landlord or Tenant may end a month-to-month tenancy by giving the other at least 60 days' written notice, and the 60-day period begins on the first day of the month after the notice is actually received. To end a fixed-term lease at its expiration, Landlord must give at least 60 days' written notice before the end of the term, and Tenant may do so by giving at least 60 days' written notice before the end of the term. For a lease of one year or more, if Landlord does not give 60 days' notice and Tenant does not give at least 45 days' notice, the tenancy continues month-to-month on the same terms. (25 Del. C. §§ 5106(c)–(d), 5108(a))

4. Rent

Tenant shall pay rent of $1,500.00 per month, due on the 1st day of each month. Rent covers the Room, use of the common areas, and these shared utilities: Water and trash.

Payment methods. Tenant shall pay rent by any of these methods accepted by Landlord: Keycove online payment (ACH or card). Rent is paid on the date Landlord receives the funds.

Late fee. If rent is not paid on time, Landlord may charge a late fee of $75.00, but only after rent is at least 5 days late. A late charge may not exceed 5% of the monthly rent and may not be imposed within 5 days after the rent due date. If Landlord does not maintain an office or other permanent place in the county where the Property is located at which rent may be timely paid, the due date is extended by 3 days. (25 Del. 5. Security Deposit

Tenant shall pay a security deposit of $1,500.00. For a rental agreement of one year or more, the security deposit may not exceed one month's rent. For a month-to-month or undefined-term tenancy, the deposit may not exceed one month's rent once the tenancy has lasted one year, and Landlord must then immediately credit to Tenant any amount above one month's rent (counting any surety bond). These limits do not apply to furnished units. A separate pet deposit of no more than one month's rent may be charged, but no pet deposit may be charged for a certified and trained support animal for a disabled resident. (25 Del. C. § 5514(a), (i))

Landlord shall place the security deposit in an escrow bank account, designated as a security deposits account and not used in the operation of any business, at a federally insured banking institution with an office in Delaware that accepts deposits. Landlord shall disclose the location of the security deposit account to Tenant. (25 Del. C. § 5514(b))

Within 20 days after this Lease ends or is terminated, Landlord shall return the security deposit, or give Tenant an itemized list of damages to the Property with the estimated cost of repairing each item together with payment of the difference between the deposit and those costs. If Landlord does not provide the itemized list within 20 days, Landlord is treated as acknowledging that no payment for damages is due. Tenant's acceptance of a payment sent with an itemized list is agreement to the listed damages unless Tenant objects in writing within 10 days after receiving it. Landlord sends the deposit and notices to the address Tenant gives in this Lease or to a forwarding address Tenant provides in writing at or before the end of the tenancy. The deposit may be applied only to damage beyond normal wear and tear that cannot be corrected by painting and ordinary cleaning, unpaid rent and late charges, and the limited re-renting expenses allowed by law. (25 Del. C. § 5514(c), (e)–(h))

Landlord and Tenant will complete a written move-in condition report, with photographs, and a move-out inspection. Items not noted at move-in are presumed to have been in good condition.

(a) Tenant's early termination option. Tenant may end this Agreement before the end date by doing all of the following: (1) giving Landlord written notice of the date Tenant will move out (the "Early Move-Out Date") at least 30 days before that date; (2) paying all rent and other charges due through the Early Move-Out Date; (3) paying an early termination fee of $3,000.00 on or before the Early Move-Out Date; and (4) moving out and returning all keys and access devices by the Early Move-Out Date. If the total rent that would have come due under this Agreement after the Early Move-Out Date is less than the early termination fee, Tenant pays that smaller amount instead.

(b) Liquidated damages, not a penalty. When Tenant completes every step in paragraph (a), Tenant owes no rent for any period after the Early Move-Out Date, and the fee is Landlord's only remedy for the early termination itself. The fee does not cover unpaid rent through the Early Move-Out Date, damage beyond normal wear and tear, or other charges allowed by this Agreement and Delaware law.

(c) If Tenant leaves early without using this option. Using paragraph (a) is Tenant's choice. If Tenant moves out before the end date without completing paragraph (a), Landlord will not charge the early termination fee. Instead, Tenant remains responsible for rent as it comes due until the earlier of the end date or the date a replacement tenant's rent begins, plus Landlord's reasonable costs of re-renting. If Tenant wrongfully moves out early, Landlord has a duty to mitigate damages, and Tenant is liable for no more than the lesser of (1) the rent for the rest of the term plus actual damage caused by Tenant beyond normal wear and tear, or (2) rent for the period reasonably needed to re-rent at a fair rental, plus any difference between the fair rental and the rent in this Lease, plus re-renting expenses, repair of damage beyond normal wear and tear, and a reasonable commission if Landlord incurs one.
