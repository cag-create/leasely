Room rental in a shared residence. New Hampshire landlord-tenant law, including its eviction procedure, applies in full.

Landlord: Sample Landlord LLC (100 Main Street, Suite 2)

Tenant: Jordan Sample (jordan@example.com)

2. Nature of This Agreement

Tenant rents Bedroom B (the "Room") at 123 Sample Ave, Apt 4 (the "Residence") and may use the shared common areas. Authorized occupants of the Room: Tenant only.

Landlord may move Tenant to a comparable room in the Residence only with Tenant's written consent or on at least 30 days' written notice with no increase in rent. The "co-living" and "room rental" terms of this Agreement govern house rules and room assignment only. Tenant has the rights of a residential tenant under New Hampshire law. If Landlord seeks to remove Tenant from possession, Landlord will use the court eviction process described in this Agreement and will never use self-help removal.

3. Term

This Agreement begins on January 1, 2027 and ends on December 31, 2027. If Tenant stays after the end date with Landlord's consent (including Landlord's acceptance of rent), the tenancy continues month-to-month on the same terms. Tenant may end a month-to-month tenancy by written notice given at least 30 days in advance; if the termination date in the notice does not fall on a rent due date, Tenant owes rent for the entire month in which the notice expires, up to the next rent due date. Landlord may end a tenancy only by a written eviction notice that states the specific reason and is served personally or left at Tenant's last and usual place of abode, given at least 30 days in advance (7 days when the reason is nonpayment of rent, substantial damage to the Premises, behavior that adversely affects the health or safety of others, or an accused perpetrator remaining after a domestic violence termination). For most residential rental property, Landlord may terminate only for a reason listed in RSA 540:2, II, which includes nonpayment of rent, substantial damage, failure to comply with a material term of this Lease, behavior adversely affecting health or safety, and other good cause (including any legitimate business or economic reason); a tenant's refusal to agree to a rent increase is good cause if Landlord gave at least 30 days' written notice of the increase. (N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. §§ 540:1-a, 540:2, 540:3, 540:5, 540:11)

12. Early Termination by Tenant (Lease Break)

(a) Tenant's early termination option. Tenant may end this Agreement before the end date by doing all of the following: (1) giving Landlord written notice of the date Tenant will move out (the "Early Move-Out Date") at least 30 days before that date; (2) paying all rent and other charges due through the Early Move-Out Date; (3) paying an early termination fee of $3,000.00 on or before the Early Move-Out Date; and (4) moving out and returning all keys and access devices by the Early Move-Out Date. If the total rent that would have come due under this Agreement after the Early Move-Out Date is less than the early termination fee, Tenant pays that smaller amount instead.

(b) Liquidated damages, not a penalty. Landlord and Tenant agree that if Tenant leaves early, Landlord's damages (rent lost while the Room is vacant and the cost of advertising, showing, screening, and turnover) are difficult to determine at the time of signing, and that the early termination fee is a reasonable estimate of those damages and not a penalty. When Tenant completes every step in paragraph (a), Tenant owes no rent for any period after the Early Move-Out Date, and the fee is Landlord's only remedy for the early termination itself. The fee does not cover unpaid rent through the Early Move-Out Date, damage beyond normal wear and tear, or other charges allowed by this Agreement and New Hampshire law.

(c) If Tenant leaves early without using this option. Using paragraph (a) is Tenant's choice. If Tenant moves out before the end date without completing paragraph (a), Landlord will not charge the early termination fee. Instead, Tenant remains responsible for rent as it comes due until the earlier of the end date or the date a replacement tenant's rent begins, plus Landlord's reasonable costs of re-renting. If Tenant leaves before the end of the Lease term, Landlord will make reasonable efforts to re-rent the Premises and will credit Tenant with rent actually received from a replacement tenant for the remainder of the term, less Landlord's reasonable costs of re-renting.

Paragraphs (a) through (c) do not apply, and Landlord will not charge the early termination fee, when Tenant ends this Agreement under any of the following laws. This Agreement cannot waive or reduce these rights, and if any part of this Agreement conflicts with them, the law controls. Each right has its own notice and documentation requirements:

Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant enters military service after signing it; or if Tenant, while in military service, signed this Lease and then receives orders for a permanent change of station (which includes separation or retirement) or to deploy with a military unit or in support of a military operation for 90 days or more; or if Tenant signed this Lease upon such orders and then receives a stop movement order of 30 days or more (or indefinite) that prevents occupancy. A spouse or dependent may terminate within one year after Tenant dies in military service or while performing covered National Guard or Reserve duty, and a Tenant who suffers a catastrophic injury or illness in such service (or the spouse or dependent, if Tenant lacks capacity) may terminate within one year after it occurs. Termination also ends the obligations of Tenant's dependents under this Lease. Tenant delivers written notice to Landlord by hand, private business carrier, mail with return receipt requested, or electronic means. Because rent is paid monthly, termination is effective 30 days after the first date on which the next rent payment is due after the notice is delivered; termination after a stop movement order is effective when the notice and orders are delivered. The notice must include a copy of the military orders (official orders, including orders for separation or retirement, or a notification, certification, or verification from the commanding officer). Rent is owed only on a prorated basis through the effective date of termination. Landlord may not impose an early termination charge, but Tenant still owes unpaid amounts due under this Lease, including reasonable charges for excess wear.   • Military service (state law) (N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:11-a). Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant is a member of the armed services reserve who is called to active duty, a member of the National Guard who is called to active duty, or a member of the armed services on active duty who is reassigned to a location outside New Hampshire. Tenant must give notice of termination within 7 days after receiving notice of being called to active duty or reassigned out of state, by written notice that complies with Section 305 of the federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (50 U.S.C. § 3955). Under that section, because rent is payable monthly, termination takes effect 30 days after the first date on which the next rent payment is due after the notice is delivered. The 7-day deadline applies to this state-law right only and does not limit any right Tenant has directly under the federal Act. As the federal Act requires, the notice must be accompanied by a copy of Tenant's military orders or a written verification from Tenant's commanding officer. Tenant owes rent only through the effective date of termination, prorated, and no early termination fee or penalty; Landlord will refund rent paid in advance for any period after that date within 30 days, as the federal Act provides.
  • Domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking (N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:11-b). Tenant may terminate this Lease if, within the most recent 150 days, Tenant or a household member residing at the Premises has been a victim of domestic violence (as defined in RSA 173-B), sexual assault (RSA 632-A), or stalking (RSA 633:3-a), or if within the most recent 150 days an event has occurred involving a victim of past domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking that, together with the past abuse, causes the victim to fear for the victim's safety. Tenant gives Landlord notice of termination and must vacate the Premises within 30 days after giving notice, or at another time Landlord and Tenant agree on. Tenant provides written verification that Tenant or the household member who is the victim: has obtained a valid protective order against the perpetrator; has started legal action to obtain a protective order; has reported the domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking to a law enforcement agency; has a written verification from a law enforcement official, victim's advocate, attorney, or health care provider of victim status; or has signed the self-certification form provided by the New Hampshire circuit court. Landlord will keep the verification, and the fact that anyone is a victim, in strict confidence and will disclose it only with the victim's written consent, when relevant and reasonably necessary in an eviction or housing-assistance proceeding, or as otherwise required by law. Tenant is liable only for rent owed through the termination date or the date Tenant actually vacates, whichever is later, and owes no early termination fee. Any security deposit remains applied to the rental of the Premises until all tenants have vacated and is then returned under RSA 540-A:7. Landlord may evict a remaining co-tenant or occupant who is the accused perpetrator.
