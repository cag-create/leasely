Texas Residential Lease Agreement

Governed by Texas Property Code, Title 8, Chapter 91 (Provisions Generally Applicable to Landlords and Tenants) and Chapter 92 (Residential Tenancies); eviction under Chapter 24

1. Parties

Landlord: Sample Landlord LLC (100 Main Street, Suite 2)

Tenant: Jordan Sample (jordan@example.com)

2. Property

Landlord leases to Tenant the residential premises at 123 Sample Ave, Apt 4 (the "Property"). Authorized occupants: Tenant only.

3. Term

This Lease begins on January 1, 2027 and ends on December 31, 2027. If Tenant stays after the end date with Landlord's consent (including Landlord's acceptance of rent), the tenancy continues month-to-month on the same terms. Either Landlord or Tenant may end a month-to-month tenancy by giving the other written notice of termination. The tenancy ends on the later of the date stated in the notice or one month after the day the notice is given. If the tenancy ends on a day that is not the beginning or end of a rental period, Tenant owes rent only through the termination date. (Tex. Prop. Code § 91.001)

4. Rent

Tenant shall pay rent of $1,500.00 per month, due on the 1st day of each month.

Payment methods. Tenant shall pay rent by any of these methods accepted by Landlord: Keycove online payment (ACH or card). Rent is paid on the date Landlord receives the funds.

Late fee. If rent is not paid on time, Landlord may charge a late fee of $75.00, but only after rent is at least 2 days late. A late fee may be charged only if any part of the rent remains unpaid two full days after the date it was originally due. The total late fee, including any initial fee and any daily fees combined, may not exceed 12 percent of the rent for the rental period if the Property is in a structure with no more than four dwelling units, or 10 percent of the rent for the rental period if the Property is in a structure with more than four dwelling units. (Tex. Prop. Code § 92.019)

5. Security Deposit

Tenant shall pay a security deposit of $1,500.00. Texas law sets no maximum amount for a residential security deposit.

Landlord will refund the security deposit, less lawful deductions, on or before the 30th day after Tenant surrenders the Property, together with a written description and itemized list of all deductions. Landlord will not deduct for normal wear and tear. Landlord is not obligated to return the deposit or provide the itemized list until Tenant gives Landlord a written statement of Tenant's forwarding address, but Tenant does not forfeit the deposit by failing to do so. No itemized list is required if Tenant owes rent when Tenant surrenders the Property and there is no controversy about the amount of rent owed. Tenant may not withhold any part of the last month's rent on the ground that the security deposit is security for unpaid rent. (Tex. Prop. Code §§ 92.103, 92.104, 92.107, 92.108)

Landlord and Tenant will complete a written move-in condition report, with photographs, and a move-out inspection. Items not noted at move-in are presumed to have been in good condition.

6. Utilities

Utilities included in the rent: Water and trash. Tenant is responsible for arranging and paying for all other utilities.

7. Pets and Parking

Pets allowed: No. Assistance animals for a person with a disability are not pets, and no pet fee or pet deposit applies to them. Parking: One assigned space.

8. Landlord Entry

Texas statutes do not set a notice period for Landlord's entry. Landlord may enter the Property at reasonable times, after reasonable advance notice to Tenant except in an emergency, to inspect, make repairs, or show the Property to prospective tenants, buyers, or lenders.

9. Maintenance and Repairs

Landlord will make a diligent effort to repair or remedy a condition that materially affects the physical health or safety of an ordinary tenant, or that arises from Landlord's failure to provide and maintain in good operating condition a device to supply hot water of a minimum temperature of 120 degrees Fahrenheit, after Tenant gives notice of the condition to the person or place where rent is normally paid and while Tenant is not delinquent in rent. Unless the condition was caused by normal wear and tear, Landlord is not required to repair a condition caused by Tenant, a lawful occupant, a member of Tenant's family, or Tenant's guest or invitee. (Tex. Prop. Code §§ 92.052, 92.056)

Tenant shall keep the Property clean and safe, use appliances and fixtures only for their intended purposes, not damage or allow damage to the Property beyond normal wear and tear, comply with building and housing codes that apply to tenants, and promptly notify Landlord in writing of any needed repair, water leak, or visible mold. Tenant shall not make alterations, paint, or change or add locks without Landlord's written consent, except as the law allows.

10. Use, Assignment, and Subletting

The Property may be used only as a private residence by Tenant and the authorized occupants. Tenant may not assign this Lease or sublet any part of the Property without Landlord's prior written consent. No illegal activity is permitted on the premises.

11. Default and Eviction

Before filing an eviction suit for nonpayment of rent, Landlord will give Tenant at least three days' written notice to vacate. If Tenant was not late or delinquent in paying rent before the month in which the notice is given, the notice will be a notice to pay rent or vacate. The notice may be delivered by mail, by delivery to the inside of the Property in a conspicuous place, by hand delivery to any tenant of the Property who is 16 years of age or older, or by e-mail or other electronic means if Landlord and Tenant have agreed in writing to electronic delivery. (Tex. Prop. Code § 24.005(a), (f-3))

Landlord may recover possession of the Property only through an eviction suit in the justice court of the precinct where the Property is located under Chapter 24 of the Texas Property Code, after giving any required notice to vacate. Landlord will not remove doors, windows, locks, or Landlord-furnished furniture, fixtures, or appliances, intentionally prevent Tenant from entering the Property, or interrupt utilities except as Sections 92.008 and 92.0081 of the Texas Property Code allow, and this Lease does not give Landlord the right to change the door locks because Tenant is delinquent in rent. (Tex. Prop. Code §§ 24.002, 24.004, 24.005, 24.0061, 92.008, 92.0081)

Tenant may end this Lease before the end date by doing all of the following: (1) giving Landlord written notice of the date Tenant will move out (the "Early Move-Out Date") at least 30 days before that date; (2) paying all rent and other charges due through the Early Move-Out Date; (3) paying an early termination fee of $3,000.00 on or before the Early Move-Out Date; and (4) moving out and returning all keys and access devices by the Early Move-Out Date. If the total rent that would have come due under this Lease after the Early Move-Out Date is less than the early termination fee, Tenant pays that smaller amount instead.

(b) Liquidated damages, not a penalty. Landlord and Tenant agree that if Tenant leaves early, Landlord's damages (rent lost while the Property is vacant and the cost of advertising, showing, screening, and turnover) are difficult to determine at the time of signing, and that the early termination fee is a reasonable estimate of those damages and not a penalty. When Tenant completes every step in paragraph (a), Tenant owes no rent for any period after the Early Move-Out Date, and the fee is Landlord's only remedy for the early termination itself. The fee does not cover unpaid rent through the Early Move-Out Date, damage beyond normal wear and tear, or other charges allowed by this Lease and Texas law.

(c) If Tenant leaves early without using this option. Using paragraph (a) is Tenant's choice. If Tenant moves out before the end date without completing paragraph (a), Landlord will not charge the early termination fee. Instead, Tenant remains responsible for rent as it comes due until the earlier of the end date or the date a replacement tenant's rent begins, plus Landlord's reasonable costs of re-renting. If Tenant abandons the Property in violation of this Lease, Landlord has a duty to mitigate its damages, including by making reasonable efforts to re-let the Property, and will credit against Tenant's liability any rent received from a replacement tenant. Any provision of this Lease that purports to waive this duty is void. (Tex. Prop. Code § 91.006)
