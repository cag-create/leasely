California Residential Lease Agreement

Governed by California Civil Code, Division 3, Part 4, Title 5, Chapter 2 (Hiring of Real Property, Cal. Civ. Code §§ 1940 et seq.), with unlawful detainer under Cal. Code Civ. Proc. §§ 1159 et seq.

1. Parties

Landlord: Sample Landlord LLC (100 Main Street, Suite 2)

Tenant: Jordan Sample (jordan@example.com)

2. Property

Landlord leases to Tenant the residential premises at 123 Sample Ave, Apt 4 (the "Property"). Authorized occupants: Tenant only.

3. Term

This Lease begins on January 1, 2027 and ends on December 31, 2027. If Tenant stays after the end date with Landlord's consent (including Landlord's acceptance of rent), the tenancy continues month-to-month on the same terms. Tenant may end a month-to-month tenancy by giving Landlord written notice at least 30 days before the termination date. Landlord must give at least 60 days' written notice if any tenant or resident has resided in the dwelling for one year or more, and at least 30 days' written notice if all have resided there for less than one year (or in the limited sale-to-owner-occupant case in Civil Code section 1946.1(d)). After all tenants have continuously and lawfully occupied the Property for 12 months, or any tenant for 24 months, Landlord may terminate only for a just cause stated in the notice under Civil Code section 1946.2 (or a stricter local ordinance), unless the Property is exempt; a no-fault termination requires one month's rent as relocation assistance or a waiver of the final month's rent. Notices must be served as Code of Civil Procedure section 1162 provides or by certified or registered mail, and Landlord may not charge a fee for serving any notice. Rent is due through the termination date. (Cal. Civ. Code §§ 1946, 1946.1, 1946.2)

4. Rent

Tenant shall pay rent of $1,500.00 per month, due on the 1st day of each month.

Payment methods. Tenant shall pay rent by any of these methods accepted by Landlord: Keycove online payment (ACH or card). Rent is paid on the date Landlord receives the funds.

Late fee. If rent is not paid on time, Landlord may charge a late fee of $75.00. Any late fee must be a reasonable estimate of the damages Landlord actually suffers from late payment, because a liquidated-damages provision in a residential lease is void unless it would be impracticable or extremely difficult to fix Landlord's actual damages. (Cal. Civ. Code § 1671(c)(2), (d))

5. Security Deposit

Tenant shall pay a security deposit of $1,500.00. For security collected on or after July 1, 2024, Landlord may not demand or receive security, however denominated, of more than one month's rent in addition to the first month's rent. A landlord who is a natural person (including a family-trust settlor or beneficiary) or an LLC whose members are all natural persons, and who owns no more than two residential rental properties with no more than four dwelling units offered for rent in total, may take up to two months' rent, except that this small-landlord exception does not apply if the prospective tenant is a service member (Military and Veterans Code § 400), and Landlord may not refuse to rent to a service member because of it. The cap does not prohibit an advance payment of at least six months' rent on a lease of six months or longer. 'Security' includes any fee or charge imposed at the start of the tenancy to reimburse the landlord for processing a new tenant (other than an application screening fee under § 1950.6), and no security may be labeled nonrefundable. If Landlord charges a service member a higher-than-standard security because of credit or housing history, Landlord must explain why in writing at signing and return the extra amount after no more than six months if Tenant is not in arrears, with the return date stated in this Lease. (Cal. Civ. Code § 1950.5(b), (c), (n))

Landlord holds the security for Tenant, and Tenant's claim to it is prior to the claim of any creditor of Landlord. If Landlord's interest in the Property ends, Landlord will within a reasonable time either transfer the remaining security to Landlord's successor and notify Tenant in writing of the transfer, any claims against the security, the amount transferred, and the successor's name, address, and telephone number, or return the remaining security to Tenant with an itemized accounting. (Cal. Civ. Code § 1950.5(d), (i))

No later than 21 calendar days after Tenant vacates, Landlord will furnish Tenant an itemized statement of the basis for, and amount of, any security received and its disposition, and will return any remaining balance. Landlord may deduct only amounts reasonably necessary for unpaid rent, repair of damage beyond ordinary wear and tear caused by Tenant or Tenant's guests or licensees, cleaning needed to return the unit to its move-in level of cleanliness, and, if this Lease authorizes it, restoring or replacing personal property or appurtenances, and may not deduct for conditions that preexisted the tenancy or for ordinary wear and tear. With the statement Landlord will include copies of bills, invoices, or receipts (or, for work by Landlord or Landlord's employees, a description of the work, the time spent, and the reasonable hourly rate) and the photographs required by law, unless repair and cleaning deductions together are $125 or less; Tenant may request the documentation within 14 days after receiving the statement. If repairs cannot be completed or documents obtained within 21 days, Landlord may deduct a good-faith estimate and must provide the final documentation within 14 days after completion or receipt. If Tenant paid the security or rent electronically, Landlord will return the balance electronically to an account Tenant designates in writing (or by another method agreed in writing). If multiple adult tenants reside in the unit and there is no other written agreement, the balance will be returned by a check payable to all adult tenants. A bad-faith retention may subject Landlord to statutory damages of up to twice the security in addition to actual damages. (Cal. Civ. Code § 1950.5(e), (g), (h), (m))

For tenancies beginning on or after July 1, 2025, Landlord will photograph the unit immediately before, or at the start of, the tenancy. Landlord will also photograph the unit within a reasonable time after possession is returned (before any repairs or cleaning to be deducted from the security) and again after those repairs or cleaning are completed. Within a reasonable time after either party gives notice of termination, or before the end of the Lease term, Landlord will notify Tenant in writing of Tenant's option to request an initial inspection, no earlier than two weeks before the tenancy ends, and of Tenant's right to be present. If Tenant requests one, Landlord will give at least 48 hours' written notice of the inspection and leave an itemized statement of proposed deductions, and Tenant may cure the listed items before moving out.

Utilities included in the rent: Water and trash. Tenant is responsible for arranging and paying for all other utilities.

7. Pets and Parking

Pets allowed: No. Assistance animals for a person with a disability are not pets, and no pet fee or pet deposit applies to them. Parking: One assigned space.

8. Landlord Entry

Landlord may enter only in an emergency; to make necessary or agreed repairs, decorations, alterations, or improvements, supply necessary or agreed services, or show the unit to prospective or actual purchasers, mortgagees, tenants, workers, or contractors; to conduct a pre-move-out inspection under Civil Code section 1950.5(f); when Tenant has abandoned or surrendered the unit; under a court order; or for other purposes the Civil Code or Health and Safety Code allow. Except in an emergency, with Tenant's consent at the time of entry, or after abandonment or surrender, Landlord will give reasonable written notice stating the date, approximate time, and purpose of entry, and will enter only during normal business hours. Twenty-four hours' notice is presumed reasonable (six days if the notice is mailed). Landlord will not abuse the right of access or use it to harass Tenant. (Cal. Civ. Code § 1954)

9. Maintenance and Repairs

Landlord will put the Property into a condition fit for occupation and repair all later dilapidations that make it untenantable (except those caused by Tenant), including providing effective waterproofing and weather protection; plumbing, gas, heating, and electrical facilities that conformed to law when installed and are maintained in good working order; hot and cold running water connected to an approved sewage system; clean and sanitary building and grounds free of debris, rodents, and vermin at the start of the tenancy and in areas under Landlord's control; adequate garbage receptacles; floors, stairways, and railings in good repair; and, for leases entered into, amended, or extended on or after January 1, 2026 (except in certain shared-kitchen, single-room-occupancy, and supportive housing), a stove and a refrigerator maintained in good working order, with any recalled stove or refrigerator repaired or replaced within 30 days after Landlord receives notice of the recall. (Cal. Civ. Code §§ 1941, 1941.1)

Tenant shall keep the Property clean and safe, use appliances and fixtures only for their intended purposes, not damage or allow damage to the Property beyond normal wear and tear, comply with building and housing codes that apply to tenants, and promptly notify Landlord in writing of any needed repair, water leak, or visible mold. Tenant shall not make alterations, paint, or change or add locks without Landlord's written consent, except as the law allows.

10. Use, Assignment, and Subletting

The Property may be used only as a private residence by Tenant and the authorized occupants. Tenant may not assign this Lease or sublet any part of the Property without Landlord's prior written consent. No illegal activity is permitted on the premises.

11. Default and Eviction

If rent is not paid when due, Landlord may serve a written notice requiring payment of the rent or surrender of possession within three days, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and other judicial holidays. The notice must state the amount due and the name, telephone number, and address of the person to whom rent is to be paid (and, if payment may be made in person, the usual days and hours that person is available), or the name and street address of a financial institution within five miles where rent may be deposited, or that payment may be made by a previously established electronic funds transfer procedure. Landlord may not charge Tenant a fee for serving the notice. (Cal. Code Civ. Proc. §§ 1161(2), 1161(6), 1162)

Landlord may recover possession of the Property only through an unlawful detainer action under Code of Civil Procedure sections 1159 et seq., after the notice required by law and, where Civil Code section 1946.2 or a local ordinance applies, only for just cause. Landlord will not, with intent to terminate Tenant's occupancy, willfully interrupt any utility service, change the locks or otherwise block Tenant's access, remove outside doors or windows, or remove Tenant's property or furnishings without Tenant's prior written consent. (Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1161; Cal. Civ. Code §§ 789.3, 1946.2)

12. Early Termination by Tenant (Lease Break)

(a) Tenant's early termination option. Tenant may end this Lease before the end date by doing all of the following: (1) giving Landlord written notice of the date Tenant will move out (the "Early Move-Out Date") at least 30 days before that date; (2) paying all rent and other charges due through the Early Move-Out Date; (3) paying an early termination fee of $3,000.00 on or before the Early Move-Out Date; and (4) moving out and returning all keys and access devices by the Early Move-Out Date. If the total rent that would have come due under this Lease after the Early Move-Out Date is less than the early termination fee, Tenant pays that smaller amount instead.

(b) Liquidated damages, not a penalty. Landlord and Tenant agree that if Tenant leaves early, Landlord's damages (rent lost while the Property is vacant and the cost of advertising, showing, screening, and turnover) are difficult to determine at the time of signing, and that the early termination fee is a reasonable estimate of those damages and not a penalty. When Tenant completes every step in paragraph (a), Tenant owes no rent for any period after the Early Move-Out Date, and the fee is Landlord's only remedy for the early termination itself. The fee does not cover unpaid rent through the Early Move-Out Date, damage beyond normal wear and tear, or other charges allowed by this Lease and California law.

(c) If Tenant leaves early without using this option. Using paragraph (a) is Tenant's choice. If Tenant moves out before the end date without completing paragraph (a), Landlord will not charge the early termination fee. Instead, Tenant remains responsible for rent as it comes due until the earlier of the end date or the date a replacement tenant's rent begins, plus Landlord's reasonable costs of re-renting. If Tenant breaches this Lease and abandons the Property, or Landlord terminates Tenant's right to possession because of Tenant's breach, this Lease ends and Landlord may recover unpaid rent earned to the termination date, plus rent that would have been earned afterward only to the extent it exceeds the rental loss Tenant proves could have been reasonably avoided, plus other damages proximately caused by Tenant's breach. Landlord will make reasonable, good-faith efforts to re-rent the Property and will credit rent received from a replacement tenant. (Cal. Civ. Code § 1951.2)

(d) Rights protected by law: no early termination fee. Paragraphs (a) through (c) do not apply, and Landlord will not charge the early termination fee, when Tenant ends this Lease under any of the following laws. This Lease cannot waive or reduce these rights, and if any part of this Lease conflicts with them, the law controls. Each right has its own notice and documentation requirements:

  • Military service (federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act) (50 U.S.C. § 3955). Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant enters military service after signing it; or if Tenant, while in military service, signed this Lease and then receives orders for a permanent change of station (which includes separation or retirement) or to deploy with a military unit or in support of a military operation for 90 days or more; or if Tenant signed this Lease upon such orders and then receives a stop movement order of 30 days or more (or indefinite) that prevents occupancy. A spouse or dependent may terminate within one year after Tenant dies in military service or while performing covered National Guard or Reserve duty, and a Tenant who suffers a catastrophic injury or illness in such service (or the spouse or dependent, if Tenant lacks capacity) may terminate within one year after it occurs. Termination also ends the obligations of Tenant's dependents under this Lease. Tenant delivers written notice to Landlord by hand, private business carrier, mail with return receipt requested, or electronic means. Because rent is paid monthly, termination is effective 30 days after the first date on which the next rent payment is due after the notice is delivered; termination after a stop movement order is effective when the notice and orders are delivered. The notice must include a copy of the military orders (official orders, including orders for separation or retirement, or a notification, certification, or verification from the commanding officer). Rent is owed only on a prorated basis through the effective date of termination. Landlord may not impose an early termination charge, but Tenant still owes unpaid amounts due under this Lease, including reasonable charges for excess wear. Rent paid in advance for any period after termination will be refunded within 30 days after the effective date. Landlord may not hold Tenant's belongings or security deposit to satisfy a claim for rent accruing after termination.
  • Military service (California Military and Veterans Code) (Cal. Mil. & Vet. Code §§ 400, 409). Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant is a service member, meaning a member of the California militia (the National Guard, State Guard, or Naval Militia) called or ordered into active state or federal service, or a member of an active or reserve component of the Armed Forces ordered into active duty under federal law, and either (a) Tenant enters a period of military service during the term of this Lease, or (b) while in military service, Tenant signs this Lease and then receives military orders for a permanent change of station or to deploy with a military unit, or as an individual in support of a military operation, for not less than 90 days. For a militia member, military service means full-time active state or federal service; for others, full-time active duty for more than seven days in any 14-day period. Tenant delivers written notice of termination to Landlord or Landlord's agent by hand delivery, private business carrier, or mail with return receipt requested.   • Destruction of the Property (Cal. Civ. Code §§ 1932(2), 1933(4), 1941.9). Tenant may terminate after making the request and allowing a reasonable time for Landlord to perform. Tenant owes rent only through the termination date.

(e) California limit. A fixed early termination fee or other liquidated-damages provision in a residential lease is void unless, from the nature of the case, it would be impracticable or extremely difficult to fix Landlord's actual damages. If Tenant breaches and abandons the Property, Landlord's recovery is limited to the damages allowed by Civil Code section 1951.2, reduced by any rental loss Tenant proves could reasonably have been avoided. (Cal. Civ. Code §§ 1671(c)(2), (d); 1951.2) The early termination fee in paragraph (a) will never exceed that limit.

(f) Security deposit. After Tenant moves out, Landlord may apply the security deposit to an unpaid early termination fee or to rent owed under paragraph (c) to the extent the law allows, and will account for the deposit as described in the Security Deposit section and as the law requires.
