District of Columbia Co-Living / Room Rental Agreement

Room rental in a shared residence. District of Columbia landlord-tenant law, including its eviction procedure, applies in full.

1. Parties

Landlord: Sample Landlord LLC (100 Main Street, Suite 2)

Tenant: Jordan Sample (jordan@example.com)

2. Nature of This Agreement

Tenant rents Bedroom B (the "Room") at 123 Sample Ave, Apt 4 (the "Residence") and may use the shared common areas. Authorized occupants of the Room: Tenant only.

Landlord may move Tenant to a comparable room in the Residence only with Tenant's written consent or on at least 30 days' written notice with no increase in rent. The "co-living" and "room rental" terms of this Agreement govern house rules and room assignment only. Tenant has the rights of a residential tenant under District of Columbia law. If Landlord seeks to remove Tenant from possession, Landlord will use the court eviction process described in this Agreement and will never use self-help removal.

3. Term

This Agreement begins on January 1, 2027 and ends on December 31, 2027. If Tenant stays after the end date with Landlord's consent (including Landlord's acceptance of rent), the tenancy continues month-to-month on the same terms. Tenant may end a month-to-month tenancy by giving Landlord at least 30 days' written notice, which takes effect on the first day of the first month at least 30 days after the date of the notice. Landlord may not require more than 30 days' notice from Tenant unless Landlord also must give at least 30 days more notice than that of any rent increase. Landlord may not end Tenant's tenancy, even when the lease term expires, as long as Tenant keeps paying the rent, except for a reason allowed by the District's eviction law and after serving the written notice that law requires. (D.C. Code §§ 42-3202(b), 42-3505.54, 42-3505.01(a))

Payment methods. Tenant shall pay rent by any of these methods accepted by Landlord: Keycove online payment (ACH or card). Rent is paid on the date Landlord receives the funds.

Late fee. If rent is not paid on time, Landlord may charge a late fee of $75.00, but only after rent is at least 5 days late. The maximum late fee is 5% of the full amount of monthly rent due, and it may be charged only if the full rent has not been paid within 5 days after the due date. Landlord will not charge interest on a late fee, deduct a late fee from a later rent payment, charge more than one late fee for the same late payment, evict Tenant for not paying a late fee, or charge a late fee on any portion of rent a rent subsidy provider is responsible for paying. Landlord may invoice an unpaid late fee, payable within 30 days, and may deduct an unpaid lawful late fee from the security deposit at the end of the tenancy. (D.C. Code § 42-3505.31)

The terms and conditions under which the deposit is paid are stated in this Lease. At the end of the tenancy Landlord will list for Tenant the interest rate for each six-month period of the tenancy. Landlord will not withhold any amount for ordinary wear and tear, including professional cleaning made necessary only by ordinary wear and tear. (14 DCMR §§ 309.1–309.3, 301.4; D.C. Landlord may recover possession only for a reason allowed by D.C. Code § 42-3505.01, after serving the written notice that section requires, through an action for possession in the Superior Court of the District of Columbia, and only by a court-ordered eviction carried out under the court's writ. Landlord will not lock Tenant out, shut off utilities, or remove Tenant's belongings without a court order. Landlord must hold a valid rental housing registration or claim of exemption and a current rental housing business license to file for possession. (D.C. Code §§ 42-3505.01, 16-1501)

12. Early Termination by Tenant (Lease Break)

(a) Tenant's early termination option. Tenant may end this Agreement before the end date by doing all of the following: (1) giving Landlord written notice of the date Tenant will move out (the "Early Move-Out Date") at least 30 days before that date; (2) paying all rent and other charges due through the Early Move-Out Date; (3) paying an early termination fee of $3,000.00 on or before the Early Move-Out Date; and (4) moving out and returning all keys and access devices by the Early Move-Out Date. If the total rent that would have come due under this Agreement after the Early Move-Out Date is less than the early termination fee, Tenant pays that smaller amount instead.

(b) Liquidated damages, not a penalty. Landlord and Tenant agree that if Tenant leaves early, Landlord's damages (rent lost while the Room is vacant and the cost of advertising, showing, screening, and turnover) are difficult to determine at the time of signing, and that the early termination fee is a reasonable estimate of those damages and not a penalty. When Tenant completes every step in paragraph (a), Tenant owes no rent for any period after the Early Move-Out Date, and the fee is Landlord's only remedy for the early termination itself. The fee does not cover unpaid rent through the Early Move-Out Date, damage beyond normal wear and tear, or other charges allowed by this Agreement and District of Columbia law.

(c) If Tenant leaves early without using this option. Using paragraph (a) is Tenant's choice. If Tenant moves out before the end date without completing paragraph (a), Landlord will not charge the early termination fee. Instead, Tenant remains responsible for rent as it comes due until the earlier of the end date or the date a replacement tenant's rent begins, plus Landlord's reasonable costs of re-renting. If Tenant moves out before the end of the term, or in bad faith refuses to take possession, any actual damages Landlord may recover are subject to Landlord's duty to mitigate those damages. (D.C. Code § 42-3505.52)

A spouse or dependent may terminate within one year after Tenant dies in military service or while performing covered National Guard or Reserve duty, and a Tenant who suffers a catastrophic injury or illness in such service (or the spouse or dependent, if Tenant lacks capacity) may terminate within one year after it occurs.   • Victims of intrafamily offenses (D.C. Code §§ 42-3505.07, 16-1001(8)). Tenant may be released from this Lease if Tenant, or a minor child for whom Tenant is the parent or guardian, is a victim of an intrafamily offense, meaning a criminal offense committed by a current or former intimate partner (spouse, domestic partner, co-parent, or dating or sexual partner), a family member, or a household member. Tenant gives Landlord written notice of termination under this right, together with the required documentation, within 90 days after the act or event cited in the court petition or reported to the qualified third party. The release takes effect on the earlier of 14 days after Landlord receives the notice and documentation, or the start of a new tenancy for the Property.
