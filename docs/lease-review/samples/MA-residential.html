3. Term

This Lease begins on January 1, 2027 and ends on December 31, 2027. If Tenant stays after the end date with Landlord's consent (including Landlord's acceptance of rent), the tenancy continues month-to-month on the same terms. Either party may end a tenancy at will (month-to-month tenancy) by written notice given at least one full rental period before the termination date and in no case less than 30 days before it; if rent is payable at intervals of three months or longer, three months' written notice is required. Under Massachusetts practice the termination date should fall on the last day of a rental period. Landlord may not bring an eviction action, or remove Tenant, until that notice period has run, and Tenant owes rent at the existing rate for any part of the notice period that Tenant remains in the Premises. A notice may include an offer of a new tenancy on different terms. (Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 186, §§ 12, 13)

Late fee. If rent is not paid on time, Landlord may charge a late fee of $75.00, but only after rent is at least 30 days late. No late fee, interest, or other penalty for late rent may be charged until the rent is 30 days overdue. (Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 186, § 15B(1)(c); 940 CMR 3.17(6)(a))

When Tenant pays a security deposit, Landlord will give Tenant a receipt signed by the person receiving it, stating the amount, the date received, the name of the person receiving it (and, if an agent, the name of Landlord), and a description of the Premises. No deduction may be made for any other purpose. A landlord who fails to return the deposit and interest as required, fails to hold it as required, or tries to enforce a lease term that conflicts with the deposit law forfeits the right to keep any portion and may owe Tenant three times the deposit, plus 5% interest, court costs, and reasonable attorney's fees. Under a written lease, the tenancy is not terminated if Tenant pays or tenders all rent then due, with interest and costs of suit, on or before the day the answer is due in the eviction case. If the total rent that would have come due under this Lease after the Early Move-Out Date is less than the early termination fee, Tenant pays that smaller amount instead.

(b) Liquidated damages, not a penalty. Landlord and Tenant agree that if Tenant leaves early, Landlord's damages (rent lost while the Property is vacant and the cost of advertising, showing, screening, and turnover) are difficult to determine at the time of signing, and that the early termination fee is a reasonable estimate of those damages and not a penalty. When Tenant completes every step in paragraph (a), Tenant owes no rent for any period after the Early Move-Out Date, and the fee is Landlord's only remedy for the early termination itself. The fee does not cover unpaid rent through the Early Move-Out Date, damage beyond normal wear and tear, or other charges allowed by this Lease and Massachusetts law.

(c) If Tenant leaves early without using this option. Using paragraph (a) is Tenant's choice. If Tenant moves out before the end date without completing paragraph (a), Landlord will not charge the early termination fee. Instead, Tenant remains responsible for rent as it comes due until the earlier of the end date or the date a replacement tenant's rent begins, plus Landlord's reasonable costs of re-renting. If Tenant leaves before the end of the Lease term, Landlord will make reasonable efforts to re-rent the Premises and will credit Tenant with rent actually received from a replacement tenant for the remainder of the term, less Landlord's reasonable costs of re-renting.
