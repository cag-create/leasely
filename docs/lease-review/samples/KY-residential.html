Landlord leases to Tenant the residential premises at 123 Sample Ave, Apt 4 (the "Property"). Authorized occupants: Tenant only.

Payment methods. Tenant shall pay rent by any of these methods accepted by Landlord: Keycove online payment (ACH or card). Rent is paid on the date Landlord receives the funds.

Late fee. If rent is not paid on time, Landlord may charge a late fee of $75.00. The late fee must be a reasonable estimate of Landlord's costs from the late payment and not a penalty.

5. Security Deposit

Tenant shall pay a security deposit of $1,500.00. Kentucky law sets no maximum amount for a security deposit.

Landlord shall deposit the security deposit in an account used only for security deposits at a bank or other lending institution regulated by Kentucky or a federal agency, and shall tell Tenant the location of the account and the account number before Tenant pays the deposit. Landlord may not keep any part of the deposit if it was not held in that separate account and the move-in and move-out damage lists were not provided. (KRS 383.580(1), (4))

At the end of the tenancy Landlord shall inspect the Property and prepare a comprehensive written list of any damage that is the basis for a charge against the security deposit, with the estimated cost of repairing each item. Tenant has the right to inspect the Property to check the list's accuracy, and Landlord and Tenant sign the list; if Tenant disagrees with any item, Tenant states the specific items in writing and signs that statement. The deposit may be applied only to damage beyond ordinary wear and tear and to rent owed. Landlord will return the balance of the deposit, with the itemized list of deductions, to Tenant's last known or reasonably determinable address within 30 days after the tenancy ends. If Tenant leaves without paying the last month's rent and does not ask for the deposit, Landlord may apply it to the rent owed after 30 days. If a refund is due and Tenant does not respond within 60 days after Landlord sends notice of the refund amount, Landlord may keep the deposit. (KRS 383.580(3)–(7))

Before Tenant pays the security deposit, Landlord shall give Tenant a comprehensive written list of any existing damage to the Property that would be the basis for a charge against the deposit, with the estimated cost of repairing it. Tenant may inspect the Property before moving in to check the list's accuracy. Landlord and Tenant sign the list, and the signatures are conclusive evidence of its accuracy except as to hidden defects. If Tenant disagrees with any item, Tenant states the specific items in writing and signs that statement.

Except in an emergency or where it is impracticable, Landlord will give Tenant at least two days' notice of intent to enter and will enter only at reasonable times. Landlord may enter without consent in an emergency, and will not abuse the right of access or use it to harass Tenant. (KRS 383.615)

9. Maintenance and Repairs

Landlord shall comply with applicable building and housing codes materially affecting health and safety; make all repairs and do whatever is necessary to put and keep the Property in a fit and habitable condition; keep common areas clean and safe; maintain in good and safe working order all electrical, plumbing, sanitary, heating, ventilating, air-conditioning, and other facilities and appliances Landlord supplies or is required to supply; and supply running water, reasonable amounts of hot water at all times, and reasonable heat between October 1 and May 1, except where heat or hot water is generated by an installation within Tenant's exclusive control. (KRS 383.595)

Tenant shall keep the Property clean and safe, use appliances and fixtures only for their intended purposes, not damage or allow damage to the Property beyond normal wear and tear, comply with building and housing codes that apply to tenants, and promptly notify Landlord in writing of any needed repair, water leak, or visible mold. Tenant shall not make alterations, paint, or change or add locks without Landlord's written consent, except as the law allows.

The Property may be used only as a private residence by Tenant and the authorized occupants. Tenant may not assign this Lease or sublet any part of the Property without Landlord's prior written consent. No illegal activity is permitted on the premises.

11. Default and Eviction

If rent is unpaid when due, Landlord may give Tenant written notice of the nonpayment and of Landlord's intention to terminate this Lease if the rent is not paid within 7 days. If Tenant does not pay within 7 days after that notice, Landlord may terminate this Lease and file a forcible detainer action in District Court. (KRS 383.660(2); KRS 383.210)

Landlord may recover possession only through a forcible detainer action in the District Court of the county where the Property is located and a warrant of restitution issued by the court. Landlord will not lock Tenant out, interrupt heat, electricity, water, gas, or other essential services, or remove Tenant's belongings to force Tenant out, except after abandonment or surrender or as the court orders. (KRS 383.200, 383.210; KRS 383.690)

12. Early Termination by Tenant (Lease Break)

(a) Tenant's early termination option. Tenant may end this Lease before the end date by doing all of the following: (1) giving Landlord written notice of the date Tenant will move out (the "Early Move-Out Date") at least 30 days before that date; (2) paying all rent and other charges due through the Early Move-Out Date; (3) paying an early termination fee of $3,000.00 on or before the Early Move-Out Date; and (4) moving out and returning all keys and access devices by the Early Move-Out Date. If the total rent that would have come due under this Lease after the Early Move-Out Date is less than the early termination fee, Tenant pays that smaller amount instead.

(b) Liquidated damages, not a penalty. Landlord and Tenant agree that if Tenant leaves early, Landlord's damages (rent lost while the Property is vacant and the cost of advertising, showing, screening, and turnover) are difficult to determine at the time of signing, and that the early termination fee is a reasonable estimate of those damages and not a penalty. When Tenant completes every step in paragraph (a), Tenant owes no rent for any period after the Early Move-Out Date, and the fee is Landlord's only remedy for the early termination itself. The fee does not cover unpaid rent through the Early Move-Out Date, damage beyond normal wear and tear, or other charges allowed by this Lease and Kentucky law.

(c) If Tenant leaves early without using this option. Using paragraph (a) is Tenant's choice. If Tenant moves out before the end date without completing paragraph (a), Landlord will not charge the early termination fee. Instead, Tenant remains responsible for rent as it comes due until the earlier of the end date or the date a replacement tenant's rent begins, plus Landlord's reasonable costs of re-renting. If Tenant abandons the Property, Landlord shall make reasonable efforts to re-rent it at a fair rental. If Landlord re-rents the Property for a term beginning before this Lease ends, this Lease ends on the date the new tenancy begins. (KRS 383.670(3))

(d) Rights protected by law: no early termination fee. Paragraphs (a) through (c) do not apply, and Landlord will not charge the early termination fee, when Tenant ends this Lease under any of the following laws. This Lease cannot waive or reduce these rights, and if any part of this Lease conflicts with them, the law controls. Each right has its own notice and documentation requirements:

  • Military service (federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act) (50 U.S.C. § 3955). Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant enters military service after signing it; or if Tenant, while in military service, signed this Lease and then receives orders for a permanent change of station (which includes separation or retirement) or to deploy with a military unit or in support of a military operation for 90 days or more; or if Tenant signed this Lease upon such orders and then receives a stop movement order of 30 days or more (or indefinite) that prevents occupancy. A spouse or dependent may terminate within one year after Tenant dies in military service or while performing covered National Guard or Reserve duty, and a Tenant who suffers a catastrophic injury or illness in such service (or the spouse or dependent, if Tenant lacks capacity) may terminate within one year after it occurs. Termination also ends the obligations of Tenant's dependents under this Lease. Tenant delivers written notice to Landlord by hand, private business carrier, mail with return receipt requested, or electronic means. Because rent is paid monthly, termination is effective 30 days after the first date on which the next rent payment is due after the notice is delivered; termination after a stop movement order is effective when the notice and orders are delivered. The notice must include a copy of the military orders (official orders, including orders for separation or retirement, or a notification, certification, or verification from the commanding officer). Rent is owed only on a prorated basis through the effective date of termination. Landlord may not impose an early termination charge, but Tenant still owes unpaid amounts due under this Lease, including reasonable charges for excess wear. Rent paid in advance for any period after termination will be refunded within 30 days after the effective date. Landlord may not hold Tenant's belongings or security deposit to satisfy a claim for rent accruing after termination.
  • Kentucky National Guard on Title 32 or State active duty (KRS 38.510). A member of the Kentucky National Guard called to active duty under Title 32 of the United States Code, or to State active duty by the Governor, under orders for 30 days or more, has the same lease termination rights as a member called to federal active duty under the federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (described above). Tenant gives written notice of termination as described for the federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act above. Tenant provides a copy of the Title 32 or State active duty orders, or equivalent documentation, as described for the federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act above. Tenant owes rent only as allowed under the federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (rent prorated to the effective termination date and no early termination fee).
  • Protective order holders (domestic violence or interpersonal protective order) (KRS 383.300). Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant, or a minor member of Tenant's household, is protected by a valid domestic violence order under KRS 403.740 or a valid interpersonal protective order under KRS 456.060 that restrains the other party from unauthorized contact. If the order was obtained before this Lease was signed, Tenant must also show Landlord a safety concern that arose after this Lease was signed. A person who is both the protected tenant and the restrained individual cannot use this right. Tenant gives Landlord written notice of termination, effective on a date stated in the notice that is at least 30 days after Landlord receives it. The notice must include a copy of the valid protective order. Tenant owes rent prorated to the effective termination date, payable when it would otherwise be due, and is not liable for any other rent or fees, including the early termination fee, due solely to the early termination. Landlord will not make a negative credit report or give a negative character reference because of the termination. No damages or penalties are owed if Tenant terminates 14 or more days before occupancy. The tenancy continues for any remaining tenants, and the restrained individual named in the order is liable to Landlord for Landlord's economic losses from the early termination and remains liable for rent if excluded from the Property.
  • Fire or casualty (KRS 383.650; KRS 383.170). If the Property is damaged or destroyed by fire, casualty, the elements, an act of God, or other cause so that enjoyment of the Property is substantially impaired, Tenant may terminate this Lease. Where the building is destroyed by fire or other casualty without Tenant's fault or neglect, Tenant is not liable for rent for the rest of the term. Tenant gives Landlord 14 days' notice of termination, but Tenant may move out immediately. Rent is accounted for as of the date of the casualty, Landlord returns all unused prepaid rent, and Tenant owes no early termination fee.
  • Landlord's material noncompliance (KRS 383.625). If Landlord materially fails to comply with this Lease, or fails to comply with Landlord's maintenance duties in a way that materially affects health and safety, Tenant may terminate this Lease, unless the condition was caused by Tenant, a family member, or a guest. Tenant gives Landlord written notice specifying the breach and stating that this Lease will end on a date at least 30 days after Landlord receives the notice if the breach is not remedied within 14 days. If Landlord remedies the breach before the termination date, this Lease continues. If substantially the same breach recurs within six months, Tenant may terminate on at least 14 days' written notice. Tenant owes no early termination fee, Landlord returns all prepaid rent, and Tenant may also recover damages.
  • Unlawful ouster or shutoff of essential services (KRS 383.655). If Landlord unlawfully removes or excludes Tenant from the Property, or willfully interrupts heat, running water, hot water, electricity, gas, or another essential service, Tenant may recover possession or terminate this Lease. No advance notice period is required. Tenant owes no early termination fee, Landlord returns all prepaid rent, and Tenant may recover up to three months' rent and a reasonable attorney's fee.
