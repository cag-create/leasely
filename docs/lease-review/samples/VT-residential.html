5. Security Deposit

Tenant shall pay a security deposit of $1,500.00. Vermont sets no statewide limit on the amount of a security deposit. A security deposit is any advance, deposit, or prepaid rent, however named, that is refundable to Tenant at the end of the tenancy. A town or municipality may adopt a security deposit ordinance that adds protections (for example, interest), but not one that limits how the deposit is held.

If Landlord's interest in the Premises ends, Landlord will transfer the security deposit to the new landlord, who will give Tenant actual notice of the new landlord's name and address with a statement that the deposit has been transferred. (9 V.S.A. § 4461(f))

Landlord will return the security deposit, together with a written statement itemizing any deductions, within 14 days after the date Landlord discovers that Tenant vacated or abandoned the Premises, or within 14 days after the date Tenant vacated if Tenant gave Landlord notice of that date, by hand delivery or by mail to Tenant's last known address. For a seasonal rental not intended as a primary residence, the deadline is 60 days. Landlord may keep all or part of the deposit only for: nonpayment of rent; damage to Landlord's property, other than normal wear and tear or damage resulting from events beyond Tenant's control; nonpayment of utility or other charges Tenant was required to pay directly to Landlord or to a utility; and expenses of removing articles Tenant abandoned. If Landlord fails to return the deposit with the statement within 14 days, Landlord forfeits the right to withhold any portion of it, and if the failure is willful Landlord is liable for double the amount wrongfully withheld plus reasonable attorney's fees and costs. (9 V.S.A. § 4461(a)-(e))

Landlord and Tenant will complete a written move-in condition report, with photographs, and a move-out inspection. Items not noted at move-in are presumed to have been in good condition.

12. Early Termination by Tenant (Lease Break)

(a) Tenant's early termination option. Tenant may end this Lease before the end date by doing all of the following: (1) giving Landlord written notice of the date Tenant will move out (the "Early Move-Out Date") at least 30 days before that date; (2) paying all rent and other charges due through the Early Move-Out Date; (3) paying an early termination fee of $3,000.00 on or before the Early Move-Out Date; and (4) moving out and returning all keys and access devices by the Early Move-Out Date. If the total rent that would have come due under this Lease after the Early Move-Out Date is less than the early termination fee, Tenant pays that smaller amount instead.

(b) Liquidated damages, not a penalty. Landlord and Tenant agree that if Tenant leaves early, Landlord's damages (rent lost while the Property is vacant and the cost of advertising, showing, screening, and turnover) are difficult to determine at the time of signing, and that the early termination fee is a reasonable estimate of those damages and not a penalty. When Tenant completes every step in paragraph (a), Tenant owes no rent for any period after the Early Move-Out Date, and the fee is Landlord's only remedy for the early termination itself. The fee does not cover unpaid rent through the Early Move-Out Date, damage beyond normal wear and tear, or other charges allowed by this Lease and Vermont law.

(c) If Tenant leaves early without using this option. Using paragraph (a) is Tenant's choice. If Tenant moves out before the end date without completing paragraph (a), Landlord will not charge the early termination fee. Instead, Tenant remains responsible for rent as it comes due until the earlier of the end date or the date a replacement tenant's rent begins, plus Landlord's reasonable costs of re-renting. If Tenant abandons the Premises before this Lease ends, Tenant remains liable for rent until the Lease expires, except that if Landlord re-rents the Premises before then, this Lease terminates on the date the new tenancy begins and Tenant owes no rent after that date. The notice must include a copy of the military orders (official orders, including orders for separation or retirement, or a notification, certification, or verification from the commanding officer). Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant is a victim of abuse, sexual assault, or stalking, or is a parent, foster parent, legal guardian, or caretaker with at least partial physical custody of such a victim, and Tenant reasonably believes it is necessary to vacate the Premises because of a fear of imminent harm to any such protected tenant from abuse, sexual assault, or stalking, or because a protected tenant was a victim of sexual assault on the Premises within the six months before the notice of termination.
