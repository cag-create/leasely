Washington Co-Living / Room Rental Agreement

Room rental in a shared residence. Washington landlord-tenant law, including its eviction procedure, applies in full.

1. Parties

Landlord: Sample Landlord LLC (100 Main Street, Suite 2)

Tenant: Jordan Sample (jordan@example.com)

2. Nature of This Agreement

Tenant rents Bedroom B (the "Room") at 123 Sample Ave, Apt 4 (the "Residence") and may use the shared common areas. Authorized occupants of the Room: Tenant only.

Landlord may move Tenant to a comparable room in the Residence only with Tenant's written consent or on at least 30 days' written notice with no increase in rent. The "co-living" and "room rental" terms of this Agreement govern house rules and room assignment only. Tenant has the rights of a residential tenant under Washington law. If Landlord seeks to remove Tenant from possession, Landlord will use the court eviction process described in this Agreement and will never use self-help removal.

3. Term

This Agreement begins on January 1, 2027 and ends on December 31, 2027. If Tenant stays after the end date with Landlord's consent (including Landlord's acceptance of rent), the tenancy continues month-to-month on the same terms. Tenant may end a month-to-month or other periodic tenancy by written notice given at least 20 days before the end of a rental period. Landlord may not end a periodic tenancy, refuse to continue a tenancy, or evict Tenant except for one of the causes listed in RCW 59.18.650(2), using the notice period stated there (for example, an uncured material breach after a 10-day notice, owner or immediate-family occupancy or sale of a single-family residence with at least 90 days' notice, or another legitimate economic or business reason with at least 60 days' notice). Every such notice must be served as RCW 59.12.040 requires and must state the facts supporting the cause. (RCW 59.18.200(1)(a); RCW 59.18.650; RCW 59.12.040)

4. Rent

Tenant shall pay rent of $1,500.00 per month, due on the 1st day of each month. Rent covers the Room, use of the common areas, and these shared utilities: Water and trash.

Payment methods. Tenant shall pay rent by any of these methods accepted by Landlord: Keycove online payment (ACH or card). Rent is paid on the date Landlord receives the funds.

Late fee. If rent is not paid on time, Landlord may charge a late fee of $75.00, but only after rent is at least 5 days late. The late fee must be a reasonable estimate of Landlord's costs from the late payment and not a penalty.

5. Security Deposit

Tenant shall pay a security deposit of $1,500.00. Washington sets no statewide cap on the amount of a security deposit. A fee or deposit to hold the unit before move-in may not exceed 25% of the first month's rent and must be credited to the first month's rent or the security deposit if Tenant moves in (RCW 59.18.253, 59.18.610(4)). On Tenant's written request, Landlord must allow deposits, nonrefundable fees, and last month's rent to be paid in installments (three equal monthly installments for a term of three months or longer, otherwise two), unless total deposits and nonrefundable fees do not exceed 25% of the first full month's rent and last month's rent is not required at move-in (RCW 59.18.610). A nonrefundable fee may never be designated as a deposit (RCW 59.18.285).

Landlord will promptly deposit the security deposit in a trust account maintained for tenant security deposits at a financial institution or licensed escrow agent located in Washington. Landlord will give Tenant a written receipt for the deposit and written notice of the name, address, and location of the depository, and will notify Tenant in writing of any change. Unless otherwise agreed in writing, Landlord is entitled to any interest paid on the trust account. If Landlord's interest in the Property is transferred, the deposit will be transferred to an equivalent trust account of the successor landlord, who will promptly notify Tenant. (RCW 59.18.270)

Within 30 days after this Lease ends and Tenant vacates (or within 30 days after Landlord learns that Tenant has abandoned the Property), Landlord will deliver to Tenant personally, or mail first-class to Tenant's last known address, a full and specific statement of the basis for retaining any part of the deposit, together with any refund due. The statement will include copies of estimates received or invoices paid that reasonably substantiate damage charges; for repairs done by Landlord or Landlord's employees, it will state the time spent and the reasonable hourly rate and include documentation of the cost of materials. No part of the deposit may be withheld for wear resulting from ordinary use, for carpet cleaning unless Landlord documents carpet wear beyond ordinary use, for repair or replacement of fixtures, equipment, appliances, or furnishings whose condition was not reasonably documented in the move-in checklist, or for more than the cost of repairing or replacing only the damaged portion of an item. If Landlord fails to comply, Landlord is liable to Tenant for the full deposit. (RCW 59.18.280)

Landlord may not collect a deposit unless, at the start of the tenancy, Landlord gives Tenant a written checklist or statement specifically describing the condition and cleanliness of, or existing damage to, the premises, fixtures, equipment, appliances, and furnishings, including walls (paint and wallpaper), carpets and other flooring, furniture, and appliances. Landlord and Tenant will sign and date the checklist, Tenant will receive a copy of the signed checklist, and Tenant may request one free replacement copy. If Landlord collects a deposit without providing this checklist, Landlord is liable to Tenant for the amount of the deposit.

9. Maintenance and Repairs

Landlord will at all times during the tenancy keep the Property fit for human habitation, including by substantially complying with applicable codes where noncompliance endangers or impairs Tenant's health or safety; keeping structural components in reasonably good repair; keeping shared or common areas reasonably clean, sanitary, and safe; providing a reasonable pest-control program at the start of the tenancy and, except for a single-family residence, controlling infestation during the tenancy unless Tenant caused it; providing reasonably adequate locks and keys; maintaining electrical, plumbing, heating, and other facilities and appliances Landlord supplies in reasonably good working order; keeping the unit reasonably weathertight; and providing facilities adequate to supply heat, water, and hot water as reasonably required by Tenant. (RCW 59.18.060)

Tenant shall keep the Room clean and safe, use appliances and fixtures only for their intended purposes, not damage or allow damage to the Residence beyond normal wear and tear, comply with building and housing codes that apply to tenants, and promptly notify Landlord in writing of any needed repair, water leak, or visible mold. 10. Use, Assignment, and Subletting

The Room may be used only as a private residence by Tenant and the authorized occupants. Tenant may not assign this Agreement or sublet any part of the Room without Landlord's prior written consent. No illegal activity is permitted on the premises.

11. Default and Eviction

If rent is not paid when due, Landlord may serve a written 14-day notice to pay rent or vacate in the form required by RCW 59.18.057. Tenant may cure by paying the rent owed within 14 days after service; if the notice is served by mail, five additional days are allowed before an eviction action may be filed. For this purpose, rent means the recurring and periodic charges identified in this Lease and does not include late fees, damages, legal costs, or other nonrecurring charges, and Tenant's right to possession may not be conditioned on paying anything other than rent. (RCW 59.12.030(3); RCW 59.12.040; RCW 59.18.057; RCW 59.18.283)

Landlord may recover possession of the Property only through an unlawful detainer action in court under chapters 59.12 and 59.18 RCW, and only for a cause listed in RCW 59.18.650. Landlord will not remove or exclude Tenant from the Property except under a court order; if Landlord does, Tenant may recover possession or terminate this Lease and recover actual damages. (RCW 59.18.290(1); RCW 59.18.650; RCW 59.12.030)

12. Early Termination by Tenant (Lease Break)

(a) Tenant's early termination option. Tenant may end this Agreement before the end date by doing all of the following: (1) giving Landlord written notice of the date Tenant will move out (the "Early Move-Out Date") at least 30 days before that date; (2) paying all rent and other charges due through the Early Move-Out Date; (3) paying an early termination fee of $3,000.00 on or before the Early Move-Out Date; and (4) moving out and returning all keys and access devices by the Early Move-Out Date. If the total rent that would have come due under this Agreement after the Early Move-Out Date is less than the early termination fee, Tenant pays that smaller amount instead.

(b) Liquidated damages, not a penalty. Landlord and Tenant agree that if Tenant leaves early, Landlord's damages (rent lost while the Room is vacant and the cost of advertising, showing, screening, and turnover) are difficult to determine at the time of signing, and that the early termination fee is a reasonable estimate of those damages and not a penalty. When Tenant completes every step in paragraph (a), Tenant owes no rent for any period after the Early Move-Out Date, and the fee is Landlord's only remedy for the early termination itself. The fee does not cover unpaid rent through the Early Move-Out Date, damage beyond normal wear and tear, or other charges allowed by this Agreement and Washington law.

(c) If Tenant leaves early without using this option. Using paragraph (a) is Tenant's choice. If Tenant moves out before the end date without completing paragraph (a), Landlord will not charge the early termination fee. Instead, Tenant remains responsible for rent as it comes due until the earlier of the end date or the date a replacement tenant's rent begins, plus Landlord's reasonable costs of re-renting. If Tenant abandons the Property before this Lease ends, Landlord will make a reasonable effort to re-rent it and mitigate damages. For a month-to-month tenancy, Tenant is liable for rent for the 30 days following the earlier of the date Landlord learns of the abandonment or the date the next rent payment would have been due. For a longer term, Tenant is liable for the lesser of the rent for the remainder of the term, or the rent accrued during the period reasonably necessary to re-rent at a fair rental plus any difference between that fair rental and the rent under this Lease, plus Landlord's actual costs of re-renting and any court costs and reasonable attorneys' fees a court awards. (RCW 59.18.310(1))
