West Virginia Co-Living / Room Rental Agreement

Room rental in a shared residence. West Virginia landlord-tenant law, including its eviction procedure, applies in full.

Landlord: Sample Landlord LLC (100 Main Street, Suite 2)

Tenant: Jordan Sample (jordan@example.com)

2. Nature of This Agreement

Tenant rents Bedroom B (the "Room") at 123 Sample Ave, Apt 4 (the "Residence") and may use the shared common areas. Authorized occupants of the Room: Tenant only.

Landlord may move Tenant to a comparable room in the Residence only with Tenant's written consent or on at least 30 days' written notice with no increase in rent. The "co-living" and "room rental" terms of this Agreement govern house rules and room assignment only. Tenant has the rights of a residential tenant under West Virginia law. If Landlord seeks to remove Tenant from possession, Landlord will use the court eviction process described in this Agreement and will never use self-help removal.

3. Term

This Agreement begins on January 1, 2027 and ends on December 31, 2027. If Tenant stays after the end date with Landlord's consent (including Landlord's acceptance of rent), the tenancy continues month-to-month on the same terms. Either Landlord or Tenant may end a month-to-month tenancy by written notice given at least one full rental period (one month) before the end of a rental period. A year-to-year tenancy requires written notice at least three months before the end of the year. No notice is required to end a lease that is set to end on a specific date. (W. Va. Code § 37-6-5)

4. Rent

Tenant shall pay rent of $1,500.00 per month, due on the 1st day of each month. Rent covers the Room, use of the common areas, and these shared utilities: Water and trash.

Payment methods. Tenant shall pay rent by any of these methods accepted by Landlord: Keycove online payment (ACH or card). Rent is paid on the date Landlord receives the funds.

Late fee. If rent is not paid on time, Landlord may charge a late fee of $75.00. West Virginia law sets no numeric limit on late fees. Any late charge must be a reasonable charge for late payment stated in this Lease. (W. Va. Code § 37-6A-2(b)(1))

5. Security Deposit

Tenant shall pay a security deposit of $1,500.00. West Virginia law sets no maximum amount for a security deposit. A pet fee or application fee is not part of the security deposit, and is nonrefundable only if the parties expressly agree in writing that it is nonrefundable.

After the tenancy ends, Landlord shall deliver to Tenant the security deposit, minus any lawful deductions, together with a written itemization of the deductions, within 60 days after the tenancy ends or within 45 days after a new tenant occupies the Property, whichever is shorter. If damage exceeds the deposit and requires a third-party contractor, Landlord shall notify Tenant in writing within that period and then has an additional 15 days to provide the itemization and repair costs. Landlord may apply the deposit only to rent due (including reasonable late charges stated in this Lease), damages from Tenant's noncompliance with this Lease less reasonable wear and tear, unpaid utilities that are Tenant's obligation and that Landlord paid, reasonable costs of removing and storing Tenant's property, and other damages or charges provided in this Lease. Landlord may deliver the deposit and notices in person or by mail to Tenant's last known or forwarding address; Tenant is responsible for giving Landlord an accurate address. If mail is returned, Landlord holds the deposit for six months for pickup at Landlord's place of business within 72 hours after Tenant's written request. (W. Va. Code §§ 37-6A-1(7), 37-6A-2)

Landlord and Tenant will complete a written move-in condition report, with photographs, and a move-out inspection. Items not noted at move-in are presumed to have been in good condition.

8. Landlord Entry

West Virginia has no statute setting an entry-notice period. Landlord will give Tenant at least 24 hours' notice before entering for inspections, repairs, or showings, and will enter only at reasonable times, except in an emergency or when Tenant consents at the time of entry. This applies to the Room; the common areas are shared and Landlord may enter them at reasonable times.

9. Maintenance and Repairs

Landlord shall deliver the Property in a fit and habitable condition at the start of the tenancy and keep it that way; maintain it in compliance with applicable health, safety, fire, and housing codes (unless a failure is the fault of Tenant, a family member, or a guest); keep common areas of a multiple-unit building clean, safe, and in repair; make all repairs needed to keep the Property fit and habitable unless the repair was needed mainly because of a lack of reasonable care by Tenant, a family member, or a guest; keep electrical, plumbing, sanitary, heating, ventilating, air-conditioning, and other facilities and appliances Landlord supplies in good and safe working order; provide waste receptacles in a multiple-unit building; and supply running water, reasonable hot water at all times, and reasonable heat from October 1 through the last day of April, except where those are generated by an installation within Tenant's exclusive control. Landlord is not required to make repairs while Tenant is behind in rent. (W. Va. Code § 37-6-30)

Tenant shall keep the Room clean and safe, use appliances and fixtures only for their intended purposes, not damage or allow damage to the Residence beyond normal wear and tear, comply with building and housing codes that apply to tenants, and promptly notify Landlord in writing of any needed repair, water leak, or visible mold. Tenant shall not make alterations, paint, or change or add locks without Landlord's written consent, except as the law allows.

11. Default and Eviction

West Virginia does not require a pay-or-quit notice before filing. If Tenant is in arrears in rent, has breached a covenant of this Lease, or has deliberately or negligently damaged the Property, Landlord may file a verified petition for summary relief in the magistrate court or circuit court of the county where the Property is located. The court schedules a hearing 5 to 10 judicial days after filing, and Tenant may file a written defense within five days after receiving notice of the hearing. (W. Va. Code §§ 55-3A-1 to 55-3A-3)

Landlord may recover possession only through a court proceeding, such as a petition for summary relief in the magistrate or circuit court under W. Va. Code § 55-3A-1 or an action for unlawful detainer, and a court order carried out by the sheriff. Landlord will not lock Tenant out, shut off utilities, or remove Tenant's belongings without a court order, except as the law allows for abandoned property. (W. Va. Code §§ 55-3A-1, 55-3A-3, 37-6-19)

12. Early Termination by Tenant (Lease Break)

(a) Tenant's early termination option. Tenant may end this Agreement before the end date by doing all of the following: (1) giving Landlord written notice of the date Tenant will move out (the "Early Move-Out Date") at least 30 days before that date; (2) paying all rent and other charges due through the Early Move-Out Date; (3) paying an early termination fee of $3,000.00 on or before the Early Move-Out Date; and (4) moving out and returning all keys and access devices by the Early Move-Out Date. If the total rent that would have come due under this Agreement after the Early Move-Out Date is less than the early termination fee, Tenant pays that smaller amount instead.

(b) Liquidated damages, not a penalty. Landlord and Tenant agree that if Tenant leaves early, Landlord's damages (rent lost while the Room is vacant and the cost of advertising, showing, screening, and turnover) are difficult to determine at the time of signing, and that the early termination fee is a reasonable estimate of those damages and not a penalty. When Tenant completes every step in paragraph (a), Tenant owes no rent for any period after the Early Move-Out Date, and the fee is Landlord's only remedy for the early termination itself. The fee does not cover unpaid rent through the Early Move-Out Date, damage beyond normal wear and tear, or other charges allowed by this Agreement and West Virginia law.

(c) If Tenant leaves early without using this option. Using paragraph (a) is Tenant's choice. If Tenant moves out before the end date without completing paragraph (a), Landlord will not charge the early termination fee. Instead, Tenant remains responsible for rent as it comes due until the earlier of the end date or the date a replacement tenant's rent begins, plus Landlord's reasonable costs of re-renting. If Tenant leaves early without using the early termination option, Landlord will make reasonable efforts to re-rent the Property, and rent received from a replacement tenant is credited against what Tenant owes.

(d) Rights protected by law: no early termination fee. Paragraphs (a) through (c) do not apply, and Landlord will not charge the early termination fee, when Tenant ends this Agreement under any of the following laws. This Agreement cannot waive or reduce these rights, and if any part of this Agreement conflicts with them, the law controls. Each right has its own notice and documentation requirements:

  • Military service (federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act) (50 U.S.C. § 3955). Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant enters military service after signing it; or if Tenant, while in military service, signed this Lease and then receives orders for a permanent change of station (which includes separation or retirement) or to deploy with a military unit or in support of a military operation for 90 days or more; or if Tenant signed this Lease upon such orders and then receives a stop movement order of 30 days or more (or indefinite) that prevents occupancy. A spouse or dependent may terminate within one year after Tenant dies in military service or while performing covered National Guard or Reserve duty, and a Tenant who suffers a catastrophic injury or illness in such service (or the spouse or dependent, if Tenant lacks capacity) may terminate within one year after it occurs. Termination also ends the obligations of Tenant's dependents under this Lease. Tenant delivers written notice to Landlord by hand, private business carrier, mail with return receipt requested, or electronic means. Because rent is paid monthly, termination is effective 30 days after the first date on which the next rent payment is due after the notice is delivered; termination after a stop movement order is effective when the notice and orders are delivered. The notice must include a copy of the military orders (official orders, including orders for separation or retirement, or a notification, certification, or verification from the commanding officer). Rent is owed only on a prorated basis through the effective date of termination. Landlord may not impose an early termination charge, but Tenant still owes unpaid amounts due under this Lease, including reasonable charges for excess wear. Rent paid in advance for any period after termination will be refunded within 30 days after the effective date. Landlord may not hold Tenant's belongings or security deposit to satisfy a claim for rent accruing after termination.
  • National Guard on State active duty (West Virginia Servicemembers Civil Relief Act) (W. Va. Code § 15-1F-11). A member of the West Virginia National Guard called to State active duty by the Governor for 30 days or more has the same lease termination rights as a servicemember on federal active duty under the federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (described above). Tenant gives written notice of termination as described for the federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act above. Tenant provides a copy of the Governor's State active duty orders, or equivalent documentation, as described for the federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act above. Tenant owes rent only as allowed under the federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (rent prorated to the effective termination date and no early termination fee).
  • Death of Tenant (W. Va. Code § 37-6-11(b)). If Tenant dies, Tenant's heir, personal representative, devisee, or assignee may terminate this Lease before it expires. Written notice is hand-delivered or mailed to Landlord, and termination takes effect on the last day of the calendar month that is two months after the date the notice is hand-delivered or postmarked. Tenant's estate remains liable for rent and other sums owed before or during the two-month notice period and for the cost of restoring the Property to its condition at the start of the tenancy, ordinary wear and tear excepted, but owes no early termination fee. This right cannot be waived, and any lease term requiring a longer notice period is void.
  • Buildings destroyed by fire or other cause (W. Va. Code § 37-6-28). If buildings on the Property are destroyed in whole or in part by fire or otherwise, without fault or negligence of Tenant, rent is reasonably reduced until they are replaced, and if Landlord does not rebuild or replace them as soon as reasonably possible, Tenant may surrender possession after that reasonable time. Tenant surrenders possession of the Property to Landlord. Tenant is relieved of all further liability for rent after the date of surrender and owes no early termination fee.
