Florida Co-Living / Room Rental Agreement

Room rental in a shared residence. Florida landlord-tenant law, including its eviction procedure, applies in full.

1. Parties

Landlord: Sample Landlord LLC (100 Main Street, Suite 2)

Tenant: Jordan Sample (jordan@example.com)

2. Nature of This Agreement

Tenant rents Bedroom B (the "Room") at 123 Sample Ave, Apt 4 (the "Residence") and may use the shared common areas. Authorized occupants of the Room: Tenant only.

Landlord may move Tenant to a comparable room in the Residence only with Tenant's written consent or on at least 30 days' written notice with no increase in rent. The "co-living" and "room rental" terms of this Agreement govern house rules and room assignment only. Tenant has the rights of a residential tenant under Florida law. If Landlord seeks to remove Tenant from possession, Landlord will use the court eviction process described in this Agreement and will never use self-help removal.

3. Term

This Agreement begins on January 1, 2027 and ends on December 31, 2027. If Tenant stays after the end date with Landlord's consent (including Landlord's acceptance of rent), the tenancy continues month-to-month on the same terms. Either party may end a month-to-month tenancy by written notice given at least 30 days before the end of any monthly period (7 days before the end of a weekly period, 30 days before the end of a quarterly period, or 60 days before the end of an annual period). Notice is delivered by mail, by hand, by e-mail under a signed electronic-notice addendum, or, if Tenant is absent, by leaving a copy at the residence. (Fla. Stat. §§ 83.57, 83.56(4))

4. Rent

Tenant shall pay rent of $1,500.00 per month, due on the 1st day of each month. Rent covers the Room, use of the common areas, and these shared utilities: Water and trash.

Payment methods. Tenant shall pay rent by any of these methods accepted by Landlord: Keycove online payment (ACH or card). Rent is paid on the date Landlord receives the funds.

Late fee. If rent is not paid on time, Landlord may charge a late fee of $75.00. The late fee must be a reasonable estimate of Landlord's costs from the late payment and not a penalty.

5. Security Deposit

Tenant shall pay a security deposit of $1,500.00. Florida law sets no maximum security deposit.

Landlord shall either (a) hold the security deposit and any advance rent in a separate non-interest-bearing account in a Florida financial institution; (b) hold it in a separate interest-bearing account in a Florida financial institution, in which case Tenant receives interest of at least 75% of the annualized average interest rate paid on the account or 5% simple interest per year, whichever Landlord elects; or (c) post a surety bond with the clerk of the circuit court and pay Tenant 5% simple interest per year. Landlord will not commingle, pledge, or use the money until it is actually due to Landlord. When interest is owed, Landlord will pay it to Tenant or credit it against rent at least once a year (but not to a Tenant who wrongfully ends the tenancy early). If Landlord rents five or more dwelling units, Landlord will give Tenant written notice, in this Lease or within 30 days after receiving the deposit, of the name and address of the depository holding it (or that Landlord has posted a surety bond) and whether Tenant is entitled to interest, and will give notice within 30 days of any change in how or where it is held. (Fla. Stat. § 83.49(1), (2), (9))

If Landlord does not intend to make a claim on the security deposit, Landlord shall return it, with any interest due, within 15 days after this Lease ends and Tenant vacates. If Landlord intends to make a claim, Landlord shall, within 30 days after this Lease ends, send Tenant written notice of the claim and the reason for it by certified mail to Tenant's last known mailing address, or by e-mail if the parties have signed an electronic-notice addendum under Fla. Stat. § 83.505. Tenant then has 15 days after receiving the notice to object in writing. If Tenant does not object, Landlord may deduct the claim and shall send Tenant the balance within 30 days after the date of the notice. If Landlord does not send the notice within 30 days, Landlord forfeits the right to make a claim against the deposit but may still sue for damages after returning it. Before vacating, Tenant shall give Landlord at least 7 days' written notice by certified mail or personal delivery that includes an address where Tenant can be reached; if Tenant does not, Landlord is relieved of the notice requirement above, but Tenant does not lose any right to the deposit. (Fla. Stat. § 83.49(3), (5))

Landlord and Tenant will complete a written move-in condition report, with photographs, and a move-out inspection. Items not noted at move-in are presumed to have been in good condition.

6. Pets and Parking

Pets allowed: No. Assistance animals for a person with a disability are not pets, and no pet fee or pet deposit applies to them. Parking: One assigned space.

7. House Rules and Guests

Tenant agrees to follow these house rules:

Quiet hours 10pm-7am. Clean shared kitchen after use.

Landlord may change the house rules on at least 30 days' written notice; a change may not reduce Tenant's rights under this Agreement or the law, or substantially change the bargain. Guests may stay no more than seven consecutive nights without Landlord's written consent. Each resident is responsible for their own share of keeping common areas clean.

8. Landlord Entry

Tenant shall not unreasonably withhold consent for Landlord to enter from time to time to inspect the Property, make necessary or agreed repairs, decorations, alterations, or improvements, supply agreed services, or show the Property to prospective or actual purchasers, mortgagees, tenants, workers, or contractors. Landlord may enter at any time to protect or preserve the Property. To make repairs, Landlord will give Tenant at least 24 hours' notice and will enter only between 7:30 a.m. and 8:00 p.m. For the other purposes listed above, Landlord may enter with Tenant's consent, in an emergency, when Tenant unreasonably withholds consent, or when Tenant has been absent for one-half of a rental payment period. If rent is current and Tenant has told Landlord of an intended absence, Landlord will enter during that absence only with Tenant's consent or to protect or preserve the Property. Landlord will not abuse the right of access or use it to harass Tenant. (Fla. Stat. § 83.53) This applies to the Room; the common areas are shared and Landlord may enter them at reasonable times.

9. Maintenance and Repairs

Landlord shall comply with applicable building, housing, and health codes, or, where none apply, keep the roof, windows, doors, floors, steps, porches, exterior walls, foundations, and other structural components in good repair and the plumbing in reasonable working condition. Landlord shall install screens in reasonable condition at the start of the tenancy and repair screen damage once a year when needed. For a unit other than a single-family home or duplex, unless otherwise agreed in writing, Landlord shall also make reasonable provision for extermination of rats, mice, roaches, ants, wood-destroying organisms, and bedbugs (with rent abated and at least 7 days' written notice if Tenant must vacate, for no more than 4 days), locks and keys, clean and safe common areas, garbage removal and outside receptacles, and functioning heat during winter, running water, and hot water. (Fla. Stat. § 83.51)

Tenant shall keep the Room clean and safe, use appliances and fixtures only for their intended purposes, not damage or allow damage to the Residence beyond normal wear and tear, comply with building and housing codes that apply to tenants, and promptly notify Landlord in writing of any needed repair, water leak, or visible mold. Tenant shall not make alterations, paint, or change or add locks without Landlord's written consent, except as the law allows.

10. Use, Assignment, and Subletting

The Room may be used only as a private residence by Tenant and the authorized occupants. Tenant may not assign this Agreement or sublet any part of the Room without Landlord's prior written consent. No illegal activity is permitted on the premises.

11. Default and Eviction

If rent is not paid when due and remains unpaid for 3 days, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and court-observed legal holidays, after Landlord delivers a written demand for payment of the rent or possession of the Property, Landlord may terminate this Lease. This notice requirement cannot be waived. If Landlord accepts partial rent after posting the notice, Landlord will give Tenant a receipt stating the amount received and the agreed date and balance due, deposit the partial rent into the court registry when filing, or post a new 3-day notice for the new amount due. (Fla. Stat. § 83.56(3)-(5))

Landlord may recover possession only by filing an action for possession in the county court where the Property is located, using the summary procedure of Fla. Stat. § 51.011, or when Tenant has surrendered or abandoned the Property. Landlord will not cut off or interrupt any utility service, change the locks or use a bootlock or similar device, remove outside doors, locks, roof, walls, or windows (except for maintenance, repair, or replacement), or remove Tenant's belongings to regain possession. (Fla. Stat. §§ 83.59, 83.67)

12. Early Termination by Tenant (Lease Break)

(a) Tenant's early termination option. Tenant may end this Agreement before the end date by doing all of the following: (1) giving Landlord written notice of the date Tenant will move out (the "Early Move-Out Date") at least 30 days before that date; (2) paying all rent and other charges due through the Early Move-Out Date; (3) paying an early termination fee of $3,000.00 on or before the Early Move-Out Date; and (4) moving out and returning all keys and access devices by the Early Move-Out Date. If the total rent that would have come due under this Agreement after the Early Move-Out Date is less than the early termination fee, Tenant pays that smaller amount instead.

(b) Liquidated damages, not a penalty. Landlord and Tenant agree that if Tenant leaves early, Landlord's damages (rent lost while the Room is vacant and the cost of advertising, showing, screening, and turnover) are difficult to determine at the time of signing, and that the early termination fee is a reasonable estimate of those damages and not a penalty. When Tenant completes every step in paragraph (a), Tenant owes no rent for any period after the Early Move-Out Date, and the fee is Landlord's only remedy for the early termination itself. The fee does not cover unpaid rent through the Early Move-Out Date, damage beyond normal wear and tear, or other charges allowed by this Agreement and Florida law.

(c) If Tenant leaves early without using this option. Using paragraph (a) is Tenant's choice. If Tenant moves out before the end date without completing paragraph (a), Landlord will not charge the early termination fee. Instead, Tenant remains responsible for rent as it comes due until the earlier of the end date or the date a replacement tenant's rent begins, plus Landlord's reasonable costs of re-renting. If Landlord retakes possession of the Property for Tenant's account, Landlord shall exercise good faith in attempting to re-rent it, using at least the same efforts Landlord used in the initial rental or uses to rent its other similar units, and rent received from re-renting will be deducted from the rent Tenant owes. (Fla. Stat. § 83.595)
