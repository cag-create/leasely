## Early termination rights that override the lease-break fee

### Military service (federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act)
*50 U.S.C. § 3955*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant enters military service after signing it; or if Tenant, while in military service, signed this Lease and then receives orders for a permanent change of station (which includes separation or retirement) or to deploy with a military unit or in support of a military operation for 90 days or more; or if Tenant signed this Lease upon such orders and then receives a stop movement order of 30 days or more (or indefinite) that prevents occupancy. A spouse or dependent may terminate within one year after Tenant dies in military service or while performing covered National Guard or Reserve duty, and a Tenant who suffers a catastrophic injury or illness in such service (or the spouse or dependent, if Tenant lacks capacity) may terminate within one year after it occurs. Termination also ends the obligations of Tenant's dependents under this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant delivers written notice to Landlord by hand, private business carrier, mail with return receipt requested, or electronic means. Because rent is paid monthly, termination is effective 30 days after the first date on which the next rent payment is due after the notice is delivered; termination after a stop movement order is effective when the notice and orders are delivered.
- **Documentation:** The notice must include a copy of the military orders (official orders, including orders for separation or retirement, or a notification, certification, or verification from the commanding officer).
- **Owes:** Rent is owed only on a prorated basis through the effective date of termination. Landlord may not impose an early termination charge, but Tenant still owes unpaid amounts due under this Lease, including reasonable charges for excess wear. Rent paid in advance for any period after termination will be refunded within 30 days after the effective date. Landlord may not hold Tenant's belongings or security deposit to satisfy a claim for rent accruing after termination.

### Military orders (Minnesota consumer law)
*Minn. Stat. § 325G.55*

- **Who:** A service member who signed this Lease and afterward is issued orders into active duty, for deployment, or for a permanent change of duty station may cancel this Lease if the activation, deployment, or change of station makes it impractical to abide by its terms.
- **Notice:** Tenant must send Landlord written notice of cancellation by certified mail. The cancellation takes effect the day after Landlord receives proper notice.
- **Documentation:** The notice must include a copy of the orders.
- **Owes:** The statute provides for cancellation at no penalty and a full refund of any money placed on deposit.

### Domestic abuse, criminal sexual conduct, sexual extortion, or harassment
*Minn. Stat. § 504B.206*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease without penalty or liability, except as stated below, if Tenant or another authorized occupant fears imminent violence after being subjected to domestic abuse, criminal sexual conduct, sexual extortion, or harassment.
- **Notice:** Before the tenancy ends, Tenant must deliver to Landlord by mail, in person, or by a form of written communication Tenant regularly uses with Landlord, a signed and dated written notice stating that Tenant fears imminent violence from a person named in the qualifying document if Tenant or the occupant remains, that Tenant needs to terminate the tenancy, the termination date, and written instructions for disposing of any personal property left behind. The tenancy ends on the date in the notice.
- **Documentation:** The notice must be accompanied by a qualifying document: a valid order for protection; a no-contact order currently in effect; a writing signed by a court official or a law enforcement official documenting that Tenant or the occupant is a victim and naming the perpetrator if known; or a statement by a licensed health care professional, domestic abuse advocate, or sexual assault counselor in the statutory form. Landlord may ask for the perpetrator's name but disclosure is not a condition of termination.
- **Owes:** A sole Tenant owes rent for the full month in which the tenancy ends, gives up any claim to return of the security deposit, and is released from rent and other charges for the rest of the term. If there are multiple tenants, the Lease ends for all tenants at the later of the end of the month or the end of the rent interval in which one tenant terminates; all tenants owe rent for that full month, give up the security deposit, and are released from the rest of the term, and the remaining tenants may reapply for a new lease. Rent and other amounts owed before termination remain due. Landlord must keep the notice, the qualifying document, Tenant's new address, and Tenant's victim status confidential. These rights cannot be waived.

### Death of Tenant
*Minn. Stat. § 504B.265*

- **Who:** If Tenant dies (or, if there is more than one tenant, all tenants die), either Landlord or the personal representative of Tenant's estate may terminate this Lease.
- **Notice:** The terminating party must give at least two months' written notice, effective on the last day of a calendar month, hand delivered or mailed first class to the other party.
- **Owes:** Tenant's estate remains liable for rent and other sums owed before or during the notice period and for restoring the Premises to their starting condition, ordinary wear and tear excepted. Any lease term requiring longer notice is void.

### Move to a medical care facility
*Minn. Stat. § 504B.266*

- **Who:** Tenant or Tenant's authorized representative may terminate this Lease if a physician, advanced practice registered nurse, or mental health professional finds that Tenant (or one of the tenants) needs to move into a medical care facility (such as a nursing home, hospice, assisted living, boarding care home, or accessible unit) and Tenant requires help with activities of daily living due to medical reasons or a disability, meets nursing facility level of care criteria, or has a qualifying mental-illness-related impairment. This does not apply if Tenant needs an accessible unit and Landlord can provide one in the same complex within two months of the request.
- **Notice:** Tenant must give at least two months' written notice, effective on the last day of a calendar month, hand delivered or mailed first class.
- **Documentation:** The notice must include a copy of the medical professional's written documentation and proof that Tenant has been accepted, or has a pending application, at the facility where Tenant needs to move.
- **Owes:** Tenant remains liable for rent and other sums owed before or during the notice period and for restoring the Premises to their starting condition, ordinary wear and tear excepted. Any lease term requiring longer notice is void.

## Open questions for the reviewing attorney

- MILITARY — judgment call: Minn. Stat. § 325G.55 (consumer protection chapter) lets a service member who receives orders into active duty, for deployment, or for a permanent change of duty station after signing a 'rental contract' cancel it the day after the landlord receives certified-mail notice with orders, 'at no penalty and with a full refund of any money which may have been placed on deposit,' where the orders make it impractical to abide by the contract. 'Rental contract' is undefined and appears alongside club, service, and membership-travel contracts, so it is unclear whether it reaches residential real-property leases. I encoded it because it is the only Minnesota statute on point and including it errs toward compliance, but it is MORE tenant-favorable than the SCRA (next-day termination; literal reading would bar deposit deductions). Attorney should decide whether to keep it. Chapters 190 and 192 (military) were searched and contain no residential lease termination provision.
- ACTION REQUIRED (product): Minn. Stat. §§ 504B.120 and 504B.146 require specific items ON THE FIRST PAGE of the lease: lease start and end dates, prorated rent amounts for partial months, all nonoptional fees, the 'Total Monthly Payment' (rent + all nonoptional fees), and whether utilities are included. Violating § 504B.120 exposes the landlord to treble damages and attorney fees. The builder must lay out page 1 accordingly; the disclosure text only describes it.
- DV termination in a multi-tenant lease ends the lease for ALL tenants (§ 504B.206, subd. 3(b)), and every tenant forfeits the security deposit; remaining tenants must reapply. This is unusual and should be explained in co-living agreements, where each room may be a separate lease.
- Nonpayment notice (§ 504B.321, subd. 1a) has mandatory verbatim statements; the eviction complaint must attach it or the case is dismissed and expunged. Keycove's notice templates (not the lease) must use the exact language.
- Late fees: 8% cap of the overdue rent payment, fee must be agreed in writing and state when it is imposed (§ 504B.177). There is no statutory grace period. A late fee is neither interest nor liquidated damages.
- Deposit penalties: failure to return or account within three weeks, or failure to give the initial/move-out inspection notices required by § 504B.182, makes the landlord liable for the withheld amount plus an equal penalty plus interest; bad-faith retention adds up to $500 punitive damages.
- Entry violations (§ 504B.211, subd. 6) can result in rent reduction up to full rescission of the lease plus up to $500 per violation; this is a court remedy, not encoded as a termination right.
- Attorney fees: if the lease lets the landlord recover attorney fees, the tenant gets reciprocal fees (§ 504B.172).
- Digital payment platforms (§ 504B.118, amended 2026 c 81): if rent is paid through an online platform, the landlord must offer a no-fee alternative when the platform is not working, and may not charge late fees or evict when both methods are down. Cash payments require receipts.
- § 504B.111 requires a written lease for buildings of 12 or more units; § 504B.115 requires giving the tenant a copy; § 504B.145 restricts automatic renewals (15–30 day advance notice).
- Fair housing: Minnesota's housing classes beyond federal are creed, sexual orientation, gender identity, marital status, and status with regard to public assistance (§ 363A.09). Age is not a housing class under § 363A.09, subd. 1.
- Local law: St. Paul rent stabilization ordinance and St. Paul tenant protections; Minneapolis tenant-protection, advance-notice, and rental licensing ordinances; § 504B.321 notice period can be longer under local law. Not researched from primary text here.
