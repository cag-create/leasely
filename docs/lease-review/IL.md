## Early termination rights that override the lease-break fee

### Military service (federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act)
*50 U.S.C. § 3955*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant enters military service after signing it; or if Tenant, while in military service, signed this Lease and then receives orders for a permanent change of station (which includes separation or retirement) or to deploy with a military unit or in support of a military operation for 90 days or more; or if Tenant signed this Lease upon such orders and then receives a stop movement order of 30 days or more (or indefinite) that prevents occupancy. A spouse or dependent may terminate within one year after Tenant dies in military service or while performing covered National Guard or Reserve duty, and a Tenant who suffers a catastrophic injury or illness in such service (or the spouse or dependent, if Tenant lacks capacity) may terminate within one year after it occurs. Termination also ends the obligations of Tenant's dependents under this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant delivers written notice to Landlord by hand, private business carrier, mail with return receipt requested, or electronic means. Because rent is paid monthly, termination is effective 30 days after the first date on which the next rent payment is due after the notice is delivered; termination after a stop movement order is effective when the notice and orders are delivered.
- **Documentation:** The notice must include a copy of the military orders (official orders, including orders for separation or retirement, or a notification, certification, or verification from the commanding officer).
- **Owes:** Rent is owed only on a prorated basis through the effective date of termination. Landlord may not impose an early termination charge, but Tenant still owes unpaid amounts due under this Lease, including reasonable charges for excess wear. Rent paid in advance for any period after termination will be refunded within 30 days after the effective date. Landlord may not hold Tenant's belongings or security deposit to satisfy a claim for rent accruing after termination.

### Military service (Illinois law)
*765 ILCS 705/16; 330 ILCS 63/50*

- **Who:** A Tenant who is an Illinois resident and a member of any component of the U.S. Armed Forces or the National Guard of any state, D.C., commonwealth, or territory (or a member of that Tenant's family who lives with Tenant at the Premises) may terminate this Lease if, after signing it, Tenant enters full-time military training or duty ordered by the President, a governor, or other military authority for more than 29 consecutive days, or if Tenant, while in military service, receives orders for a permanent change of station or to deploy for at least 90 days, whether the Lease was signed before or during military service.
- **Notice:** Termination takes effect 30 days after Tenant delivers notice to Landlord, except that when rent is paid monthly, termination takes effect 30 days after the next rent due date following the notice.
- **Documentation:** Tenant must give Landlord a copy of the orders calling Tenant to military service for more than 29 consecutive days and any orders extending that service.
- **Owes:** Landlord shall return any unearned rent paid in advance and the security deposit, except amounts for actual damages or repairs as provided in this Lease. Refusing a valid termination is a civil rights violation under the Illinois Human Rights Act.

### Domestic or sexual violence (Safe Homes Act)
*765 ILCS 750/15, 750/20, 750/30*

- **Who:** Tenant may vacate without liability for later rent if, when Tenant vacates, Tenant or a member of Tenant's household is under a credible imminent threat of domestic or sexual violence at the Premises, or if Tenant or a household member was a victim of sexual violence on premises owned or controlled by Landlord and Tenant vacates as a result.
- **Notice:** Tenant must give Landlord written notice, before or within 3 days after vacating, that Tenant is vacating because of the threat or the sexual violence. For sexual violence on the premises, the notice must state the date of the violence, and the violence must have occurred no more than 60 days before the notice (or as soon as practicable if hospitalization, shelter, or counseling prevented timely notice).
- **Documentation:** For sexual violence on the premises, Tenant must give Landlord at least one form of evidence: medical, court, or police evidence, or a statement from an employee of a victim services or rape crisis organization from which Tenant or a household member sought services.
- **Owes:** Tenant is not liable for rent for the period after Tenant vacates, but remains responsible for rent for the period before Tenant vacated and gave the required notice. Landlord will not disclose to a prospective landlord that Tenant exercised these rights. These rights may not be waived or modified in this Lease or any separate agreement.

### Undisclosed flood zone or flooding
*765 ILCS 705/25(e)*

- **Who:** If Landlord did not disclose that the Premises are in a FEMA Special Flood Hazard Area, Tenant may terminate this Lease after learning of the omission. If Landlord failed to make a required flood disclosure and flooding then damages Tenant's personal property, affects habitability, or affects Tenant's access to the Premises, Tenant may terminate this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant must give written notice of termination no later than 30 days after learning of Landlord's failure to disclose, or no later than 30 days after the flood, as applicable.
- **Owes:** Landlord shall return all rent and fees paid in advance within 15 days after Tenant's notice. Tenant may also sue to recover damages for personal property lost or damaged by flooding.

### Radon hazard
*420 ILCS 46/26(b), (e)*

- **Who:** If, within 90 days after the Lease begins, a radon test arranged by Tenant shows radon above the Illinois Emergency Management Agency's recommended action level and Landlord elects not to mitigate, Tenant may terminate this Lease. If Landlord did not provide the required radon pamphlet, records, and disclosure form, Tenant may test at any time during the Lease, and if a radon hazard is shown and Landlord does not dispute the result with its own licensed radon contractor's test or does not mitigate, Tenant may terminate within 60 days.
- **Notice:** Tenant must give Landlord copies of the radon test results within 10 days after receiving them. Landlord may obtain a confirming test by a licensed radon contractor within 30 days after Tenant's notice.
- **Documentation:** Tenant's radon test results, including any records or reports of radon concentrations.
- **Owes:** Landlord may not withhold any part of the security deposit for radon testing or mitigation when Tenant terminates for an undisclosed radon hazard.

### Landlord's failure to pay utilities
*765 ILCS 735/1*

- **Who:** If Landlord is required by this Lease to pay for water, gas, or electric service and fails to pay for it, Tenant may terminate this Lease, or may pay the utility to keep service on and deduct that amount from rent.
- **Notice:** 
- **Owes:** Termination does not release either party from obligations that arose under this Lease before termination.

## Open questions for the reviewing attorney

- ACTION REQUIRED (product): The Summary of Rights for Safer Homes Act (765 ILCS 752, effective Jan. 1, 2026) requires the Illinois Department of Human Rights' published summary to be attached as the FIRST PAGE of every written residential lease (new and renewal), with each tenant's signature in the acknowledgment space at the bottom of each page. Missing proof creates a presumption of noncompliance; liability is the greater of actual damages (up to $2,000) or $100, plus attorney fees. The data file can only describe this; the builder/e-sign flow must actually insert the IDHR form as page 1 and collect per-page tenant signatures.
- Fee transparency (765 ILCS 705/35, effective Jan. 1, 2027 for leases entered after that date; owner-occupied buildings of 6 or fewer units exempt): all non-optional fees must appear on the first page of the lease or the tenant does not owe them; the lease must say whether utilities are included; application fees are capped at $50 (with a receipts exception), and fees for lease renewal, eviction notices, after-hours maintenance requests, routine maintenance, pest control not caused by the tenant, and move-in/move-out walk-throughs are banned. Not encoded because the effective date is after research date, but Keycove leases signed for 2027 starts are covered.
- Military (765 ILCS 705/16) is broader than the SCRA: any lease (signed on or after Jan. 1, 2013), including leases signed during service, and family members living with the servicemember may terminate. It covers only service members who are Illinois residents. Refusing a valid termination is a civil rights violation.
- Safe Homes Act is drafted as an affirmative defense to a suit for rent, not a notice-and-terminate procedure, and it is silent on co-tenants and on documentation for the 'credible imminent threat' route (the court decides by preponderance). The lease text states the statutory conditions; the attorney should confirm co-tenant treatment.
- Security Deposit Return Act 710/1 (as amended by P.A. 103-224, eff. 1-1-24) now reads 'a lessor of residential real property' with no unit-count threshold; older versions applied only to 5+ unit buildings. Bad-faith noncompliance = 2x the deposit plus fees. Deposit interest (765 ILCS 715) applies only to properties with 25+ units, so interestRequired is false statewide.
- Radon (420 ILCS 46/26): § 15 says the Act applies to leased properties 'to the extent specified in Section 25', but § 25 was repealed by P.A. 103-298; § 26 itself says the disclosure 'shall be provided to each tenant'. The definition of 'residential real property' (1–4 units) is used for sales; § 26 uses 'dwelling unit'. Encoded as applying to every dwelling unit below the third story (per the § 20(9) exclusion). The landlord must also hand over the IEMA 'Radon Guide for Tenants' pamphlet. Confirm scope.
- Flood disclosure (765 ILCS 705/25) must be signed by both parties and included in the written lease or renewal; the statutory form uses checkboxes and counts, which need input fields in the builder. Lower-level (garden/basement/first-floor) units must disclose flooding in the last 10 years.
- Self-help eviction: no single statewide statute expressly bans lockouts outside the Eviction Act's exclusive-remedy structure; Chicago RLTO § 5-12-160 and Cook County RTLO ban lockouts expressly. Statewide prohibition rests on case law (not verified from a primary source).
- Habitability: Illinois has no general statewide habitability statute; the implied warranty of habitability is case law (leading case not verified from a primary source). 765 ILCS 742 repair-and-deduct does not apply to owner-occupied buildings with 6 or fewer units, condos, co-ops, or public housing; the lease text states the rule generally, so the attorney should decide whether to condition it.
- Landlord Retaliation Act (765 ILCS 721, eff. 1-1-25) lets a tenant subjected to retaliation terminate the lease and recover deposit, interest, and prepaid rent plus the greater of 2 months' rent or 2x damages; it is a court remedy and not encoded as a termination right.
- No statewide late-fee cap, grace period, entry-notice period, deposit cap, or move-in checklist requirement verified. 735 ILCS 5/9-218 requires 100+ unit properties with an on-site office to accept rent there without a fee.
- Local law (significant, not researched from primary text here): the Chicago Residential Landlord and Tenant Ordinance and the Cook County Residential Tenant and Landlord Ordinance impose their own deposit-handling and interest rules, entry-notice periods, late-fee limits, required summary attachments, and lockout bans that are stricter than state law. Evanston, Oak Park, Mount Prospect, Urbana, and Champaign also have landlord-tenant ordinances. A Chicago or Cook County property needs a local addendum.
