## Early termination rights that override the lease-break fee

### Military service (federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act)
*50 U.S.C. § 3955*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant enters military service after signing it; or if Tenant, while in military service, signed this Lease and then receives orders for a permanent change of station (which includes separation or retirement) or to deploy with a military unit or in support of a military operation for 90 days or more; or if Tenant signed this Lease upon such orders and then receives a stop movement order of 30 days or more (or indefinite) that prevents occupancy. A spouse or dependent may terminate within one year after Tenant dies in military service or while performing covered National Guard or Reserve duty, and a Tenant who suffers a catastrophic injury or illness in such service (or the spouse or dependent, if Tenant lacks capacity) may terminate within one year after it occurs. Termination also ends the obligations of Tenant's dependents under this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant delivers written notice to Landlord by hand, private business carrier, mail with return receipt requested, or electronic means. Because rent is paid monthly, termination is effective 30 days after the first date on which the next rent payment is due after the notice is delivered; termination after a stop movement order is effective when the notice and orders are delivered.
- **Documentation:** The notice must include a copy of the military orders (official orders, including orders for separation or retirement, or a notification, certification, or verification from the commanding officer).
- **Owes:** Rent is owed only on a prorated basis through the effective date of termination. Landlord may not impose an early termination charge, but Tenant still owes unpaid amounts due under this Lease, including reasonable charges for excess wear. Rent paid in advance for any period after termination will be refunded within 30 days after the effective date. Landlord may not hold Tenant's belongings or security deposit to satisfy a claim for rent accruing after termination.

### Connecticut National Guard on active state service
*Conn. Gen. Stat. § 27-34a*

- **Who:** A member of the Connecticut National Guard whom the Governor orders into active state service has the same lease termination rights as a servicemember on federal active duty under the Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (50 U.S.C. § 3955). This covers a member who signed this Lease before being ordered into active state service, and a member who signed this Lease while in service and then receives orders for a permanent change of station or to deploy for 90 days or more.
- **Notice:** Tenant ends this Lease by delivering written notice of termination to Landlord. Because rent is paid monthly, termination takes effect 30 days after the first date on which the next rent payment is due after the notice is delivered.
- **Documentation:** The notice must include a copy of the member's military orders.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes rent only through the effective termination date, prorated. Landlord shall refund any rent paid in advance for a period after that date within 30 days and may not charge an early termination fee. Tenant remains responsible for unpaid amounts and damage beyond normal wear and tear that are due under this Lease at termination.

### Victims of family violence or sexual assault
*Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-11e*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease without penalty or liability for the remaining term if Tenant is a victim of family violence (as defined in Conn. Gen. Stat. § 46b-38a), or Tenant or a dependent of Tenant is a victim of sexual assault under the statutes listed in § 47a-11e, and Tenant reasonably believes it is necessary to vacate the Property because of fear of imminent harm to Tenant or Tenant's dependent.
- **Notice:** Tenant must give Landlord written notice at least 30 days before the date Tenant intends to terminate this Lease.
- **Documentation:** The notice must include a statement made under oath or affirmation that Tenant or Tenant's dependent is a victim of family violence or sexual assault, stating the intended termination date, and stating that Tenant has vacated or will vacate and remove all possessions by that date (and that anything left behind is abandoned). It must also include either a copy of a police or court record detailing an act of family violence or sexual assault against Tenant or Tenant's dependent, dated no more than 90 days before the notice, or a signed written statement from an employee of the Office of Victim Services of the Judicial Department or the Office of the Victim Advocate detailing such an act, dated no more than 30 days before the notice.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes no rent or penalty for the period after the termination date. Tenant remains liable for any rent unpaid before termination and for property damage Tenant caused. Any other tenant on this Lease remains liable under it, and any occupant without a right to occupy must leave by the termination date.

### Fire or casualty damage
*Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-14*

- **Who:** If fire or other casualty damages or destroys the Property so that Tenant's enjoyment of it is substantially impaired, and the damage was not caused by Tenant's negligence or willful act, Tenant may move out immediately and terminate this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant must notify Landlord in writing within 14 days after moving out of Tenant's intent to terminate. This Lease then ends as of the date Tenant moved out.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes no rent while the impairment continues. Rent is accounted for as of the date of the fire or casualty, and Landlord shall return the security deposit and any prepaid rent as required by Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-21.

## Open questions for the reviewing attorney

- STANDARDIZED SUMMARY FORM (engineering + attorney): Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-7d(d) (P.A. 25-44) bars any landlord, on and after April 1, 2026, from providing a written rental agreement unless its FIRST PAGE is a completed copy of the Department of Housing standardized rental terms summary form (English and Spanish versions exist). Violation = civil penalty of one month's rent plus attorney's fees (§ 47a-7d(f)). The disclosure text above only recites compliance; the generator must actually render the DOH form as page one of every Connecticut lease and co-living agreement. § 47a-7d(a) also requires advertised rent to include all mandatory periodic fees.
- Month-to-month notice is a judgment call. Connecticut has no statute fixing a notice period to end a month-to-month tenancy: under §§ 47a-3b/47a-3d the tenancy lapses at the end of each month and the landlord then serves a 3-day notice to quit under § 47a-23. We used a contractual 30-day notice for both parties (more protective of the tenant than the statute). Confirm the 30-day tenant-side requirement is acceptable and that it is not read as limiting the landlord's statutory notice-to-quit remedy.
- Military: Connecticut has no landlord-tenant-code military termination statute. § 27-34a(a) gives Connecticut National Guard members ordered into active state service 'all of the protections' of the SCRA, which we read to include 50 U.S.C. § 3955 lease termination; § 27-34a(b) adds telecom/gym contract termination only. How § 3955's 'military orders for a permanent change of station or to deploy ... 90 days' prong maps onto state active service orders is not addressed by the statute; please confirm the eligibility sentence.
- Family violence termination (§ 47a-11e) covers family violence and listed sexual assault offenses only; it does not expressly cover stalking or trafficking (although § 46a-64c lists trafficking victims as a fair housing class). It applies to agreements entered into or renewed on or after Jan. 1, 2011 (sexual assault: Jan. 1, 2014). § 47a-7b separately requires lock changes within 48 hours for tenants protected by a stay-away order.
- Late fee: § 47a-15a(b) allows a late charge only if the agreement contains a valid written late-charge agreement; capped at lesser of $5/day (max $50) or 5% of the delinquent payment, one charge per payment. compute.ts clamps to min($50, 5% of rent), which is the maximum ever allowed; a per-day accrual below $50 must be stated by the landlord.
- Entry: § 47a-16 requires only 'reasonable written or oral notice'; the 24-hour figure in entryRule is a lease term, not a statutory number.
- Habitability/disclosure formatting: § 47a-3f requires the sprinkler notice in at least 12-point boldface uniform font; the builder currently renders disclosures as plain list items. Bed bug duties apply only to units other than single-family unattached units (§ 47a-7a(a)(7)).
- Fees: § 47a-4d bars any application fee and any move-in/move-out fee; only a tenant screening report fee of up to $50 (CPI-adjusted by the Commissioner of Housing) is allowed, with a copy of the report and receipt to the applicant. Rent increases need 45 days' written notice (§ 47a-4e). Revenue-management software is an unfair trade practice from Jan. 1, 2026 (§ 47a-4f).
- Protected tenants: § 47a-23c (age 62+ or disability, buildings/complexes of 5+ units) limits eviction to listed good causes and limits rent increases to 'fair and equitable' amounts reviewable by a fair rent commission or the court; the DOH notice must be given at lease signing or renewal (§ 47a-23c(e)).
- Local law: many Connecticut municipalities have fair rent commissions (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 7-148b et seq., not encoded); several cities (e.g., New Haven, Hartford, Stamford) have rental licensing/registration and housing code requirements. Fair housing exemptions in § 46a-64c(b) (owner-occupied room rentals and owner-occupied two-family homes) may matter for co-living, but the agreement should not rely on them.
- Co-living: a room rental is a 'dwelling unit' under § 47a-1, so the full chapter (including summary process) applies; a 'roomer' paying weekly rent is week-to-week (§ 47a-3b). Hotel-style occupancy is transient only if under 30 days or not a primary residence (§ 47a-2(c)).
