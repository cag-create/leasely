## Early termination rights that override the lease-break fee

### Military service (federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act)
*50 U.S.C. § 3955*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant enters military service after signing it; or if Tenant, while in military service, signed this Lease and then receives orders for a permanent change of station (which includes separation or retirement) or to deploy with a military unit or in support of a military operation for 90 days or more; or if Tenant signed this Lease upon such orders and then receives a stop movement order of 30 days or more (or indefinite) that prevents occupancy. A spouse or dependent may terminate within one year after Tenant dies in military service or while performing covered National Guard or Reserve duty, and a Tenant who suffers a catastrophic injury or illness in such service (or the spouse or dependent, if Tenant lacks capacity) may terminate within one year after it occurs. Termination also ends the obligations of Tenant's dependents under this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant delivers written notice to Landlord by hand, private business carrier, mail with return receipt requested, or electronic means. Because rent is paid monthly, termination is effective 30 days after the first date on which the next rent payment is due after the notice is delivered; termination after a stop movement order is effective when the notice and orders are delivered.
- **Documentation:** The notice must include a copy of the military orders (official orders, including orders for separation or retirement, or a notification, certification, or verification from the commanding officer).
- **Owes:** Rent is owed only on a prorated basis through the effective date of termination. Landlord may not impose an early termination charge, but Tenant still owes unpaid amounts due under this Lease, including reasonable charges for excess wear. Rent paid in advance for any period after termination will be refunded within 30 days after the effective date. Landlord may not hold Tenant's belongings or security deposit to satisfy a claim for rent accruing after termination.

### Domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking
*14 M.R.S. §§ 6000, 6001(6), 6002(4)*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant is a victim of domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking and terminates because of an incident or threat of domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives Landlord written notice at least 30 days in advance if this Lease has a term of one year or more, or at least 7 days in advance if this Lease has a term of less than one year or the tenancy is at will.
- **Documentation:** With the notice, Tenant provides documentation of the conduct, including the perpetrator's name. Acceptable documentation includes: a statement signed by a Maine-based sexual assault counselor, domestic violence advocate, or victim witness advocate; a statement signed by a health care provider, mental health care provider, or law enforcement officer, including the license number if licensed; a copy of a protection from abuse or protection from harassment complaint or temporary or final order; a copy of a police report about an incident of domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking; or a copy of a criminal complaint, indictment, or conviction for such a charge.
- **Owes:** Tenant is not liable for rent due after the date the notice expires or the date Tenant vacates, whichever is later, and owes no early termination fee; if Tenant prepaid rent for the month in which the tenancy ends, Landlord need not refund it. Tenant is not liable for damage to the Premises related to the domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking beyond the value of the security deposit if the alleged perpetrator is a tenant and Tenant gives Landlord written notice of the damage and the documentation described above within 30 days after the damage occurs.

### Sexual harassment by Landlord
*14 M.R.S. § 6001(6)(D-1), (H)*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant is the victim of sexual harassment by Landlord or Landlord's agent.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives Landlord written notice at least 30 days in advance if this Lease has a term of one year or more, or at least 7 days in advance if this Lease has a term of less than one year or the tenancy is at will.
- **Documentation:** Tenant provides documentation of the conduct, including the perpetrator's name, of the same kinds accepted for domestic violence terminations (such as a statement from an advocate, health care provider, or law enforcement officer, a protection from harassment complaint or order, a police report, or a criminal complaint).
- **Owes:** Tenant owes no rent for any period after the termination and no early termination fee.

### Elevated radon level
*14 M.R.S. § 6030-D(8)*

- **Who:** If a radon test of the building conducted under Maine's rental radon law shows a radon level of 4.0 picocuries per liter of air or higher, either Landlord or Tenant may terminate this Lease.
- **Notice:** The terminating party gives the other at least 30 days' notice.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes rent only through the termination date and no early termination fee. Landlord may not keep any part of the security deposit because of the termination, apart from deductions for actual cause permitted by 14 M.R.S. § 6033.


## State disclosures in the lease

- **Late Fee Notice** (14 M.R.S. § 6028(3)) *Only if this Lease provides for a late fee*: Landlord may charge a late fee of up to 4% of one month's rent if rent is not paid within 15 days after it is due.
- **Total Price Disclosure** (14 M.R.S. §§ 6030(4), 6030-J) *Required whenever Tenant will pay any mandatory recurring fee or optional recurring fee in addition to rent*: Before entering into this Lease, Landlord gave Tenant, and both parties signed, a plain-language written disclosure of the total rent, any mandatory recurring fee, any optional recurring fee, any utility service costs (or, if Landlord could not obtain them, a residential rental energy efficiency disclosure statement), and any other cost Tenant will pay under this Lease, and each party received a copy. This Lease is unenforceable unless Landlord has received a signed copy of that disclosure.
- **Optional Recurring Fees** (14 M.R.S. § 6030-I) *Only if any optional recurring fee is charged*: Tenant may choose whether to use any service or property feature for which an optional recurring fee is charged, and may stop using it and stop paying the fee at any time without penalty. Landlord will not deny or terminate this Lease because Tenant opts out.
- **Smoking Policy Disclosure** (14 M.R.S. § 6030-E): This Lease states whether smoking is prohibited on the Premises, allowed on the entire Premises, or allowed only in limited areas, and, if allowed in limited areas, identifies those areas. Before signing this Lease or paying a deposit, Tenant acknowledged this smoking policy in writing.
- **Radon Disclosure** (14 M.R.S. § 6030-D(2), (8)) *Not required for a building used exclusively for short-term rentals of 100 days or less with no renewal or extension*: Before Tenant signed this Lease or paid a deposit, Landlord gave Tenant written notice, on the form prescribed by the Maine Department of Health and Human Services, of the date and results of the most recent radon test of the building, whether mitigation has been performed to reduce radon, Tenant's right to conduct a radon test, and the risk associated with radon, and Tenant acknowledged receiving it. If a test shows radon at 4.0 picocuries per liter or more, either party may terminate this Lease on at least 30 days' notice.
- **Residential Energy Efficiency Disclosure** (14 M.R.S. § 6030-C) *When Tenant will pay for an energy supply for the unit, or on Tenant's request*: Before Tenant signed this Lease or paid a deposit, Landlord provided a residential energy efficiency disclosure statement under 35-A M.R.S. § 10117, signed by both parties, which Landlord will keep for at least 3 years. Tenant has the right to obtain from the energy supplier a 12-month history of energy consumption for the unit and the cost of that consumption.
- **Bedbug Disclosure** (14 M.R.S. § 6021-A(2)(D)): Before renting the Premises, Landlord disclosed whether any adjacent unit is currently infested with or being treated for bedbugs. On request, Landlord will disclose the last date on which the Premises and any adjacent unit were inspected for bedbugs and found free of an infestation.
- **Common Area Heat and Utilities** (14 M.R.S. § 6024) *Only for a unit in a multi-unit building where Tenant will pay for a utility serving common areas or other areas outside the unit*: Tenant has agreed in writing to pay for heat, electricity, or another utility serving common areas or areas outside Tenant's unit in return for a stated reduction in rent or other specified fair consideration that approximates the actual cost of providing that service.
- **Rent and Fee Increases** (14 M.R.S. § 6015): Landlord may increase rent or any mandatory recurring fee only after giving Tenant at least 45 days' written notice. If an increase, alone or combined with other rent increases in a 12-month period, totals 10% or more, Landlord will give at least 75 days' written notice.

## Open questions for the reviewing attorney

- Return deadline: 14 M.R.S. § 6033(2)(A) requires a written lease to state the return period, which may not exceed 30 days; the lease must actually state 30 days (or less). Tenancies at will get 21 days.
- Up-front payments: § 6022-A (2023 Me. Laws c. 594, effective 2024) caps move-in payments at first month's rent + deposit (max 2 months) + disclosed mandatory recurring fees, so the builder must not require prepaid last month's rent in Maine. Application fees are banned except the actual cost of one background, credit, or other screening check per 12 months, with a copy of the report to the applicant (§ 6030-H).
- Total price disclosure (§ 6030-J): if any mandatory or optional recurring fee is charged, a separate signed disclosure is required before signing, and § 6030(4) makes the lease unenforceable without it. Keycove should generate and collect this signature whenever fees (parking, pet rent, amenity fees, etc.) appear.
- Domestic violence rent liability: § 6001(6)(D) says a victim 'is not liable for any unpaid rent under the victim's lease', while § 6002(4) says the victim is not liable for rent 'beyond the date the notice expires or the date the victim vacates ... whichever is later' (no refund of prepaid rent for that month). I encoded the § 6002(4) reading because it is more specific; the attorney should consider whether § 6001(6)(D) also forgives past-due rent. Neither section addresses co-tenants. Waiver is void under § 6030(1).
- Early termination fees: § 6030(2)(E) (2023 Me. Laws c. 594) makes any lease charge for discontinuing tenancy unenforceable except reasonable re-letting expenses when the tenant breached notice requirements, unpaid rent, and reasonable repair costs. A flat or liquidated early termination fee should not be used for Maine; the builder's {{early_termination_fee}} option should be disabled or limited to actual re-letting costs.
- Military: Maine has no state lease-termination statute for servicemembers. 37-B M.R.S. § 387 bars forcible entry and detainer against the dwelling of a National Guard or Reserve member ordered to federal or state duty (and family) without leave of court, and § 389-A allows stays; neither is a termination right. Federal SCRA only.
- Nonpayment: the 7-day notice and cure/reinstatement rules in § 6002 apply by their terms to tenancies at will. For fixed-term leases, termination for breach follows the lease (§ 6001(1)), and if a written lease lacks termination/notice language for material breach, § 6001(1-B) lets the landlord use § 6002 procedures and lets the tenant terminate on 7 days' written notice for the landlord's substantial breach. The builder should include a termination-for-breach clause in Maine leases; I recommend it mirror the § 6002 7-day notice and cure language. All landlord termination notices must attach the Judicial Branch form (§ 6001(7), effective Jan. 1, 2024) and include right-to-contest language (§ 6002).
- Casualty: § 6010 bars recovery of rent for any period when a residential unit is unfit for use or habitation due to fire or other unavoidable casualty; this cannot be contracted around for residential leases. It suspends rent rather than granting a termination right, so it is not encoded in earlyTermination.
- Energy disclosure: beginning Jan. 1, 2030, a tenant who did not receive a signed energy efficiency disclosure may terminate on 30 days' written notice (§ 6030-C(3), 2025 Me. Laws c. 411); not yet in effect, so not encoded as a termination right.
- Owner-occupied small buildings (5 or fewer units, one occupied by the landlord) are exempt from the security deposit chapter (§ 6037), cash-receipt rule (§ 6022), and the § 6026 repair-and-deduct remedy; the builder applies the deposit rules uniformly, which is compliant but stricter than required for those owners.
- Heat: any agreement to provide heat below 68 degrees must be a separate written document, revocable, at least 12-point type, with a minimum of 62 degrees and a stated rent reduction, and is barred if someone over 65 or under 5 lives there (§ 6021(6-A)). Common-area utility cost shifting requires a written agreement with fair consideration (§ 6024).
- Tenant data: § 6025-B (2025 Me. Laws c. 767) bars disclosing tenant personal information to harass or force a tenant out without a legitimate business purpose ($1,000 minimum damages); relevant to Keycove data sharing.
- The Maine Attorney General must publish a model residential lease (§ 6030-K, first by Dec. 1, 2025, updated biennially); the attorney may want to compare Keycove's Maine lease against it.
- Late fees: § 6028 defines rent as late only if not paid within 15 days after the due date, so the earliest a fee may be charged is the 16th day after the due date; the 4% cap applies to one month's rent, and the written notice must be given at lease signing.
- Fair housing: the Maine Human Rights Act housing section (5 M.R.S. § 4581-A, as amended by 2025 Me. Laws c. 269) does not list age or marital status for housing; source of income is protected as receipt of public assistance including housing subsidies. § 4581(4)(B) exempts renting one unit of an owner-occupied 2-family dwelling and renting not more than 4 rooms of an owner-occupied one-family dwelling, except from the advertising and broker provisions; co-living rooms in an owner-occupied home may therefore be exempt from parts of the state act (federal rules differ). Not encoded.
- Local law (not verified): Portland has a rent control ordinance (Rent Stabilization, Portland City Code ch. 6) with registration and annual-increase limits, and Portland and other cities have adopted longer notice periods for rent increases and at-will terminations. Check local ordinances before using the statewide notice periods in Portland, South Portland, or Bangor.
