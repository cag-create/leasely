## Early termination rights that override the lease-break fee

### Military service (federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act)
*50 U.S.C. § 3955*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant enters military service after signing it; or if Tenant, while in military service, signed this Lease and then receives orders for a permanent change of station (which includes separation or retirement) or to deploy with a military unit or in support of a military operation for 90 days or more; or if Tenant signed this Lease upon such orders and then receives a stop movement order of 30 days or more (or indefinite) that prevents occupancy. A spouse or dependent may terminate within one year after Tenant dies in military service or while performing covered National Guard or Reserve duty, and a Tenant who suffers a catastrophic injury or illness in such service (or the spouse or dependent, if Tenant lacks capacity) may terminate within one year after it occurs. Termination also ends the obligations of Tenant's dependents under this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant delivers written notice to Landlord by hand, private business carrier, mail with return receipt requested, or electronic means. Because rent is paid monthly, termination is effective 30 days after the first date on which the next rent payment is due after the notice is delivered; termination after a stop movement order is effective when the notice and orders are delivered.
- **Documentation:** The notice must include a copy of the military orders (official orders, including orders for separation or retirement, or a notification, certification, or verification from the commanding officer).
- **Owes:** Rent is owed only on a prorated basis through the effective date of termination. Landlord may not impose an early termination charge, but Tenant still owes unpaid amounts due under this Lease, including reasonable charges for excess wear. Rent paid in advance for any period after termination will be refunded within 30 days after the effective date. Landlord may not hold Tenant's belongings or security deposit to satisfy a claim for rent accruing after termination.

### National Guard on state active duty (state law)
*NMSA 1978, § 20-4-7.1(A); 50 U.S.C. § 3955*

- **Who:** A Tenant who is a member of the National Guard of New Mexico or of any other state or territory and is ordered to state active duty for 30 or more consecutive state duty days, or to federally funded duty in an operational homeland-security role under 32 U.S.C. § 502, has the rights, benefits, and protections of the federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act. A Tenant who enters such duty after signing this Lease, or who while on such duty receives orders for a permanent change of station or to deploy for 90 days or more, may terminate this Lease on the terms below. Termination by such a Tenant also ends the obligations of the Tenant's dependents under this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant delivers written notice of termination to Landlord by hand delivery, private business carrier, U.S. mail with return receipt requested, or electronic means. Because rent is payable monthly, termination takes effect 30 days after the first date on which the next rental payment is due after Landlord receives the notice.
- **Documentation:** The notice must include a copy of Tenant's military orders.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes rent prorated to the effective termination date and any other amounts due under this Lease at termination, such as reasonable charges for excess wear, but Landlord will not charge any early termination fee. Landlord will refund any rent paid in advance for the period after the effective date within 30 days after that date.

### Landlord's failure to deliver possession
*NMSA 1978, § 47-8-26(B)–(C)*

- **Who:** If Landlord fails to deliver possession of the Property at the time this Lease says occupancy begins, 100% of the rent abates until possession is delivered and Tenant may terminate this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives Landlord written notice of termination, effective immediately.
- **Owes:** Landlord will return all prepaid rent and deposits. Tenant owes no early termination fee.

### Landlord's material noncompliance
*NMSA 1978, § 47-8-27.1(A)(1), (B), (E)*

- **Who:** If Landlord materially fails to comply with this Lease, or fails to comply with the Act in a way that materially affects health and safety, Tenant may terminate this Lease, unless the condition was caused by the deliberate or negligent act or omission of Tenant, a member of Tenant's family, or another person on the Property with Tenant's consent.
- **Notice:** Tenant delivers written notice to Landlord specifying the acts and omissions constituting the breach and stating that this Lease will terminate on a date at least seven days after Landlord receives the notice if Landlord does not make a reasonable attempt to remedy the breach within seven days. If Landlord makes a reasonable attempt to adequately remedy the breach before that date, the Lease does not terminate. If the last day to remedy falls on a weekend or federal holiday, the period extends to the next business day.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes no early termination fee. Once possession is restored to Landlord, Landlord will return the balance of any prepaid rent and the deposit to which Tenant is entitled.

### Fire or casualty damage
*NMSA 1978, § 47-8-31*

- **Who:** If the Property is damaged or destroyed by fire or casualty to an extent that substantially impairs Tenant's enjoyment of the dwelling unit, Tenant may vacate and terminate this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant notifies Landlord in writing within seven days after vacating of Tenant's intention to terminate; the Lease terminates as of the date Tenant vacated.
- **Owes:** Rent is accounted for as of the date Tenant vacated, and Landlord will return the balance of prepaid rent and recoverable deposits. Tenant remains responsible for damage caused by Tenant's negligence.

### Unlawful or harassing entry by Landlord
*NMSA 1978, §§ 47-8-24(F), 47-8-38(B)*

- **Who:** If Landlord makes an unlawful entry, makes a lawful entry in an unreasonable manner, or makes repeated lawful demands for entry that unreasonably interfere with Tenant's quiet enjoyment or unreasonably harass Tenant, Tenant may terminate this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives Landlord written notice of termination.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes no early termination fee and may recover damages and reasonable attorney's fees.

### Unlawful exclusion or interruption of services
*NMSA 1978, §§ 47-8-36, 47-8-48(C)*

- **Who:** If Landlord, without a court order, knowingly excludes or removes Tenant or attempts to do so by changing locks, blocking entrances, interrupting utilities or services, removing Tenant's property, removing appliances or fixtures, or any willful act making the Property inaccessible or uninhabitable, Tenant may terminate this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives Landlord written notice of termination.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes no early termination fee, may abate 100% of the rent for each affected day, and may recover damages and a civil penalty of two times the monthly rent.


## State disclosures in the lease

- **Manager and Owner Identification** (NMSA 1978, § 47-8-19): The person authorized to manage the Property, and an owner of the Property or the person authorized to act for the owner for service of process and for receiving and receipting for notices and demands, is Landlord at the name, address, and telephone number stated in this Lease unless Landlord designates another person in writing. Landlord will keep this information current. If Landlord does not provide this information, Tenant is relieved of the obligation to give Landlord notices required by the Uniform Owner-Resident Relations Act.
- **Copy of Lease and Rules** (NMSA 1978, §§ 47-8-20(G), 47-8-23(F)): Landlord has provided Tenant a written copy of this Lease before occupancy begins and copies of all existing rules and regulations. A rule adopted after Tenant signs this Lease is enforceable against Tenant only if Tenant receives reasonable notice of its adoption and it does not substantially modify Tenant's bargain.
- **Application of Rent Payments and Guests** (NMSA 1978, § 47-8-15(E), (G)): Unless Landlord and Tenant agree otherwise in writing, Tenant's rent payments will not be applied to deposits or damages. Landlord will not charge a fee for occupancy of the dwelling unit by a reasonable number of guests for a reasonable length of time.
- **Utility Bills in Multi-Unit Housing** (NMSA 1978, § 47-8-20(F)) *Only for multi-unit housing*: If the dwelling unit is separately metered, Tenant may obtain a copy of the utility bill for the unit on request. If the unit is submetered, or if common-area utility costs are apportioned among units and passed on to residents, Tenant may obtain on request copies of the bills being apportioned and the calculations used to apportion them. The share of common-area costs that would be allocated to an empty unit will not be allocated to the remaining residents. Landlord may charge an administrative fee of no more than $5.00 for each monthly request.
- **Interest on Deposit** (NMSA 1978, § 47-8-18(A)(1)) *Only if this Lease has a term of one year and the deposit is greater than one month's rent*: Because this is an annual rental agreement and the deposit exceeds one month's rent, Landlord will pay Tenant interest on the deposit each year equal to the passbook interest permitted to savings and loan associations in New Mexico by the federal home loan bank board.

## Open questions for the reviewing attorney

- Deposit cap is term-dependent: § 47-8-18(A)(2) caps deposits at one month's rent only for agreements shorter than one year; for an annual agreement there is no cap, but a deposit above one month's rent triggers annual passbook interest (§ 47-8-18(A)(1)). maxMonths is set to 1 as the conservative value; if the builder allows more than one month on a 12-month lease it must include the deposit_interest disclosure. The 'passbook interest permitted ... by the federal home loan bank board' benchmark is obsolete (the FHLBB was abolished in 1989) — attorney should advise what rate to use.
- Deposit return timing is internally inconsistent: § 47-8-18(C) says 30 days from termination or resident departure, 'whichever is later', while the forfeiture rule in § 47-8-18(D) runs 30 days from 'termination of the tenancy'. The lease text tracks both; safest practice is to send within 30 days of the earlier event.
- Late fees (2025 amendment, Laws 2025, ch. 122): 5% of rent per rental period in default; rent-only base; notice of the fee by the end of the next rental period. No statutory grace period, so graceDays is null.
- Domestic violence: New Mexico has NO statutory DV/sexual assault lease-termination right. The UORRA text (current through Laws 2025, ch. 122) contains only an eviction defense for substantial-violation evictions (§ 47-8-33(J)). Bills to add a termination right (e.g., SB 423 (2019), SB 338 (2021), SB 400 (2025)) do not appear in the compiled code. The NMOneSource PDF was generated July 2026; attorney should confirm no 2026 enactment.
- Military: § 20-4-7.1(A) extends SCRA 'rights, benefits and protections' to National Guard members (any state) ordered to state active duty for 30+ consecutive state duty days, or federally funded 32 U.S.C. § 502 homeland-security operational duty. The SCRA-trigger mapping to state duty is our interpretation. § 20-1-8.1 separately lets deployed residents suspend municipal/county utilities but does not address leases.
- Fair housing: the NM Human Rights Act housing provision (§ 28-1-7(G)) does not list familial status or source of income (federal FHA still covers familial status). Owner-occupied buildings with up to four units are exempt (§ 28-1-9(D)) — relevant to co-living — and an owner of no more than three single-family dwellings who rents one without any discriminatory notice or advertisement is exempt as to that dwelling (§ 28-1-9(A)). 'Spousal affiliation' is listed but not defined in § 28-1-2.
- 2025 amendments to note in the builder: listings must disclose base rent and all fees (§ 47-8-19.1); screening fee capped at $50 with notice, receipt, and refund rules (§ 47-8-19.2); one screening fee per applicant per 90 days across the same ownership, and copies of screening reports must be provided (§ 47-8-19.3); 60 days' notice before increasing any lease fee (§ 47-8-19.4); $250 penalty for unauthorized screening fees (§ 47-8-48(B)).
- Waivers: no lease may waive rights or remedies 'under the law' (§ 47-8-16). Prevailing-party attorney fees apply in suits to enforce the lease or the Act (§ 47-8-48(A)). There is no landlord's lien (§ 47-8-36.1).
- Substantial violations (drug distribution, deadly weapons, serious harm, sexual assault, etc. — § 47-8-3(V)) allow a 3-day termination notice (§ 47-8-33(I)); other material noncompliance requires a 7-day cure notice given within 30 days of the breach (§ 47-8-33(A)–(C)).
- Abandonment is defined as absence of more than 7 days without notice AFTER rent is delinquent (§ 47-8-3(A)); property must be stored 30 days after abandonment, 14 days after voluntary surrender, or 3 days after a writ (§ 47-8-34.1). The landlord may request a contact person for a deceased sole occupant (§ 47-8-34.2) — the builder could add this optional clause.
- Local law: rent control is preempted (§ 47-8A-1). Albuquerque, Santa Fe, and Las Cruces may have additional local housing ordinances (e.g., source-of-income or rental registration rules) — not verified here.
