## Early termination rights that override the lease-break fee

### Military service (federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act)
*50 U.S.C. § 3955*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant enters military service after signing it; or if Tenant, while in military service, signed this Lease and then receives orders for a permanent change of station (which includes separation or retirement) or to deploy with a military unit or in support of a military operation for 90 days or more; or if Tenant signed this Lease upon such orders and then receives a stop movement order of 30 days or more (or indefinite) that prevents occupancy. A spouse or dependent may terminate within one year after Tenant dies in military service or while performing covered National Guard or Reserve duty, and a Tenant who suffers a catastrophic injury or illness in such service (or the spouse or dependent, if Tenant lacks capacity) may terminate within one year after it occurs. Termination also ends the obligations of Tenant's dependents under this Lease.
- **Notice:** Tenant delivers written notice to Landlord by hand, private business carrier, mail with return receipt requested, or electronic means. Because rent is paid monthly, termination is effective 30 days after the first date on which the next rent payment is due after the notice is delivered; termination after a stop movement order is effective when the notice and orders are delivered.
- **Documentation:** The notice must include a copy of the military orders (official orders, including orders for separation or retirement, or a notification, certification, or verification from the commanding officer).
- **Owes:** Rent is owed only on a prorated basis through the effective date of termination. Landlord may not impose an early termination charge, but Tenant still owes unpaid amounts due under this Lease, including reasonable charges for excess wear. Rent paid in advance for any period after termination will be refunded within 30 days after the effective date. Landlord may not hold Tenant's belongings or security deposit to satisfy a claim for rent accruing after termination.

### Military service (state law)
*N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:11-a*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if Tenant is a member of the armed services reserve who is called to active duty, a member of the National Guard who is called to active duty, or a member of the armed services on active duty who is reassigned to a location outside New Hampshire.
- **Notice:** Tenant must give notice of termination within 7 days after receiving notice of being called to active duty or reassigned out of state, by written notice that complies with Section 305 of the federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (50 U.S.C. § 3955). Under that section, because rent is payable monthly, termination takes effect 30 days after the first date on which the next rent payment is due after the notice is delivered. The 7-day deadline applies to this state-law right only and does not limit any right Tenant has directly under the federal Act.
- **Documentation:** As the federal Act requires, the notice must be accompanied by a copy of Tenant's military orders or a written verification from Tenant's commanding officer.
- **Owes:** Tenant owes rent only through the effective date of termination, prorated, and no early termination fee or penalty; Landlord will refund rent paid in advance for any period after that date within 30 days, as the federal Act provides.

### Domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking
*N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:11-b*

- **Who:** Tenant may terminate this Lease if, within the most recent 150 days, Tenant or a household member residing at the Premises has been a victim of domestic violence (as defined in RSA 173-B), sexual assault (RSA 632-A), or stalking (RSA 633:3-a), or if within the most recent 150 days an event has occurred involving a victim of past domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking that, together with the past abuse, causes the victim to fear for the victim's safety.
- **Notice:** Tenant gives Landlord notice of termination and must vacate the Premises within 30 days after giving notice, or at another time Landlord and Tenant agree on.
- **Documentation:** Tenant provides written verification that Tenant or the household member who is the victim: has obtained a valid protective order against the perpetrator; has started legal action to obtain a protective order; has reported the domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking to a law enforcement agency; has a written verification from a law enforcement official, victim's advocate, attorney, or health care provider of victim status; or has signed the self-certification form provided by the New Hampshire circuit court. Landlord will keep the verification, and the fact that anyone is a victim, in strict confidence and will disclose it only with the victim's written consent, when relevant and reasonably necessary in an eviction or housing-assistance proceeding, or as otherwise required by law.
- **Owes:** Tenant is liable only for rent owed through the termination date or the date Tenant actually vacates, whichever is later, and owes no early termination fee. Any security deposit remains applied to the rental of the Premises until all tenants have vacated and is then returned under RSA 540-A:7. Landlord may evict a remaining co-tenant or occupant who is the accused perpetrator.

## Open questions for the reviewing attorney

- Payment methods (important for Keycove): RSA 540-A:3, X (effective Jan. 1, 2025) bars requiring rent, renewal, or extension payments solely by electronic funds transfer, including autopay; at least one non-electronic method must be allowed. A Stripe-only rent flow would violate this for NH units. Violations of 540-A:3, X are excluded from the 358-A civil penalties (540-A:4, IX(d)) but remain prohibited.
- Just cause: for 'restricted property' (everything except single-family houses whose owner owns no more than 3 single-family houses, units in owner-occupied buildings of 4 or fewer units, and bank-owned foreclosed houses) the landlord needs a statutory reason to evict, including at lease end. 2024 N.H. Laws c. 9 (effective Jan. 1, 2025) added RSA 540:2, II(i): expiration of a lease whose original or renewed term totals 12 months or more is a ground if the landlord gave at least 60 days' written notice of nonrenewal and files within 6 months. 'Other good cause' before an eviction notice based on tenant conduct requires a prior written warning (540:2, III). The builder's month-to-month rule should not promise no-cause termination for restricted property.
- 2025 N.H. Laws c. 263 amended RSA 540:2 effective July 1, 2026, and 2026 N.H. Laws c. 308 amended RSA 540-A:2, 540-A:3, 540-A:4, and 540:13 effective Oct. 8, 2026; the profile reflects the text in force on 2026-11-08.
- Security deposit: 'all funds in excess of the monthly rent ... for any purpose' (540-A:5, II) means prepaid last month's rent, pet deposits, and similar charges count toward the one-month/$100 cap; this is my reading of the definition. The deposit subdivision does not apply to the exempt small landlords in 540-A:5, I. Interest is owed only if the deposit is held for one year or longer (540-A:6, IV). The move-in condition notice is required even when no receipt is required (540-A:6, I(c)).
- Late fees: no statute regulates residential late fees. The only statutory figure is the $15 liquidated damages a tenant pays to cure a nonpayment eviction (540:9). The court demand/eviction notice forms (540:5, II) set the minimum content of notices.
- Military: RSA 540:11-a (2004) covers reservists and National Guard members 'called to active duty' and active-duty members reassigned out of state. The statute does not say whether state active duty counts; I left the eligibility text general. Its 7-day notice deadline is stricter than SCRA, so the lease states that the federal right is unaffected. No NH statute extends SCRA wholesale to state active duty (RSA 110-B:71 covers only arrest and civil-process privileges).
- Domestic violence termination (RSA 540:11-b) was added by 2024 N.H. Laws c. 9, effective Jan. 1, 2025. The statute says only 'notice' (not 'written notice') for the termination itself; the verification must be written. RSA 540:2, VII separately bars evicting a victim solely for being a victim and requires lock changes at the tenant's expense on a protective order. Waivers of chapter 540 rights are void (540:28).
- Entry: New Hampshire uses a consent standard with no fixed hours (540-A:3, IV-V, V-d); landlords may petition the court for access under 540-A:4. Abandoned personal property must be stored with reasonable care for 7 days (540-A:3, VII).
- Application fees: any application/renewal fee and the background/credit check requirement must be disclosed in writing before collection, and unused amounts beyond actual screening cost and reasonable administrative costs refunded within 30 days if the applicant is not accepted (540-A:3, VIII).
- Owners of restricted property must file the name of an in-state agent for service of process with the town or city clerk within 30 days of becoming owner (540:1-b); this is an owner obligation, not a lease clause.
- Co-living and exemptions: chapter 540 does not apply to rooms in rooming or boarding houses rented to transient guests for fewer than 90 consecutive days, or to a single-family home that is the owner's primary residence where the occupant has no lease (540:1-a, IV(a), (d)). The state fair housing law exempts rooms in an owner-occupied private home and owner-occupied buildings of 4 or fewer units, except for discriminatory advertising (354-A:15, II-III). Federal Fair Housing Act exemptions differ.
- Fair housing: RSA 354-A:8 lists age, sex, gender identity, race, creed, color, marital status, familial status, disability, national origin, and sexual orientation. RSA 354-A:10's introductory list omits sexual orientation and creed while paragraph VI includes sexual orientation; I relied on 354-A:8's declaration of rights for the class list.
- Mitigation: I found no New Hampshire statute; the case law position was not verified from a primary source, so basis is 'unclear' and the lease voluntarily commits Landlord to credit re-rental income. No statutory early termination fee cap, and no other statutory early termination rights (540:13-d is only a defense to nonpayment eviction).
- No municipal rent control exists in New Hampshire; Manchester, Nashua, and other cities enforce local housing codes under RSA 48-A that may exceed RSA 48-A:14 (not researched).
